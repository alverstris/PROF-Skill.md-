Findings and rationale

Use this reference when a teaching decision needs justification or the authoring method is being revised. Keep the rationale out of ordinary lesson prose unless the learner asks for it.

Origin and evidence limits

PROF distils Learning_and_Teaching_Review.md, revision 3, and the user's subsequent requests for an AI authoring skill with Overleaf/LaTeX/PDF styling and reliable operation without supplied materials. The review combined retrieved Phoenix learning excerpts, inspected CSEA teaching guides and cards, French requests and an MRI handbook. The recovered chat excerpts were incomplete; several older generated files and independent learner outcomes were unavailable.

The most defensible working description is a need to connect meaning, legitimacy and use. It is not a visual/auditory learning-type diagnosis. The user had already requested many of the effective practices; inconsistent implementation was a central teaching failure. Do not infer laziness, inability, a memory disorder or universal study habits from help-seeking records.

Current explicit clarification, 2026-10-05: asking the student is not failure of PROF. Use the evident course convention; when it is not evident, ask. Clarification, partial attempts and honest pending work are legitimate application. The failure is presenting teaching as usable/complete when its actual route does not justify that claim. This clarification governs interpretation of the historical question-cost observations below; do not optimise away necessary dialogue.

Relevant research, used narrowly

- [Xie et al. (2019), A theory of instruction for introductory programming skills](https://www.benjixie.com/publication/cse-2019/cse-2019.pdf): distinguishes reading and writing code semantics from recognising and constructing purposeful patterns. Its exploratory evaluation with nine novice programmers has confounds and supplies limited design support, not proof of an ideal sequence or of effectiveness for this learner. Considered during the subsequent review of structural explanations.
- [Chi et al. (1989), Self-explanations](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/epdf/10.1207/s15516709cog1302_1): a small observational mechanics study connects useful self-explanation with attention to principles and gaps. It does not justify requesting a verbose explanation of every line.
- [Atkinson, Renkl and Merrill (2003), Fading worked-out steps](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/200772684_Transitioning_From_Studying_Examples_to_Solving_Problems_Effects_of_Self-Explanation_Prompts_and_Fading_Worked-Out_Steps): probability studies support a structured handover from worked examples to solving, with principle-focused prompts. This is not proof of one ideal sequence for every domain.
- [Karpicke and Blunt (2011), Retrieval practice](https://learninglab.psych.purdue.edu/downloads/2011/2011_Karpicke_Blunt_Science.pdf): undergraduate science-text experiments support delayed recall and inference under the tested conditions. Recognising an answer should not be treated as equivalent to generating it.
- [Butler et al. (2017), Applying knowledge to different examples](https://bpb-us-e2.wpmucdn.com/sites.wustl.edu/dist/8/805/files/2026/06/Butler-et-al.-2017-Retrieving-and-applying-knowledge-to-different-examples-promotes-transfer-of-learning.pdf): structured application studies support variations requiring new inferences. Changing only the numbers can leave the central decision untouched.
- [Cepeda et al. (2008), Spacing effects](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1111/j.1467-9280.2008.02209.x): delayed revisiting can help, but fact-learning results do not prescribe an optimal physics or French schedule for this learner.
- [Rogowsky, Calhoun and Tallal (2020), Learning-style preferences](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2020.00164/full): a reading/listening study does not support matching instruction to fixed modality labels. It does not deny useful individual preferences.

These references were considered in the source review or the subsequent targeted method review identified above. Do not present them as a fresh systematic review or a personal effectiveness guarantee. Preserve the distinction between direct requirements, observed episodes, plausible inferences and design heuristics. Update the method when actual new-task performance supplies better evidence.
