# SASIS: diagnose the failed question and choose the rewrite

Read this guide before diagnosing or repairing a SASIS case and after recovery. It governs explicit PROF self-iteration only. A student question, a supported partial answer or an honest stop is valid student behavior. The defect to repair is the specific gap in the claimed teaching route, or a fault in the trial, rather than the fact that a question was asked. Do not optimise for fluent answers or fewer questions.

## Collect all issues and establish their causes before choosing rewrites

Apply the protocol's full-iteration audit and issue register. Collect every supported issue across the whole document, all required source portions, responses/questions and author checks; do not stop when the first defect or shared cause is found. For each issue identify every supported cause and affected instance, grouping duplicates without losing their evidence. The earliest blocked operation orders a repair within one failure; it never limits the audit to that operation or to one cause. Re-audit later and previously blocked operations after repairs, add newly exposed defects to the same iteration, and satisfy the protocol's closure gate before progressing to new material. Assess underteaching more severely than modest additional explanation while also repairing excessive reteaching and obstructive padding.

Preserve the original question, student answer/question, exact visible notes and complete OCR baseline. Identify the earliest consequential operation the student cannot justify, rather than classifying only the final wrong answer. Inspect actual permitted passages and their conditions, including legitimate alternative deductions. Recall of the current call's actually supplied baseline/document and reuse of its supported earlier deductions are valid; do not misclassify them as memory contamination. Do not invent a premise from another document/call, external subject recollection, a syllabus title, an evaluator's correct answer or a later patch. A short omission is not automatically a necessary bridge; check whether another valid route survives. Conversely, a technically possible reconstruction of an extensive untaught theorem need not constitute usable beginner teaching.

Classify both the question demand and the cause. One derivation question can expose missing notation, an absent assumption, a hidden mathematical operation, a convention decision or an invalid task. Repair the upstream cause first; do not add several unrelated explanations to cover all possibilities. Where the full baseline already supplies the required operation, locate it and check actual delivery, navigation, transfer to the new representation and the student's working. Do not assume completed OCR means no slips, and do not reteach an entire A-level topic just because one answer failed.

Give each issue every evidence-supported disposition that applies: (1) teaching defect; (2) source/technical defect; (3) baseline defect; (4) assessment/evaluator defect; (5) input/runner/restriction defect; (6) supported student slip or uncertain diagnosis; or (7) legitimate question with dependent work pending. Record causal dependencies and distinct repairs; do not force several supported causes into one label. An incorrect answer alone does not prove a PROF defect. A correct unsupported answer cannot establish lesson sufficiency.

If the cause remains ambiguous, use a fresh, discriminating probe with the same frozen permitted premises. For example, separate reading the representation from selecting the method or from performing the algebra. Do not tell the student the suspected missing rule. Preserve the ambiguous result as unverified until the evidence distinguishes causes. Do not claim a particular learner misconception from one simulated answer.

## Rewrite according to the blocked operation

The table specifies concrete repairs, not a requirement to insert every activity into every document. Apply rows supported by the case. The obligation IDs refer to SKILL.md. Every teaching repair includes a before/after passage locator and a fresh check of the blocked operation; the final column adds the discriminating check that prevents a merely cosmetic repair.

| Question demand or observed block | Required rewrite or other repair | Fresh evidence required |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Meaning, purpose or identifying an instance: "What is this?" or "Why introduce it?" | T1: replace a name-only or circular introduction with an operational definition in already supplied terms. State what object/quantity it describes, its inputs and conditions, and the problem it enables the learner to solve. Include one minimal instance and a nearby non-instance where the distinction is consequential. Put this before the first required use. | Identify and explain a new instance/non-instance and use the definition in a small task. Repeating the definition verbatim is insufficient. |
| Reading or constructing a representation: "What does this symbol/diagram mean?" | T2: rewrite the first representation to identify object roles, type/shape, indices, order, axes/basis, domains and units as relevant. Decode one actual expression/diagram and construct one from a verbal or physical description. Explain a new operation's action on these objects, rather than adding a detached glossary. | Read an unfamiliar valid representation and construct a counterpart; include an order, shape or domain distinction that the original teaching hid. |
| Missing prerequisite: "How can I do this mathematical operation?" | T2-T4/T9: first inspect the complete baseline. If the operation is granted, provide the specific connection to its new use where that connection was missing. If it is new, insert the smallest complete prerequisite bridge before use, with an executable worked step and an attempt, or reorder the unit so the prerequisite is actually taught first. Research an absent subject premise rather than supplying remembered knowledge. | Perform the prerequisite and then its new application. Check that later steps no longer require another hidden prerequisite; retain a brevity control where the prerequisite is already sufficient. |
| Legitimacy or derivation: "Why does this follow?" or "Where did that term go?" | T3-T4: replace the pivotal jump with a connected chain stating the starting law/definition, assumptions, substitutions and consequential intermediate mathematical steps. Label a convention, approximation, deduction and independently sourced physical premise accurately. State the condition under which cancellation, division, interchange, limiting or differentiation is allowed when consequential. Put the chain at the jump. | Justify the disputed step from permitted premises and handle a changed assumption or boundary case that would prevent the same step. A citation, final formula or "by algebra" cannot close the gap. |
| Method selection: "How do I know which method applies?" | T4-T6: rewrite the decision point with observable selection criteria and a short comparison of two plausible methods/cases. Demonstrate why one meets the conditions and the other does not, or why both are legitimate. Then place an unlabelled selection task before its solution; remove headers that reveal the method under test. | Choose and justify a method in a changed case where the previously successful method fails or another is preferable. Merely solving a named-method exercise does not pass. |
| Executing a connected application: "I understand the pieces, but cannot start/finish this problem." | T4-T6: replace disconnected fragments with one complete worked route from the question/data to the result, including setup, decisions, consequential operations and result checks. Add a supported initial attempt at the actual first opportunity, then fade the support in a later attempt. Preserve necessary links when shortening. | Solve a fresh connected task without a revealed route, and test the actual early-prefix notes separately. Do not let explanations appearing after the first exercise retroactively support that exercise. |
| Physical interpretation or result checking: "What does the answer mean?" | T1-T4/T10: connect the mathematical quantity/sign/vector to the defined physical quantity, measurement or prediction. Show a relevant unit/dimension check and a limiting, sign, scale or conservation check where informative. Correct the calculation too if the interpretation exposes a technical error. | Explain a new result and reject or qualify a plausible result using the supplied physical checks. A unit label attached to the final number alone is insufficient. |
| Transfer, applicability or changed conditions: "Does this still work if .?" | T3-T6/T8: rewrite the controlling assumptions and dependency of the method/result on them. Add a paired case with a changed condition, representation or constraint and work through what must change; include a genuine exception/countercase where warranted. Keep practice that requires recognising that change. | Solve and justify an independently changed case. New names or numbers alone do not test transfer, and a rote repetition of the paired example does not establish it. |
| Integration across topics, lectures or source parts: "How does this connect to what came before?" | T2-T4/T11: rewrite the actual seam: reconcile notation and conditions using course evidence, expose the dependency, and place the required prior passage visibly in the tested notes or explicitly teach it here. Reorder causal dependencies and integrate source portions where needed. A contents list, completed-topic ledger or summary of an absent lesson cannot provide student knowledge. | Solve a task using the connection with all required earlier teaching actually visible. Check affected downstream sections and a fresh-context condition; do not reuse a student's previous lesson answer as memory. |
| Stuck despite the core explanation, or misleading/missing help: "What should I try next?" | T7/T11: rewrite help at the earliest divergence. A hint should give an actionable next move without quietly providing the whole answer; the solution must show the missing decision and reasoning, not just the result. Match symbols, assumptions and task versions and make the help reachable in the requested format. Core teaching must still support the main no-external-help trial. | Recheck the unassisted core. If staged help was expressly authorised, freeze separate hint and solution portions and check assisted responses separately. Inspect actual navigation/rendering; the existence of a help heading is insufficient. |
| Overload, repeated definitions or hard-to-find premises obstruct use | T9/T11: shorten redundant passages while retaining the causal route; divide a long part at coherent prerequisite boundaries, put necessary premises near use, and give a visible route through core and optional detail. For large-source work, process and integrate bounded source portions instead of repeatedly rewriting from memory. Do not infer a learner's capacity from one answer. | Check the original blocked operation, integrated source coverage and an exact-brevity/passing control. A shorter or longer document alone proves neither adequacy nor improvement. |
| Course convention missing or contradictory | Consult the actual course material for a clear convention and rewrite definitions and dependent uses consistently if it settles the issue. If it does not, ask the actual student/user the focused defining question and leave dependent work pending. The restricted student can flag uncertainty but cannot invent the course choice. Do not select a remembered/popular default or write both routes to conceal the unresolved decision. | Verify internal consistency when a convention is supplied. Until then, the appropriate student outcome is a justified question/partial answer; do not mark dependent teaching complete or call the question a PROF failure. |
| Wrong scientific/mathematical premise, faulty source or extraction | T3/T10: verify the actual source passage/equation independently, resolve transcription and scientific errors, and rewrite the incorrect premise and every affected derivation, example, task, hint and solution. Label a correction to supplied material and its evidence. More explanation of a false premise is not a repair. | Use an independently checked reference route and relevant dimensional/limiting/numerical or alternative-method checks. Re-run all checks invalidated by the correction; source download evidence alone cannot certify the content. |
| Baseline absent, truncated, stale for a claimed current profile, or operationally incomplete | Repair baseline admission/delivery first. Verify a genuine baseline-content defect against the relevant official OCR material and correct/freeze the packet as a separate profile revision. A qualification title cannot substitute for operational content. Do not quietly add the missing knowledge to the student from memory or change the baseline during a frozen condition. | Keep the original trial under its original inputs; any changed baseline starts a newly labelled condition. Recheck affected baseline-positive tasks and then the teaching task. An input failure cannot be counted as a lesson pass or diagnosed as a missing university explanation without further evidence. |
| Ambiguous, impossible, wrongly graded or contaminated assessment | Repair task/evaluator conditions: state incidental data and model boundaries, remove unintended prerequisites or revealing method cues, or correct the reference route and support audit. Preserve the original result. If a task is intended to be underdetermined, recognise a valid question/partial answer rather than silently turning it into a solvable problem. | Re-preflight the task against permitted inputs and administer a fresh held-out task. A changed grade, leaked solution or answer-aware rewrite is not proof the original lesson improved. |
| Student slip despite an adequate, visible route | Verify the supported route and earliest erroneous working. Give local feedback or a discriminating new probe; change teaching only if evidence locates an instructional gap (for example, an unexplained sign convention). Accept valid alternatives. Do not add a universal PROF rule or pages of remediation for an isolated arithmetic slip. | Use a fresh task that separates execution from conceptual understanding. Record uncertainty or the slip honestly; neither one student error nor majority AI agreement establishes a systemic teaching failure. |
| Correct answer uses an externally remembered/unsupported rule; access or role contamination | Give no lesson-sufficiency credit to the unsupported move. Repair the restriction/runner/evaluator failure, inspect surviving permitted deductions and use a relevant missing-bridge or hypothetical-rule control. Verified unauthorised access invalidates the affected trial. Do not patch the notes after the fact to legitimise the old answer or score correctness as compliance. | Run a fresh restricted condition with warranted moves. Lawful recall of actual current-call inputs is valid. Report instruction-only isolation honestly; a student saying "I used no memory" is not evidence of technical erasure or sufficient teaching. |
| Required content not delivered/read, context shift or broken media/help/tool | Restore and verify the actual input or accessible representation, including needed diagrams, equations, timestamps or supplied calculator/table data. Split overlong notes coherently or use the recorded larger/whitelisted runner. Repair the producing workflow where it caused omission; do not expand conceptual prose before establishing the real input. | Re-admit the complete baseline and exact visible notes, rerun the affected case and inspect affected source coverage/artefacts. Keep the failed original as an input/workflow result, not an unqualified student-learning result. |
| Apparent retention failure after a previous trial | A fresh SASIS student has no prior-trial memory. Supply required earlier teaching in its notes; for a durable study document, improve explicit later retrieval/connection opportunities where missing under T8. Do not invent a learned-knowledge ledger or treat previous answers as starting knowledge. | Check the supplied-input route and later retrieval tasks as document capabilities. Simulated fresh-context reconstruction cannot establish human delayed retention; report that limit. |

## Choose the extent and the PROF change

Use a local rewrite when the actual defect is confined to a definition, step, decision, task or help item and the surrounding dependency route survives. Use a structural rewrite when the missing prerequisite changes the order, several representations conflict, multiple sections depend on a wrong assumption, or fragmented sources leave an unusable connected route. Rebuild the affected dependency chain from its first defect, not the entire document by default. Invalidate and retest every materially affected check. Preserve correct content and passing concise/expert behavior.

For every issue save: stable issue ID, question/capability and frozen condition; original answer/question; relevant blocked moves and their real passage/baseline locators; all supported causes, affected instances and surviving alternatives; chosen table row(s); exact before/after passages and help/dependency changes; any required research or actual student question; and fresh check outcomes. Keep the whole iteration's register and coverage audit, not just the currently repaired defect. An empty field, checklist tick, obligation heading or page count cannot close an issue. A pending question has its pending dependency and next action, rather than a fabricated rewrite.

Then decide whether evidence warrants editing PROF. A document repair is not automatically a skill repair. If the author omitted an existing rule, inspect why it was lost and strengthen the specific execution/recovery trigger only when supported. If a necessary rule is absent or inadequate, edit the relevant obligation/reference so it specifies (a) the observed trigger, (b) the concrete author action, and (c) the evidence that would show the resulting teaching supports that operation. If a source, baseline, task or runner alone was faulty, repair that component; change PROF only for a demonstrated handling deficiency. Do not insert topic-specific answers or a generic "explain more" mandate as the skill improvement.

Freeze the saved skill change. Have a fresh author generate the affected teaching from the original task/source conditions using the actual candidate PROF, rather than receiving the patched passage as an answer template. Recheck the triggering operation and a structurally changed held-out task with new students; include a passing/over-application control that the new instruction might damage. Keep baseline and task conditions matched when comparing teaching revisions; if a component must change, label the comparison limit. A manual lesson patch is diagnostic, not proof the skill now produces it.

Advance from material A to fresh material B with the saved effective skill and repeat the corpus loop. Re-open affected earlier evidence after later revisions and integrate all required source portions. No mapping entry, repaired example, single successful answer or early pass substitutes for the full assigned queue and current final evidence.
