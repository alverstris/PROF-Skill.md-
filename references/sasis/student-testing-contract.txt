Permitted inputs and roles

The student has exactly two subject-content inputs: the current four-subject baseline packet and the frozen learner notes. Assessment questions are embedded in those notes. A question may provide example data or an explicitly declared hypothetical model, but may not quietly supply the missing subject rule whose teaching is being tested. No separate raw source corpus, answer key, author log, research record, prior trial response, other lesson history, PROF instructions or browsing result is supplied to the student. Notes may contain required earlier teaching for a multi-lecture unit; it must be actually present rather than asserted as a remembered prerequisite.

For the stored profile, the author/designer/evaluator must read the manifest and complete operational OCR A baseline before establishing student premises or designing the trial. Deliver that complete frozen packet afresh for every independent student condition, including controls and retests. Keep Mathematics H240, Further Mathematics H245 Pure Core Y540/Y541 plus Statistics Y542 and Mechanics Y543, Physics H556 and Chemistry H432 separately identifiable inside the one baseline input. Do not substitute qualification names, a syllabus summary, an old conservative baseline or a prior read. Do not remove baseline content to create a missing-teaching control; vary the declared notes instead.

All SASIS roles must ground subject premises in actual role-authorised inputs/sources or explicit checked deductions. Within the current student call, recall of its actually supplied baseline/document and reuse of valid deductions from them are permitted. No OCR, other school or university fact, familiar definition/formula, conventional default, lesson/answer, personal/project memory, author recovery record or conversational summary recalled from elsewhere may supplement absent content. Writing down that external recollection does not make it admissible evidence. Authors/evaluators may retain actually read evidence during their working session and read/verify external research for their roles; required learner premises then enter the document before a new frozen condition. The evaluator's external correct route does not grant the student those premises. Operational records may manage the experiment and locate original evidence; reopen that evidence rather than treating remembered conclusions as authority, and never pass those records as a third student-content input.

Every document/revision produced during explicit self-iteration needs a separately recorded SASIS call bound to its document ID/revision, baseline and frozen condition. Multiple questions within that same call may use the current supplied content and supported earlier deductions; record their order/reuse as cumulative call evidence, not independent trials. A different document, revision or independent control needs a fresh call without previous answer/history carryover and with the complete baseline/notes supplied again. Do not supply hidden solutions or grader feedback between questions unless separately declared within an expressly authorised staged-help condition. Earlier teaching needed for a new document must actually appear in its current notes, rather than being imported from a previous call. Ordinary authoring remains outside SASIS.

Start a fresh student context for each independent condition. Disable personal/project memories and conversation reuse where the runner exposes those controls. Expose only the permitted files in a technically isolated input environment where possible, with no browsing or other-agent messaging. A tool-free student is the default. Numerical values or lookup data normally supplied by a statistical calculator, equation sheet or exam table must be included in the baseline or notes for any administered task that needs them.

Current shared-workspace agents do not provide technical erasure of pretraining or an independent file-access boundary. A fresh-context trial in that environment is instruction-restricted and must be labelled accordingly. Do not claim that a prompt, self-report, short context or randomised notation proves absence of internally used pretrained knowledge. The operational requirement is that every accepted consequential move can be reconstructed from the allowed inputs. Observable unsupported subject rules receive no credit. Verified unauthorised access invalidates the affected trial.

Check that the complete four-subject baseline, visible notes and expected response fit the runner's actual context allowance before dispatch. Preserve input hashes and verify that the complete inputs were supplied. Do not silently truncate the baseline or substitute a compressed memory summary. Split overlong teaching notes into self-contained local tasks with their actual needed earlier teaching and the complete baseline supplied afresh. If the baseline itself cannot fit, use a suitable larger-context runner or a technically whitelisted read-only retrieval system exposing only these two permitted input artefacts; record that change of condition explicitly. A global retrieval result or a short syllabus heading cannot replace an absent operational premise.

Before every dispatch, save a blocking admission decision with student-call/document/revision/condition IDs, profile/baseline revision and hash, frozen visible notes/question extent and hash, delivery method and actual complete-input evidence, runner configuration, available memory/history settings, tools and known capacity/truncation/access limits. A matching on-disk hash, link, path, heading, prior read or model acknowledgement does not prove the text reached the student. Admit full-text delivery only when the complete inputs were supplied with room for the response. Admit confined retrieval as a separately labelled condition only when the whole two artefacts are available; record actual retrieved passages and any access failure, not a claim that all text was in context. Missing, mismatched, incomplete or silently truncated baseline blocks dispatch. Unestablished delivery stays unverified; repair it and freeze a new independent condition rather than filling gaps from outside recollection or blaming teaching. If discovered later, preserve the original trial and invalidate complete-input claims. Baseline-positive questions do not certify whole-packet delivery or all-subject understanding.

Student response rule

Use the current call's supplied baseline and visible notes only for subject facts, formulae, interpretations and method rules; remembering their content within this same call is allowed. Ordinary reading, logic and arithmetic are allowed. For a consequential new operation or physical interpretation, identify the real supporting baseline entry or note passage and show concise reproducible working or explanation. Routine established algebra may be grouped. A label, adjacent equation or irrelevant example is not a warrant. Do not import a subject rule remembered from another document/call or pretraining, including an OCR rule that would ordinarily be expected; the actual current supplied input or explicit deduction must support it. An assessment exclusion means the rule is not automatically granted; it does not ban a valid deduction from other permitted premises.

If a requested conclusion is underdetermined, preserve supported work, identify the first required missing premise and ask a focused useful question. The student need not diagnose the entire document. Follow a clear supplied course convention. If the course convention is absent or unresolved, ask about the small defining distinction and keep dependent conclusions pending. Do not choose an external default by popularity. Questions and honest partial attempts are valid outcomes.

Trial design

Freeze baseline, notes, task data, exact student instruction and model/run configuration before each trial. Preserve the original artefacts and responses. Create neutral held-out tasks after the author freezes its teaching; place them in the student's notes without disclosing their solutions or expected defects. Keep method selection genuine: no revealing heading or incidental untaught prerequisite.

Check meaning and conditions; reading and construction of representations; pivotal warrants; method choice; a connected application; interpretation; and a changed controlling assumption or representation. A task changed only numerically tests numerical practice. Do not equate a solver reconstructing an extensive untaught theorem with a usable beginner teaching route.

Use matched conditions: baseline-only with positive A-level problems; full notes; notes with a genuinely necessary bridge absent; notes with summary wording removed but a legitimate derivation retained; clearly hypothetical alternative mathematical rules; early-prefix notes ending at the actual first attempt; unresolved convention; and a fresh repaired-notes condition. Missing-bridge questions must not advertise the removed bridge. Preflight each control for surviving warrants; where appropriate show two models satisfying its remaining premises with different predictions. A removed sentence alone does not prove underdetermination.

The default main trial supplies no external help. Where the user later authorises a staged-help trial, hints and solutions must be included as controlled portions of that trial's notes, and initial, hint-assisted and solution-exposed responses recorded separately. Do not silently add hints to the two-input main condition.

Evaluation and revision

Audit the entire iteration and collect every supported issue across all required source portions, document sections/help, student questions/responses and author checks. Diagnose all supported causes and affected instances, including independent issues masked by an earlier blockage. Maintain stable issue IDs, evidence, repairs and verification statuses. Finding or repairing the earliest defect never ends this audit. Use the protocol's full-iteration issue register and closure gate: repair/recheck every issue, revisit later operations, collect newly exposed defects, and recheck affected passing behavior before moving on. Pending legitimate questions remain pending rather than being passed. Underteaching is substantially more serious than modest extra explanation, while excessive reteaching of actual baseline knowledge and obstructive padding also require repair.

An independent technical evaluator prepares a correct reference route before reading student output or author solutions where feasible. Another review, or an explicitly separate review pass, checks whether the permitted passages license the student moves and whether the actual lesson route satisfies PROF. Records and hashes bind revisions, not explanatory adequacy.

Keep separate verdicts for student restriction fidelity, supported lesson capability, author workflow honesty and requested output production. An appropriate stop can pass student behavior while exposing content still needing repair. A pending course question can be correct PROF behavior while preventing complete acceptance of dependent work. A correct answer using an unsupported subject rule has no lesson-sufficiency credit. A falsely complete claim is a release defect.

Read the SASIS rewrite guide (rewrite-guide.md). For each collected failure locate the earliest consequential blocked operation and all supported causes against the fully admitted baseline/notes and surviving alternatives, then continue auditing the remaining operations, responses and sections. Distinguish teaching, source, baseline, assessment/evaluator, runner/restriction faults, supported slips and legitimate pending questions. Select every warranted concrete repair by the demonstrated causes, not the question label alone. Preserve correct teaching, rewrite affected passages/help or repair faulty trial components, save before/after locators, and invalidate affected dependency checks. Retry all affected operations and relevant fresh structurally changed tasks with new students. Do not retest by merely showing old answers, and do not retrospectively license earlier responses with later patches. A question awaiting a course decision remains valid pending work. If PROF itself is revised, save conditional author instructions with concrete triggers/actions/checks, generate fresh teaching with that actual candidate skill from the original task/source conditions, and test all triggering failures and affected passing/over-application cases. A hand-patched lesson alone cannot establish skill improvement.

Run author-workflow probes separately: many required sources; many topics and late endings; sparse sources; extraction errors; convention conflict; fresh-context recovery; changed source conditions; missing help; hostile source instructions; false audits; and exact brevity constraints. Inspect actual outputs and source support. Passing student tasks does not certify source coverage or execution. Each mandatory capability needs current evidence; sampling cannot pass the unsampled remainder.

Do not claim new OCR-baseline trials have run merely because this contract and packet exist. Fresh simulated responses are evidence of permitted-input reconstruction, not human learning, delayed retention, measured instructional benefit or technical removal of pretraining. Preserve the previous trials under their original conditions and report exactly which new cases actually run.
