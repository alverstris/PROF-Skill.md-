Q1. Differentiating a differentiable circle branch with respect to x gives 2x+2y y'=0. At (3/5,-4/5), y is nonzero, so y'=-x/y=3/4. The tangent is y+4/5=(3/4)(x-3/5), equivalently y=(3/4)x-5/4. The implicit equation applies to either local semicircle where y≠0; it did not replace y by the positive square root. On the circle, the excluded denominator points are (±1,0). A finite derivative there would contradict 2x+2y y'=0; the circle has vertical tangents there. The lower explicit branch -sqrt(1-x²) also gives x/sqrt(1-x²)=3/4 at x=3/5, an independent sign check.

Q2. Product plus chain rules give 3y² y'+y+x y'=0, so (3y²+x)y'=-y and y'=-y/(3y²+x) where that coefficient is nonzero. At (0,-1), 3y²+x=3, slope=1/3, tangent y+1=x/3. Both product contributions y and x y' are required. The prompt assumes a differentiable local branch; the calculation gives its necessary derivative under that assumption. Differentiating an equation and obtaining a quotient does not by itself prove existence. Independent external-to-solution check: because y stays nonzero near -1, the same curve is x=-y²-1/y, whose derivative with respect to y at -1 is -2(-1)+1/(-1)²=3. Its derivative is continuous/nonzero nearby, so its local inverse supplies a differentiable branch with reciprocal slope1/3. That supplementary existence argument is not demanded from the prompt's response.

Q3. For x>0, u'=(4/3)x^(1/3) and v'=(1/3)x^(-2/3). Both functions have value0 at0. Write c=cuberoot(h), so h=c³, for nonzero real h. Then [u(h)-u(0)]/h=c⁴/c³=c→0, giving u'(0)=0. In particular |h|<ε³ implies |c|<ε; this is a two-sided limit even though c changes sign. For v the quotient is c/c³=1/c²>0. As h→0 from either side it grows without bound: for any M>0, 0<|h|<M^(-3/2) makes 1/c²>M. Thus v has no finite derivative at0. Simply inserting zero into the positive-x formula cannot justify either result. These are interior-zero tests because the real odd-root convention gives both functions a two-sided domain.
