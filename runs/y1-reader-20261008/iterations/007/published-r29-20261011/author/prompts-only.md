P1. From the definitions sinh(x) = (e^x - e^(-x))/2 and cosh(x) = (e^x + e^(-x))/2, differentiate q(x) = cosh(2x). Show the inner derivative and the sign of the e^(-2x) contribution. Find q'(0), and explain why the ordinary cosine derivative does not supply the sign here. This is a generated application of the lecture's hyperbolic definitions. A complete response has the derivative, its value at zero and the sign explanation.

P2. Differentiate F(x) = sin(x)/(1 + cos(x)). Choose and name a rule, simplify your result, and state the real inputs excluded by the original function. Explain why simplifying the derivative does not remove those exclusions. This is generated practice. A complete response includes the rule's numerator before simplification, the simplified derivative and the original domain restriction.

P3. The curve x^2 + xy + y^2 = 7 passes through (1,2). Find dy/dx there and write the tangent line. Show how differentiation of xy treats both factors, and check the coefficient you divide by at the stated point. This is generated practice adapting the lecture's implicit-differentiation method to a changed relation. A complete response gives the differentiated relation, the slope, the nonzero-division check and the tangent equation.

P4. Derive d(cos x)/dx from the difference quotient, not by quoting the derivative. Use radians, cos(x+h) = cos x cos h - sin x sin h, lim_(h->0) sin(h)/h = 1 and lim_(h->0) (cos(h)-1)/h = 0. Show the two terms whose limits you take and explain which quantities remain fixed during that limit. This is an adapted task from the lecture's stated exam-review demand. A complete response preserves the minus sign and explains why it is legal to pull sin x and cos x outside the limit.

P5. For x > 0, find the derivative of y = x^x. Explain why inserting r = x into the constant-power rule d(x^r)/dx = r x^(r-1) is not justified. This is generated transfer practice. A complete response states a representation or logarithmic equation that keeps the varying exponent visible, differentiates it, and gives the derivative in terms of x.

(b) Differentiate B(x) = exp(x^2 arctan(x)), then evaluate B'(1). Identify the two contributions from differentiating its exponent and use arctan(1) = pi/4.
These are generated mixed applications. A complete response separates function and derivative domains in (a), and shows both factors from the product in the exponent in (b).
