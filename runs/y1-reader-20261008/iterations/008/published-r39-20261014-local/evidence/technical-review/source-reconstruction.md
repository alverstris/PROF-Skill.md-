Independent reconstruction by source demand

S01: geometric linear approximation and arbitrary basepoint (PDF p2 / printed p1, Figure 1 and boxed formula)

Let a=x0 and h=x-a. A line through (a,f(a)) of slope f'(a) is L(a+h)=f(a)+f'(a)h. This is the baseline tangent equation, not an extra line-fitting rule. The exact derivative definition yields

  [f(a+h)-f(a)-f'(a)h]/h -> 0 as h -> 0,

when f is differentiable at a, with h nonzero in the quotient. This supplies the local first-order meaning of the approximation. It does not supply a numerical accuracy threshold or prove globally increasing error with distance. Figure 1 depicts a tangent at the marked point, its intercept is not generally f(a), and x-a is the horizontal change from contact.

S02: logarithm at 1 and shifted input (PDF p2 lower half)

For f(x)=ln x, f(1)=0, f'(1)=1; L(x)=x-1. With u=x-1, x=1+u and the basepoint becomes u=0, giving ln(1+u) approximately u. The function input must stay positive: x>0, equivalently u>-1. Near-zero input refers to u, not x. This is a change of coordinate, not a change of the physical/function value. A learner can deduce it from baseline substitution and tangent rules.

S03: five basic linear formulas and graphical map (PDF p3 / printed p2, five-item list, proofs and Figure 2)

At 0, (f0,f'0) are sin:(0,1), cos:(1,0), exp:(1,1), log1p:(0,1), power (1+x)^r:(1,r), yielding x, 1, 1+x, x, 1+rx. Trig input is in radians. For arbitrary real fixed r, choosing 1+x>0 supplies a common real smooth domain; r is fixed as x tends to 0. The actual substituted small argument matters. The shorthand |x| much less than 1 is not a uniform precision guarantee as r varies without bound. Figure 2a maps sin near origin to tangent y=x; 2b maps cos at (0,1) to horizontal tangent y=1. A horizontal tangent can still be the first-order polynomial although its actual degree is 0.

S04: combining approximations, reciprocal shortcut, consistent order, derivative recovery (PDF p3 bottom and p4 upper half / printed pp2-3, Example 2 and footnote 1)

Define F(x)=exp(-2x)(1+x)^(-1/2), x>-1. The factor linear forms are 1-2x and 1-x/2. Their exact polynomial product is 1-(5/2)x+x^2. Only 1-(5/2)x is justified as the first-order product; the isolated x^2 coefficient is incomplete because both factors' omitted quadratic terms contribute. The source two-step denominator route uses sqrt(1+x) approximately 1+x/2 then reciprocal approximately 1-x/2; the footnote's direct exponent -1/2 is the simpler permitted alternative. All reciprocals are defined near zero. Independent derivative check: let g=F'/F=-2-1/[2(1+x)]; F(0)=1, F'(0)=-5/2. A bare arbitrary closeness assertion F approximately P does not by itself license reading off derivatives; the first-order residual property licenses that interpretation.

S05: limit from derivative/linear term (PDF p4 middle, Example 3)

f(x)=(1+2x)^10 has f(0)=1 and f'(x)=20(1+2x)^9, so its derivative-definition quotient tends to 20. Independent finite binomial expansion gives 1+20x+180x^2+960x^3+...+1024x^10 exactly. After subtraction/division by nonzero x, the remaining terms beyond 20 tend to zero. This validates the limit without relying on a vague approximately sign after division. To justify that route generally one needs a residual which, divided by x, tends to zero; small absolute numerator error alone is insufficient. This example is described as a first-exam question by the source; do not invent further assessment provenance.

S06: supplied relativity model, change of small parameter, ratio/units, GPS relevance (PDF p4 bottom, p5 upper half, p6 bottom, p7 top / printed pp3-6; Example 4 and Figure 3)

Use the source model T'=T/sqrt(1-v^2/c^2), with T>0, c>0 and speed magnitude 0<=v<c; write z=(v/c)^2. T is proper elapsed time onboard the moving clock; T' is the corresponding interval in the chosen observer inertial frame. Interpreting T' as merely any wristwatch reading without specifying the same interval/events is imprecise. Treat constant relative speed/inertial comparison and absence of gravitational effects as the model conditions; orbiting GPS needs additional corrections. This law is an explicit supplied physical premise, not deduced from A-level algebra.

The dimensionless ratio is T'/T=(1-z)^(-1/2), approximately 1+z/2 at first order in z and 1+z/2+3z^2/8 at second order. Those are degree 2 and degree 4 in v respectively: the word linear/quadratic refers to z, not v. Time intervals retain units by multiplying the ratio by T. The speed sign disappears because z uses a square. v=0 gives exact ratio 1; for 0<v<c the ratio exceeds 1. Thus any directional claim must distinguish the positive interval and speed domain. At the source v=4 km/s, c=3*10^5 km/s, z=16/(9*10^10)=1.777777...*10^-10, z/2=8.888888...*10^-11, 3z^2/8=1.185185...*10^-20. Exact Decimal calculation confirms error after the linear ratio about 1.18518518536*10^-20; after quadratic about 1.75582990425*10^-30. For a fixed ground-frame day of 86400 s the kinematic satellite lag is about 7.68000000034 microseconds under this speed-only model. This is useful scale verification, not a source-required extra exercise or a complete GPS model.

Figure 3 identifies observer, satellite and speed arrow only; no orbital radius, gravitational field or clock synchronization construction is encoded. The source GPS statement is qualitatively supported by primary official material. The atomic-clock comparison is a historical/instrument-specific remark, not a mathematical consequence or an evergreen universal limit.

S07: general quadratic, curvature and factor 1/2 (PDF p5 lower half and p6 upper half / printed pp4-5, Figure 4)

A polynomial Q(a+h)=A+Bh+Ch^2 has Q(a)=A, Q'(a)=B, Q''(a)=2C. Requiring equality with f's value, first derivative and second derivative at a uniquely gives A=f(a), B=f'(a), C=f''(a)/2. For an actual quadratic f this construction is exact. This proves coefficient matching; a blanket local error theorem still needs appropriate regularity and a warrant. For example C2 near a is a sufficient smoothness condition for f(a+h)-Q(a+h)=o(h^2); the name should be explained if used consequentially. The source's smooth elementary functions have the necessary derivatives near their stated admissible basepoints. For cos at a=0, f0=1, f'0=0, f''0=-1, hence 1-x^2/2. Figure 4 shows matched contact, horizontal tangent and downward curvature; the cosine sits above this parabola for the pictured small nonzero x. A second-order polynomial may have zero quadratic coefficient, as sine does; it need not have actual degree exactly two.

Independent sufficient-condition warrant: C2 on an open interval containing a means the first and second derivatives exist there and are continuous there. Baseline integration by parts/fundamental theorem gives, for either sign of sufficiently small h,

  f(a+h)-f(a)-h f'(a)=integral_0^h (h-t) f''(a+t) dt.

Subtract (h^2/2)f''(a). Substitution t=hs rewrites the residual divided by h^2 as integral_0^1 (1-s)[f''(a+hs)-f''(a)] ds, whose absolute value is bounded by half the maximum variation of f'' over the segment. Continuity makes that variation tend to zero. This is an explicit reviewer deduction from supplied calculus, not an inference that the lesson already teaches this theorem. Smoothness checks in the first applicable cases reduce to usual derivative formulas and keeping the logarithm/root arguments and denominators admissible. This sufficient condition is not asserted to be necessary.

S08: five quadratic forms and logarithm derivative check (PDF p6, list and proof)

The values of f''(0) for sin/cos/exp/log1p/power are 0, -1, 1, -1, r(r-1). Thus Q=x, 1-x^2/2, 1+x+x^2/2, x-x^2/2, 1+rx+r(r-1)x^2/2. Differentiating (1+x)^(-1) gives -(1+x)^(-2) as in the source logarithm proof. The same domain/radian/fixed-parameter conditions from S03 apply. These formulas also agree directly with the actual baseline FM27 expansions, which are independent of this lecture document.

S09: quadratic product and order accounting (PDF p7, Example 5)

Factor quadratics are exp(-2x) approximately 1-2x+2x^2 and (1+x)^(-1/2) approximately 1-x/2+3x^2/8. Constant-times-quadratic and linear-times-linear contributions all count: x^2 coefficient 2+1+3/8=27/8. The complete product of those two polynomials is 1-(5/2)x+(27/8)x^2-(7/4)x^3+(3/4)x^4; discard the degree-3 and degree-4 terms for a second-order result. Those higher coefficients are not the true higher coefficients of F because the factors' missing higher terms also contribute. Independent derivative check using g from S04: F''=F(g^2+g'), g'=1/[2(1+x)^2]; F''(0)=25/4+1/2=27/4, and dividing by 2 yields 27/8. A separate exact-rational convolution through cubic gives true next coefficient -163/48. Signed numerical checks at x=+/-0.1 and +/-0.01 discriminate sign or missing-cross-term errors and show local error scaling; they do not prove a global comparison. Original-source qualifications, not findings against unseen teaching

Q01 (PDF p3 proof of sine and final line in power proof): the source writes f(x)=x and f(x)=f(0)+f'(0)x=1+rx as exact equalities in places where it means the approximating polynomial. They are false identities for generic sine/power inputs. Preserve equality for the polynomial construction and approximation for the original function.

Q02 (PDF p5, displayed time-dilation linear approximation): T'=T(1-v^2/c^2)^(-1/2) approximately 1+(1/2)(v^2/c^2) drops the factor T. It equates time with a dimensionless ratio. Correct either to T' approximately T[1+v^2/(2c^2)] or to T'/T approximately 1+v^2/(2c^2). The adjacent annotation visibly has exponent 4 on (1+u), although it sets r=-1/2; this is another original typo. Both are confirmed visually rather than extraction guesses.

Q03 (PDF p2 statement that accuracy deteriorates as one moves away): local tangent accuracy does not prove monotonic error as distance increases for a general differentiable function. Counterexample f(x)=x+x^2(x-1)^2 at base 0 has tangent L=x; error is positive at x=1/2 but zero at x=1, farther away. For a globally affine function error is always zero. Use a local statement and any relevant function-specific sign/order warrant.

Q04 (PDF p5 'best-fit parabola'): Taylor matching means agreement of value, slope and curvature at the basepoint. It is not a least-squares or global optimality claim unless a separate criterion is given. p5 'only used when higher accuracy is needed' is motivation, not an exclusive mathematical use classification; higher order is not guaranteed strictly better at every finite input and can coincide when the added coefficient vanishes.

Q05 (PDF p7 atomic clocks): the dimensionless 10^-20 term is a correction to a ratio, not an absolute time uncertainty. Whether detectable depends on elapsed time, frequency stability, systematic uncertainty, averaging and the instrument. The dated 2025 primary laboratory result below reports 5.5*10^-19 systematic fractional uncertainty, not a universal theorem ruling out every 10^-20 measurement. Do not repeat the source's blanket best-clock statement as a current 2026 fact without a suitably scoped current source. The lesson can retain the robust mathematical comparison that the quadratic correction is about 10^-20 and far smaller than the leading 10^-10-scale change.

Q06 (source lists/approximation reasoning): restrict proximity to the actual substituted argument; preserve logarithm/root denominator domains. Approximation signs alone neither prove a limit after division nor uniquely determine derivatives. A valid first-order/second-order residual or the explicit coefficient-construction route supplies the needed license.
