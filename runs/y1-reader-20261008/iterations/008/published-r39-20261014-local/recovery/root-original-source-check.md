Source coverage and verified mathematics

- PDF page 2 / printed 1: tangent line at x0 has value f(x0) and slope f'(x0); ln at x0=1 gives L(x)=x−1 and the substitution u=x−1 gives ln(1+u)≈u near u=0. Domain x>0, equivalently u>−1. Figure 1 depicts local tangent matching; its generic distance/accuracy prose must not become a universal monotonic error theorem.
- PDF page 3 / printed 2: base-zero linear polynomials are x, 1, 1+x, x, 1+rx for sin, cos, exp, ln(1+x), and (1+x)^r. Trigonometric arguments are radians. For arbitrary fixed real r the real power is smooth on a positive-base neighbourhood 1+x>0. The sine and binomial proofs use approximate-polynomial equalities as if they were exact equalities for the original functions; preserve the intended approximation and distinguish it from the function. Figure 2 supplies the local shapes of sine/tangent and cosine/constant tangent.
- PDF pages 3–4 / printed 2–3: e^(−2x)/sqrt(1+x), on x>−1, has f(0)=1, f'(0)=−5/2, f''(0)=27/4. Direct derivative check: f'=e^(−2x)[−2(1+x)^(−1/2)−(1/2)(1+x)^(−3/2)]; f''=e^(−2x)[4(1+x)^(−1/2)+2(1+x)^(−3/2)+(3/4)(1+x)^(−5/2)]. Thus P1=1−5x/2 and P2=1−5x/2+27x²/8. The printed inference f'(0)=+5/2 is a source sign typo. Multiplying first-order truncated factors yields an x² cross term but omits other same-order contributions; it cannot establish the true quadratic coefficient.
- PDF page 4 / printed 3: for f(x)=(1+2x)^10, the difference quotient [f(x)−f(0)]/x tends to f'(0)=20. The derivative definition provides an exact limit warrant. Approximate numerator substitution alone requires an error term whose quotient tends to zero; the displayed approximate arithmetic alone is not that warrant.
- PDF pages 4–6 / printed 3–5: the borrowed special-relativity model is T'=T(1−v²/c²)^(−1/2). It is an introduced model premise rather than a theorem of the approximation method. With q=v²/c², its dimensionless factor has P1(q)=1+q/2 and P2(q)=1+q/2+3q²/8. For positive elapsed T and 0≤v<c, T'≥T, with equality at v=0; strict comparisons require 0<v<c. The page-5 displayed approximation omits its required factor T (or would need an explicit left-side ratio T'/T). Ground/satellite labels and Figure 3 do not by themselves specify an exact real GPS/gravitation model.
- PDF pages 5–6 / printed 4–5: matching value, first derivative and second derivative yields a polynomial a+b h+c h² with a=f(x0), b=f'(x0), c=f''(x0)/2. The factor 1/2 follows from twice differentiating the quadratic term. This explains the coefficient; a general error-order claim still requires its applicable smoothness theorem/conditions. Local matching is distinct from a global least-squares fitting claim. Figure 4 shows cos and 1−x²/2, which agree locally but separate away from zero.
- PDF page 6 / printed 5: quadratic polynomials are x, 1−x²/2, 1+x+x²/2, x−x²/2 and 1+rx+r(r−1)x²/2 for the five corresponding functions. A zero x² coefficient is allowed (sine). The logarithm derivative calculation is correct: f'(0)=1 and f''(0)=−1.
- PDF page 7 / printed 6: second-order products retain every contribution of the requested order; the exponential/power example gives coefficient 2+1+3/8=27/8. Terms of degree ≥3 are discarded only with the appropriate local error interpretation. The clock expansion is quadratic in q, hence quartic in v; name the small variable rather than silently interchanging polynomial degree.

Numerical and qualitative qualifications for later audit

Using the source's c=300000 km/s and v=4 km/s gives q=1.777777777777…×10^−10, q/2=8.888888888888…×10^−11, and 3q²/8=1.185185185185…×10^−20. The source's 10^−10 and 10^−20 are order-of-magnitude statements. Absolute time error is T times the omitted dimensionless correction; required accuracy and elapsed duration matter. The historical best-atomic-clock/GPS statements require appropriate primary-source support and scope if retained as factual claims. No current clock performance assertion is established by this source inspection.

Counterexample to treating generic local accuracy prose as global monotonicity: at x0=0 the cosine tangent approximation is 1. Its error is 2 at x=π and returns to 0 at x=2π. Being farther from the base point therefore does not universally make the actual error larger. This is a qualification of the original source, not an established failure of the unseen new generation.
