Independent derivations and checks

I1 Tangent: differentiability at a means f(a+h)=f(a)+f'(a)h+e(h), with e(h)/h→0; identifying L(a+h)=f(a)+f'(a)h gives value/slope matching and justifies reading a slope coefficient from a genuine first-order expansion. A loose numerical approximation alone does not determine an exact derivative.

I2 Logarithm/base shift: derivative1/x at1 gives L=x-1. Substitution x=1+u is exact, so log(1+u) approximately u at u=0. Domain u>-1; smallness concerns u, not x≈0. Trigonometric derivatives and approximations require radians.

I3 Basic coefficient table (value,first derivative,second derivative at0): sin=(0,1,0); cos=(1,0,-1); exp=(1,1,1); log(1+x)=(0,1,-1); (1+x)^r=(1,r,r(r-1)). For arbitrary real r, 1+x>0 is a sufficient real domain. Fixed r is important: |x| small alone does not give a uniform small numerical error over arbitrarily large r.

I4 Combined function derivative, independent of factor approximations: let f=e^(-2x)(1+x)^(-1/2). f'/f=-2-1/[2(1+x)], yielding f(0)=1,f'(0)=-5/2. Differentiate: f''/f=(-2-1/[2(1+x)])²+1/[2(1+x)²], hence f''(0)=25/4+1/2=27/4. Therefore L=1-(5/2)x,Q=1-(5/2)x+(27/8)x². Expanded retained-polynomial product is1-(5/2)x+(27/8)x²-(7/4)x³+(3/4)x⁴; the extra cubic/quartic terms of that product are not the actual full Taylor coefficients of f and must not be retained as such. Domain x>-1.

I5 Limit: finite binomial expansion (1+2x)^10=1+20x+180x²+... . For nonzero x quotient=20+180x+...; every further term tends to0 as x→0, proving20. A first-order expansion with e(x)/x→0 gives the same valid inference; 'approximately20' without remainder control is heuristic rather than a proof of exact limit.

I6 Quadratic1/2: P(a+h)=A+Bh+Ch², so P(a)=A,P'(a)=B,P''(a)=2C. Matching derivatives forces A=f(a),B=f'(a),C=f''(a)/2. If f has continuous second derivative locally, remainder is o(h²); bounded continuous third derivative gives a cubic-order bound. This is a local derivative-matching polynomial, not a least-squares best fit over an interval. Degree may fall below2.
