## Confirmed source errors

1. PDF 3, sine proof: after defining f(x)=sin x, the sentence ends with f(x)=x. This is an incorrect equality; the intended conclusion is f(x) approximately x near zero. For example, sin(1) is not 1.
2. PDF 3, power proof: f(x)=f(0)+f'(0)x=1+rx is printed as an equality. For general r this is an approximation; r=2 supplies the exact omitted x^2 term. It is exact only for special cases such as r=0 or 1.
3. PDF 5, time-dilation expansion: the right side of T'=T(1-v^2/c^2)^(-1/2) approximately 1+(1/2)v^2/c^2 loses the factor T. Corrected either T' approximately T[1+v^2/(2c^2)] or T'/T approximately 1+v^2/(2c^2). Dimensional inconsistency and v=0 expose the error.
4. PDF 5, bracketed reminder in that same line: (1+u)^4 approximately 1+ru prints exponent 4, although r=-1/2 is explicitly specified. The intended general exponent is r.

## Missing conditions, proof limits, and physical qualifications

- The PDF 2 sentence that approximation grows less accurate as one moves away is a local intuition, not a general monotonic-error theorem. A differentiable function may meet its tangent elsewhere; no universal claim about all farther points follows from differentiability.
- A numerical approximation sign alone cannot establish an exact derivative or limit. The conclusions in Examples 2-3 are correct because the retained linear expansion has o(x) remainder (indeed O(x^2) here); this justification is not stated when the notes infer exact values or divide an approximate relation by x.
- The quadratic formula needs sufficient local smoothness, for example f in C^2 near x0 for an o(h^2) remainder. The notes explain the factor 1/2 by exactness on quadratics but do not prove a general error theorem or give an error tolerance. A 'best-fit parabola' here means local derivative matching, not an unspecified least-squares optimum.
- Small |x| is not a uniform guarantee across arbitrarily large r or coefficients. The powers use fixed r and a positive base near zero. Dropping terms is an asymptotic order statement, not a guaranteed number of correct digits at an unstated x.
- The Planet Quirk model isolates the kinematic special-relativity factor; interpreting a real orbit also requires gravitational potential, the reference frame, synchronization, and signal-propagation effects. The source explicitly acknowledges other approximations, so omission of those calculations is not by itself a wrong GPS claim. NIST Technical Note 1385, section 2.3, independently confirms the kinematic clock slowdown and separate gravitational and synchronization corrections: https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/Legacy/TN/nbstechnicalnote1385.pdf (accessed 2026-10-08; relevant printed pages 3-4 / PDF pages 9-10).
- PDF 7's claim about the best atomic clocks is a dated empirical aside, not a mathematical consequence of the size of beta^4. No clock instrument, averaging interval, or accuracy/stability quantity is specified; this review does not certify a present-day universal precision limit. The computed correction size is independently confirmed above.
