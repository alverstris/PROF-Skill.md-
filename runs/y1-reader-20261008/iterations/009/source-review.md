## Confirmed source error

PDF 8 defines inflection points as points where f''=0, then says concavity 'usually' changes. Vanishing f'' is only a candidate condition when the derivative exists, not a definition or sufficient test. Counterexample: f(x)=x^4 has f''(0)=0 but f''=12x^2 is positive on both sides, so concavity does not change. Conversely x^(1/3) changes concavity at zero while f'' is undefined there (under definitions allowing a vertical tangent). The displayed cubic itself is correct because its concavity actually changes.

Minor notation error: Figure 6's caption repeats 'y = y ='; it does not change the function.

## Missing conditions and proof/figure limits

- PDF 3 calls only f'=0 points critical points. As a stationary-point convention this is coherent, but the unrestricted sketching rubric omits points in the domain where f' does not exist. A continuous cusp such as |x| at zero is an extremum missed by this rule and by a discontinuities-only preliminary step. Nondifferentiable points and domain boundaries must also divide the sign analysis. They are omissions of scope, distinct from the false inflection definition.
- Signs of f' and f'' determine monotonicity/concavity on intervals where the required differentiability holds. Disconnected domains cannot be treated as one interval: although (1/x)'<0 everywhere in its domain, -1<1 and f(-1)=-1<f(1)=1. The reciprocal example correctly warns against this; its phrase 'jumps from -infinity to +infinity' describes divergent one-sided limits informally, not a finite jump discontinuity or a value at zero.
- The second-derivative table establishes strict local extrema for an interior stationary point when f'' exists with the indicated nonzero sign (with f' defined in a neighborhood). It does not establish an absolute extremum. The f''=0 case is not included and is inconclusive: x^4, -x^4, and x^3 respectively supply minimum, maximum, and neither at zero.
- PDF 3 Figure 3 uses hollow circles to highlight all three roots and two stationary points of a polynomial. Reading these conventionally as excluded points would contradict the continuous polynomial and the calculated values. The coordinates are included; this is a marker ambiguity, not evidence of holes or altered source domain.
- The ln x/x endpoint discussion samples x=2^(-n) and x=2^n. If n is taken to range over reals this is a complete reparametrization; if n is an integer sequence, values on that sequence alone do not prove a full limit. Full independent witnesses: for 0<x<e^(-1), ln x/x<-1/x, proving the negative divergence; for 2^n<=x<2^(n+1), 0<=ln x/x<=(n+1)ln 2/2^n, proving the positive-end limit. The latter bound tends to zero, for instance by a binomial lower bound on 2^n. Thus the source's conclusions are sound, with a possible discrete-sequence proof step left implicit.
- The initial extrema sketches and tangent-sign diagrams are representative pictures. Concave-up does not require that a slope pass through zero; the captions describe their illustrated arcs, not every convex function.
