## Complete content and visual coverage

- PDF 1: Attribution/cover; no mathematics or figures.
- PDF 2 / printed 1: Example 1, Figure 1, and Example 2 setup. For f(x)=ln x/x on x>0, f'=(1-ln x)/x^2 changes positive to negative at e, so the maximum value is 1/e, attained at x=e. The graph's marked point, dashed guides, label x0=e, and 1/e height are correct. Its vertical and horizontal asymptotic branches are drawing limits, not included endpoints. Distinguishing abscissa from function value is mathematically sound.
- PDF 3 / printed 2: Example 2 Figure 2 and Example 3 cylinder setup. Figure 2 shows a continuous schematic curve with filled left/right endpoints and five marked stationary points: three peaks and two valleys. The tallest interior peak is red and labelled max; the lowest point is the red right endpoint labelled min. No numerical values can be extracted because axes/scales are absent. Figure 3 shows an open cylinder, height h and radius r, with a dashed rear bottom edge. The top ellipse is the rim, not an extra lid. For an ideal thin cylinder with V>0,r>0,h>0, V=pi r^2 h and S=pi r^2+2pi r h correctly count one disk and the side.
- PDF 4 / printed 3: Entire can optimization, endpoints/limits, Figure 4, and final proportions. Eliminating h correctly gives S(r)=pi r^2+2V/r on r>0. Differentiation gives S'=2pi r-2V/r^2 and the unique stationary r=(V/pi)^(1/3). Its derivative has the sign of pi r^3-V, so this point is the unique global minimum. S tends to +infinity at 0+ and +infinity; there is no maximum. S''=2pi+4V/r^3>0 is an independent witness. At the minimum, h=V/(pi r^2)=r, and S_min=3pi r^2=3pi^(1/3)V^(2/3). Figure 4 has axes S,r, a positive minimum, and arrows labelled 'to infinity' at both ends. Its left arrow is schematic evidence of divergence at r=0+, not an included finite endpoint. The source's correct r and h/r are unaffected by the erroneous displayed substitutions listed below.
- PDF 5 / printed 4: Wire Example 4, Figure 5, and all area calculations. The partition is length x and 1-x; square sides x/4 and (1-x)/4; area A=[x^2+(1-x)^2]/16=(x-1/2)^2/8+1/32. Thus A'=(2x-1)/8, unique critical point x=1/2, minimum A=1/32, and endpoint values A(0)=A(1)=1/16. Figure 5's colour segments, 0,x,1 marks, and side labels agree with these formulas. It depicts a generic unequal split, not the optimum.
- PDF 6 / printed 5: Wire conclusion and Figure 6. The drawn quadratic has its minimum at (1/2,1/32), symmetric endpoint heights 1/16, horizontal labels 1/2 and 1, and vertical Area label. These values are correct. The three hollow circles are point-highlighting markers, not deleted function values; treating the middle circle as a hole would contradict the explicitly attained minimum. Endpoint admissibility depends on the physical interpretation discussed below.

## Confirmed source errors

1. PDF 4, first displayed line: h=V/(2pi r) is wrong. The volume constraint implies h=V/(pi r^2). The very next S substitution and later h calculation use the correct expression. Units also expose the misprint: V/r has dimensions of area rather than height.
2. PDF 4, the printed interval 0<=r<infinity incorrectly includes r=0. For positive volume, both the physical can and 2V/r require r>0; zero is a limiting boundary, not a feasible endpoint.
3. PDF 4, the prose calls the second term 2/r; it should be 2V/r. The divergence conclusion remains correct for fixed V>0.
4. PDF 4, minimum-area line: the substitution 2V(V/pi)^(1/3) uses the positive exponent where 2V(V/pi)^(-1/3) is needed. The following stated answer 3pi^(-1/3)V^(2/3) is also incorrect; the answer is 3pi^(1/3)V^(2/3). Independent witness: V=pi gives r=h=1 and actual S=3pi from the original area formula, not 3pi^(1/3).
5. PDF 4, Step 5 says S 'will achieve its max and min' at candidates/endpoints. A maximum does not exist for this S on r>0 because S is unbounded above. Read as a general method, this statement needs an existence condition and inclusion of any nonsmooth candidates; read literally for this example, the maximum claim is false.
6. PDF 5, Figure 5's caption says Example 5, but the wire problem is Example 4.

## Conditions and omissions, separate from the errors

- The finite candidate comparison principle relies on an admissible domain and on an extremum actually existing. For a continuous function on a closed bounded interval, extrema exist; interior candidates also include points where the derivative is undefined. On open/unbounded domains, boundary limits are not values attained at endpoints. The notes' discontinuity warning is useful but does not state these full conditions.
- The can model presumes positive fixed volume, ideal cylindrical geometry and an open top, with no material thickness, seams, minimum dimensions, or manufacturing constraints. These assumptions are implicit in its surface-area formula. The source's infinity-at-the-ends wording is informal limit notation, not literal real-valued endpoint evaluation.
- The wire wording initially asks to cut into two pieces and make two squares. If both pieces must have positive length, 0<x<1: there is no maximum, only supremum 1/16, approached as either length tends to zero. If a zero-length piece is admitted and one square may degenerate, x in [0,1], then 1/16 is attained at 0 or 1. The final source paragraph explicitly recognizes nonattainment in the open interval and the one-square endpoint interpretation; its answer is valid only for that expanded feasible set. This is a modelling qualification, not an arithmetic error.
- A sketch cannot supply numeric maxima where no scale/function is provided (Example 2). It can identify which plotted candidate has the greatest/least height, as the source does.
- Figure 6's hollow circles are visually ambiguous under the usual convention for excluded points. The formulas and prose establish inclusion of x=1/2; endpoint inclusion is conditional on allowing degenerate squares. No interpretation can infer a removed interior minimum from the graphic alone.

All four numbered examples and all six figures were checked. No external lookup was needed; algebra, dimensional analysis, sign analysis and the explicit source resolve the consequential points. Open uncertainties: none beyond the stated physical choice about zero-length wire pieces.
