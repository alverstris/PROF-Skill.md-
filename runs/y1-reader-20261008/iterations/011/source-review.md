## Page coverage and independent technical witnesses

- PDF 2 / printed 1: Example 1 and Figure 1. The police are fixed at perpendicular distance 30 ft from a straight road; D is slant distance to the moving car and x is the road distance from the perpendicular foot. Figure 1 places the police above the road, car to the right, car-motion arrow to the left, labels 30,D=50,x, and a horizontal double-arrow indicating x. These agree with the intended right triangle, although no right-angle square is drawn. D^2=900+x^2; at D=50, the positive branch is x=40. Differentiating in time gives xx'=DD', so x'=(50/40)(-80)=-100 ft/s. The car's speed is |x'|=100 ft/s=750/11 mph, approximately 68.18 mph. Exact conversion of 65 mph is 286/3 ft/s, approximately 95.33 ft/s; the source's 95 ft/s is a rounded value and leaves the conclusion unchanged. The speed magnitude exceeds the limit.
- PDF 3 / printed 2: Both alternate radar derivatives, general strategy, and Example 2 cone setup/Figure 2. D=sqrt(900+x^2) gives D'=xx'/D and the same -100 ft/s. The suggested inverse x=sqrt(D^2-900) gives dx/dD=D/sqrt(D^2-900), valid on the positive x branch with D>30. Figure 2 draws an inverted cone, elliptical horizontal top, r from its center to the rim, and vertical h to the apex. It is a generic r,h cone; distinguishing water dimensions from the fixed full tank is completed explicitly on the next page. V=(pi/3)r^2h is correct for the water cone as well as for a full cone with the corresponding dimensions.
- PDF 4 / printed 3: Figure 3, similarity, all tank calculations, and satellite reference inspected. The figure is a half axial section: full height 10, full radius 4, lower blue water triangle of height h and radius r, shared slanted wall, horizontal water surface, and right angle on the large triangle. It is not a full-width triangle; the marked 4 is a radius. Similarity gives r/h=4/10=2/5 for h>0, hence V=(4pi/75)h^3, V'=(4pi/25)h^2h'. At h=5, the water radius is 2 and cross-sectional area is 4pi, so independently V'=4pi h'=2 yields h'=1/(2pi) ft/min. The source result is numerically correct but omits its final units. The bottom reference to Problem Set 3 supplies only a satellite sensitivity preview, not the full problem statement.
- PDF 5 / printed 4: Satellite Figure 4, derivative equations, and mirror Figure 5 inspected. Figure 4 has a filled satellite at the top of the fixed vertical segment c; L is the horizontal ground distance to the slanted line's intersection with the ground; h is the slant length. A right-angle square joins c and L. The vertical, horizontal, and slanted lines extend beyond the triangle for drawing purposes; these extensions are not new distances or negative lengths. Thus L^2+c^2=h^2 is consistent with the actual labels. Holding c constant gives 2LL'=2hh' and, where L>0 and h' is nonzero, L'/h'=h/L. Independently L(h)=sqrt(h^2-c^2) gives dL/dh=h/L for h>c>=0. The finite ratio tends to this derivative as the measurement increment tends to zero. ## Conditions, notation limits, and omissions

- The radar measures the rate of change of the line-of-sight distance, not the road speed. Its negative D' indicates decreasing distance in this setup. The source's phrase that -100 ft/s 'exceeds' the limit must refer to the magnitude 100, not an ordering comparison of signed velocity with a positive speed limit.
- The police position, perpendicular offset, and road geometry are fixed. The 50-ft separation is an instantaneous value, not a constant in the differentiation. The source correctly delays substituting changing quantities. Its blanket instruction not to plug in given values before derivatives is procedural shorthand; truly constant dimensions such as 30,4,10 may be substituted beforehand, as it actually does.
- Dividing by x uses x=40!=0. At closest approach x=0, D'=0 for any finite signed road velocity, and that instantaneous radial rate alone cannot recover road speed through DD'/x. The positive square-root inverse applies only on the displayed side of the perpendicular foot; a signed x or a separate branch is needed after passing it.
- The cone model assumes an upright rigid cone with a horizontal free surface, ideal volume geometry, and net inflow 2 cubic ft/min (no unaccounted leakage/outflow). The physical filling interval is 0<=h<=10, but the finite derivative formula solved for h' uses 0<h<10; at an initially empty apex, the ideal constant-inflow model has singular h' as h tends to zero, and after capacity is reached it no longer describes the water level without overflow modelling.
- PDF 3 calls V the volume of the tank even though the differentiated quantity is the volume of water. PDF 4 explicitly makes r,h the changing water dimensions and resolves the intended meaning. The tank's capacity is fixed, while V(t) here changes until full. This is a terminology inconsistency, not a wrong volume formula.
- In the satellite sketch, h labels a hypotenuse/range; it is not the vertical altitude, which is c. Dropping the derivative of c presumes it is fixed. If c varies, LL'+cc'=hh'. The sensitivity is undefined in the usual finite form at L=0 when c>0 and grows without bound as h decreases to c; the linear approximation is particularly local near that boundary. For finite Delta h, the exact quotient is (2h+Delta h)/(sqrt((h+Delta h)^2-c^2)+sqrt(h^2-c^2)), provided both slant lengths are admissible and Delta h!=0. This directly confirms the stated small-increment limit h/L.
- The ratio L'/h' presumes h'!=0; dL/dh is the underlying derivative even when a particular time trajectory has zero h'. Delta L/Delta h is an approximation to that derivative, not an exact finite-change identity.
- The mirror paragraph merely names Delta a/Delta theta or its reciprocal. A reciprocal derivative requires local invertibility and a nonzero finite derivative; it cannot be assumed at a focus/caustic or singular geometry without a specified model. No unique formula can be certified from this packet because the exact geometry and variables are not defined. The referenced problem set is outside the lecture-specific required source packet; no additional problem statement was inferred from the drawing.
