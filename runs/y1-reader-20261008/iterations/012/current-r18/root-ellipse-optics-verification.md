D012 independent check of the source's ellipse reflection statement

The source includes the general focus-to-focus reflection property, while its local drawing illustrates the lowest point. This technical check verifies that property; it does not require an unnecessary optics chapter in the teaching. The law of specular reflection is treated as the explicit physical premise stated by the source.

Let P lie on the nondegenerate ellipse |P-A|+|P-B|=L>|A-B|. Define unit vectors u=(P-A)/|P-A| and v=(P-B)/|P-B|. For a tangent displacement vector t, differentiating the two distances along a differentiable curve on the ellipse gives (u+v) dot t=0. Thus n=(u+v)/|u+v| is a normal. The sum cannot vanish here: opposite unit directions would put P between the foci and make L=|A-B|.

The incident propagation direction from A is u. Reflection across the tangent gives u-2(u dot n)n. Since u and v have unit length, 2u dot (u+v)=2+2u dot v=|u+v|². Therefore the reflected direction is u-(u+v)=-v=(B-P)/|B-P|, toward B. The scalar cancellation was checked symbolically, retaining the nonzero denominator condition. This proves the geometric consequence under the stated reflection law, rather than inferring the whole ellipse claim only from its bottom-point drawing.

At the lowest point the tangent is horizontal and its normal vertical, so the source's angles from vertical are the relevant angles to the normal. Equality of the two acute angles there agrees with this general property. This is mathematical/source verification, not an added reader input, a new source law, or a claim about real imperfect mirrors.
