P1. For f(x)=x^2 on [1,4], check the two MVT hypotheses, calculate the secant slope, and find every c strictly between 1 and 4 for which f'(c) equals that slope. Explain what equality of these two slopes means geometrically. A complete response identifies the interval conditions as well as c.

P2. Decide whether the MVT applies to each function on the given interval, and independently check whether its conclusion actually holds. (a) f(x)=|x| on [-1,2]. (b) g(x)=x for 0<=x<1, with g(1)=2, on [0,1]. For each, give the secant slope, the possible interior derivative values, and the specific hypothesis that fails. Distinguish failure of a hypothesis from a general assertion that a conclusion can never hold without it.

P3. For f(x)=x^2, take a=2 and x=2.2. Write the tangent-line estimate at a, calculate the exact value and its signed error (exact minus estimate), and find the MVT point c between a and x. Explain why replacing f'(c) by f'(a) changes an exact identity into an approximation. A complete response distinguishes the two slopes and states all four numerical results.

P4. A differentiable function p on the real line has p'(x)<0 everywhere and p(2)=7. What can you conclude about p(5) and p(-1), and why? Then decide whether the converse claim, 'every differentiable strictly increasing function has derivative strictly positive everywhere', is true. Use q(x)=x^3 to justify your decision, establishing its strict increase rather than relying on a sketch. A complete response gives the inequalities for p and a justified conclusion for the converse.

Explain why arbitrarily many strict finite lower bounds alone do not prove that the infinite series equals e^x. A complete response includes the derivative link and the missing limiting claim.
