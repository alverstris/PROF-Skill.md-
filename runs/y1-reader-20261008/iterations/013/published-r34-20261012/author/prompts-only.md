D013 exercise prompts — frozen prompt-only packet

Q1. For f(t)=t^3 on [-1,1], check the mean value theorem's hypotheses, calculate the secant slope, and find every c in (-1,1) with that derivative. Explain what the number of answers shows about the theorem. Your response should distinguish the theorem's existence guarantee from the calculation of the points.

Q2. Define j(t)=t for 0≤t<1 and j(1)=2. On [0,1], calculate the endpoint secant slope and every interior derivative value. Decide whether the mean value theorem applies and explain the exact condition that fails using a one-sided limit. Your response should explain why differentiability at every interior point alone is insufficient.

Q3. For f(t)=t^3, use a=1 and x=2. Calculate the tangent-line estimate at x, the exact value, the signed error f(x)-[f(a)+f'(a)(x-a)], and a point c giving the exact mean-value formula. Is the midpoint c=(a+x)/2 valid here? Justify using the derivative equation, not a sketch.

Q4. Let f be continuous on an interval I and differentiable at each interior point of I, with f'(t)<0 there. Prove that f is strictly decreasing by comparing arbitrary a<b in I and stating why the mean value theorem applies on [a,b]. Then consider k(t)=0 for t<0 and k(t)=1 for t>0, with domain all nonzero real numbers. Its derivative is zero everywhere on its domain. Explain why it need not be one constant on the whole domain, and state what can correctly be concluded on each side of zero.

Q5. For every x>0 prove e^x>1+x+x^2/2+x^3/6+x^4/24 by continuing the derivative-and-anchor chain. Identify the previously established inequality that gives the sign of your derivative, and handle x=0 separately. Explain why proving every finite polynomial lies below e^x would not, by itself, prove that their infinite sum equals e^x.

Q6. Without using an infinite series, establish for every x>0 the two-sided bound x/(1+x)<ln(1+x)<x. Choose the difference functions and the interval on which to compare them with zero, showing derivatives, signs and anchor values. Then decide whether the upper inequality ln(1+x)<x remains true for -1<x<0, and justify the direction of the comparison with the anchor. A complete response must account for the domain, strictness and the endpoint x=0.
