<a name="h1"></a>
Hint Q1

For the operator, make two entries: the derivative of the input $`x^2`$, and $`x`$ times that input. Add the entries. For the equation, insert $`x=0`$ into $`y=ae^{-x^2/2}`$ before choosing $`a`$. A division step cannot decide what happens when its divisor is zero: put the zero function into $`y'+xy`$ directly.

<a name="h2"></a>
Hint Q2

The factor that depends on $`y`$ is $`1+y^2`$. Its reciprocal has antiderivative $`\arctan y`$. After integration and the initial condition, compare the right side of your implicit relation with the range $`(-\pi/2,\pi/2)`$ of arctangent. Solve the resulting inequality in $`x`$ before extending any tangent formula periodically.

<a name="h3"></a>
Hint Q3

Start with rise over run for the ray: $`(-4)/2`$ at the given point, and $`y/x`$ at a general point. The tangent slope is twice that quantity. In $`y=ax^2`$, substituting $`(2,-4)`$ gives a one-step equation for $`a`$. Check the denominator of the original slope condition before discussing zero.

[Return to Q3](teaching.md#q3)

<a name="h4"></a>
Hint Q4

Eliminate $`a`$ from $`y=ax^2`$ and $`y'=2ax`$, then take the negative reciprocal of the resulting slope where allowed. The implicit family has form $`x^2/4+y^2/2=c`$; the point $`(2,1)`$ determines $`c`$. Divide by that $`c`$ and look for squared denominators. In the explicit square root choose the sign that makes $`y(2)=1`$, and require a strictly positive radicand for a finite derivative.

Hint Q5

In (i), the right-hand side is already a known function of $`x`$. In (ii), the right-hand side also contains $`y`$, so move the $`y`$-factor to the derivative side, retaining $`x<0`$ and checking any zero case separately. You can use $`\ln\lvert x\rvert`$ on this interval. For a proposed answer, compare its derivative with the equation's right side and also insert the supplied initial input; these are independent requirements.
