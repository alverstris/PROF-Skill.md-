Hint Q1

The statement about exponentiation describes the cases that survived division. Substitute the constant function y=0 into the equation before any division. For the other initial condition, use the fact that the exponential factor equals 1 at x=0. Then differentiate the proposed function and compare it with $`-xy`$.

Hint Q2

First locate the zero of $`1-y`$. For the nonconstant branch use

```math
\int\frac{dy}{1-y}=\int x\,dx.
```

The derivative of $`1-y`$ with respect to y is −1, so the left antiderivative needs a minus sign. After imposing the initial value, check the sign of $`y-1`$ using positivity of the exponential.

Hint Q3

Translate the slope condition first: the origin-to-point slope is $`y/x`$. After separation, the right antiderivative is $`3\ln|x|`$. Keep $`|x|`$ through exponentiation; on this interval $`|x|=-x`$. Use the point to determine the coefficient and sign, then calculate the derivative there independently.

Hint Q4

Substitute P into $`x^2/4+y^2/2=C`$ to fix the member of the perpendicular family. To read its semiaxes, divide by C and compare each denominator with a squared semiaxis. The negative y-coordinate chooses the lower square-root branch. Its radicand must be positive when y is strictly negative; combine that condition with positive x.

Hint Q5

For (a), no unknown y remains on the derivative's right side. Integrate, then use the point to fix the additive constant.

For (b), differentiate $`a/x`$ while a is fixed and then substitute $`a=xy`$. Take the negative reciprocal of the resulting slope, not of the curve's y-coordinate. The separated equation is $`y\,dy=x\,dx`$. Its constant is fixed by the point, and the point's positive y-coordinate decides the square-root sign. Compare the two slopes at that point to check the construction.
