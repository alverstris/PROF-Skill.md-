P1. For f(x)=x² on [0,2], use four equal strips. Construct the right-endpoint sum R4 and left-endpoint sum L4. Give a justified interval containing the area and its width. Explain why the ordering holds. Target: build and interpret finite rectangle bounds; a successful response includes sample positions, products, both sums and the monotonicity warrant.

P2. For f(x)=2−x on [0,2], use n equal strips and any selected point ci in strip i. Construct the right sum Rn, left sum Ln and arbitrary-tag sum Sn. Evaluate the common limit by using sum(i=1..n)i=n(n+1)/2. Explain why Rn≤Sn≤Ln for every permissible selection and why this establishes the integral independently of tags. Target: transfer bounds when monotonicity reverses; a successful response gives finite formulas and the shrinking enclosure, not only an antiderivative.

P3. In a generated one-year borrowing model, t is elapsed time in years, 0≤t≤1. Borrow continuously at rate f(t)=6000t dollars/year (the coefficient has the corresponding units when t carries units). Simple interest, without compounding or repayments, is charged at r=0.06/year. An amount dP borrowed at time t owes dP[1+r(1−t)] at year end, with all time differences in years. Construct and evaluate integrals for the borrowed principal B and final debt D; identify interest D−B and its units. Compare D with debt from borrowing the same total principal uniformly through the year, explaining the timing effect. Target: construct a weighted accumulation and interpret the weight; a successful response explains amount per strip and remaining interest time before evaluating.

P4. Revisit later, initially without rereading. (a) State the equal-width arbitrary-tag definition of the definite integral of a continuous function on [a,b], defining each symbol and stating what must be independent of the tags. (b) For f(x)=3−2x on [0,2], find both the definite integral and geometric area between graph and axis, choosing an efficient method and explaining the difference. (c) Set A(b)=integral from 0 to b of (3−2x) dx. Find A'(b) and say whether A is increasing at b=2; reconcile this with the nonnegative geometric area found in (b). Target: retained recall in (a), transfer to signed accumulation in (b,c); successful response distinguishes signed and unsigned quantities and supplies the sign-change location and warrants.
