Independent mathematical checks:

- For b > 0, n equal right-endpoint rectangles under x squared have total b cubed times sum(i squared)/n cubed. The exact sum gives b cubed times (1/3 + 1/(2n) + 1/(6n squared)); the left sum differs by b cubed/n. Both tend to b cubed/3.
- In a staircase with unit-height layers of square sides n,n−1,...,1, the inner pyramid can have side n and height n, and the enclosing pyramid side n+1 and height n+1. Their volumes bound the staircase between n cubed/3 and (n+1) cubed/3. In the source prose, “inner/outer prism” is an inaccurate name for these comparison pyramids. The diagram/formula show the intended pyramids.
- For the line x, the right sum is b squared times (n+1)/(2n), tending to b squared/2, the geometric triangle area. Differentiating the two area formulas gives b squared and b respectively; these examples alone do not prove the general accumulation theorem.
- A general Riemann sum must sample each c_i within its own subinterval. A finite endpoint sum is an approximation unless the model makes each rectangle exact; the existence of one chosen limiting sum does not alone establish sample-independent integrability.
- A borrowing rate in dollars/year multiplied by a year-width gives dollars. The source's final total-unit wording “dollars per year” is incorrect. Under continuous borrowing and fixed simple interest without repayment, the end-of-year debt is integral f(t)[1+r(1−t)] dt. The factor is dimensionless when 1−t is an elapsed time in years.
- For a constant continuous rate of 12000 dollars/year and r=0.06/year, the principal total is 12000 dollars and final debt is 12360 dollars. Twelve exact end-of-month loans of 1000 dollars instead give 12330 dollars. The right-endpoint discrete model for n equal loans differs from the continuous result by 360/n dollars. A lesson must distinguish the timing model if comparing them.
