## Interpretation decisions and precision limits

1. **Usable competence is the adopted baseline assumption.** Completion is not treated as mere exposure, but it is also not evidence of one individual's actual mastery. The baseline is suitable for provisional prerequisites; student diagnostics are separate and were not run here.
2. **Physics A is not reduced to elementary mechanics.** Two-dimensional collisions, potentials, escape, capacitor/radioactive exponentials, quarks and medical-imaging calculations remain inside it. The old conservative boundary would understate the specification.
3. **Formal calculus is separated by subject.** Physics A requires graphical rate/area reasoning and spreadsheet modelling but does not require explicit derivatives/integrals or solving SHM differential equations. A-level Mathematics/Further Mathematics can supply those tools independently. The user's Further Mathematics options are Statistics Y542 and Mechanics Y543; their formal methods are not attributed to H556.
4. **Formulas are accompanied by assumptions.** Constant acceleration, negligible drag, uniform/exterior spherical fields, nonrelativistic energy, small angles, steady/unloaded dividers, fixed R/C, constant attenuation coefficients, ideal transformers and echo geometry are made explicit.
5. **Symbols are disambiguated.** The baseline uses ℰ for e.m.f., U for stored/potential energy and Y for Young modulus where useful. OCR uses E for several different quantities, so a raw symbol does not determine its meaning.
6. **Doppler signs are explained.** The booklet's fractional wavelength/frequency shifts are used as magnitudes; signed frequency and wavelength changes oppose one another for a receding source. Magnetic force uses charge magnitude for size and sign for direction. Flux angle is to the surface normal.
7. **Some convenient derived rules are included as derivations, not extra OCR mandates.** Spring effective stiffness, r = mv/(|q|B), v = E/B, echo depth, X-ray cutoff and half-value thickness follow from the specified equations. A separately memorised pendulum period, MRI, lenses, general electromagnetic field equations and university quantum formalism are not inferred from H556.
8. **Supplied data are not confused with recall obligations.** The baseline includes constants and conversions to be self-contained. Several simple formulas explicitly required for recall (F = ma, W = mg, R = V/I, ½mv², mgh) are included even when absent from the formula booklet.
9. **Practical experience and assessment competency are distinguished.** The January 2026 handbook stresses experience of apparatus/techniques alongside CPAC skills. The specification contains some older wording about competence in apparatus; the baseline does not claim that a certificate proves expert operation of every instrument. Uncertainty/graph evaluation required in written exams need not all be assessed as endorsement criteria.
10. **Support examples were not copied uncritically.** The current mathematics handbook contains apparent arithmetic/description errors in selected worked examples: its power uncertainty row mixes a 400 mW result with an inconsistent intermediate percentage calculation, and its dice discussion misleadingly makes half-life depend on starting population. The baseline uses physically coherent standard propagation and decay rules. These observations are limited to those examples and do not invalidate the specification or the useful handbook guidance.

## Authoring review and accepted corrections

The Mathematics and Chemistry authors independently checked overlapping rules on 7 October 2026. This was a content review of the authored baseline, not a student diagnostic or a measurement of mastery. The following corrections were accepted and applied to both the internal Markdown source and the regenerated plain-text baseline:

- The ultrasound Doppler magnitude now uses `|cos θ|` when θ is an unrestricted directed beam-flow angle. It identifies transmitted frequency, flow speed, sound speed, the low-speed condition and approach/recession signs; the unmodified cosine magnitude is also explained for an explicitly acute angle. This tightens the sign convention in H556 6.5.3(g) without adding a different imaging technique.
- Radioactive λ is described as a constant conditional decay rate per surviving nucleus, rather than an exact finite-interval probability. The already specified exponential survival law implies interval decay probability `1 − exp(−λΔt)` and its small-interval approximation `λΔt`. The spreadsheet change `ΔN ≈ −λNΔt` now states the same small-λΔt condition. This is an elementary interpretation of H556 6.4.3(d–f), not an attribution of university probability theory. The population-independent half-life was independently confirmed; the support-handbook anomaly recorded above was not carried into the baseline.
- Both ideal-gas shortcuts explicitly require fixed particle number and composition, in addition to their respective fixed temperature or volume. Fixed volume alone cannot preserve `p/T` when the particle count changes.
- Heat-capacity and latent-heat expressions now specify the process/transition and distinguish transferred heat from internal-energy change when expansion work occurs. A stated closed-system energy balance `ΔU = Q − W`, with W defined as work output and unchanged bulk kinetic/gravitational potential energy, is identified as a deduction from energy conservation when the transfers are established. The packet does not forbid that deduction or silently supply further thermodynamic identities or numerical gas heat capacities.
- The kinetic-pressure equation now states its common-particle-mass condition and how to interpret its quantities for a chosen species of an ideal mixture. Mean translational kinetic energy is common at a common temperature, whereas rms speed depends on species mass; a mixture cannot silently use one species' mass for all particles.

An independent Physics review of the Further Mathematics Y543 mechanics packet checked rotation orientation, signed work/power, smooth oblique collisions and restitution, centre-of-mass integrals and geometry, unilateral circular constraints, dimensional analysis, and formal-calculus attribution against current H245 content 6.01–6.06 and the supplied formula booklet. Corrections were sent to the owning author; those files were not edited by the Physics author. The Mathematics A H240 calculus and mechanics statements were also read for cross-subject conflicts. Its formal component differentiation/integration is attributed to Mathematics, while the Physics packet retains graphical rate/area reasoning; these are compatible.
