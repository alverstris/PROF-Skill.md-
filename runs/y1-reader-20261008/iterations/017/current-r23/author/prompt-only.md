D017 important-answer check packet — prompts only, fixed before teaching solutions

Course: MIT 18.01 Fall 2006 Lecture 19, First Fundamental Theorem of Calculus. Use course convention FTC 1 = definite integral evaluation F(b)-F(a). All five tasks below are generated, not quoted assessment items. Baseline prerequisites: polynomial/exponential/trigonometric differentiation and antiderivatives, chain rule, signed integration, simple inequalities, absolute value, graph sign and elementary kinematics. Teaching will explicitly supply orientation/additivity, comparison and substitution warrants before relevant tasks. No learner solutions are in this packet.

P1. Evaluate integral from 1 to 2 of x^5 dx. Use F(x)=x^6/6, state why it is an admissible antiderivative, and explain whether using F(x)+7 changes the answer. Criterion: endpoint subtraction and explanation of the constant.

P2. A particle has velocity v(t)=2t-2 metres per second, with t the numerical time in seconds, from t=0 to t=3. Calculate displacement and distance travelled. Explain which is an odometer increment. Also evaluate integral from 3 to 0 of v(t) dt and explain why reversing integration endpoints does not describe additional physical travel. Criterion: sign split, units, endpoint orientation and physical distinction.

P3. Starting only from 1+t <= e^t for 0 <= t <= 2, derive a numerical lower bound for e^2 by integrating. Then state the correct comparison between integral from 2 to 0 of (1+t) dt and integral from 2 to 0 of e^t dt, explaining its direction. Criterion: evaluated simpler integral, algebraic bound and justified reverse orientation.

P4. Evaluate integral from 0 to 1 of x(2-x^2)^4 dx. Choose and state a substitution, transform the entire integral including differential and endpoints, and check the sign of the final answer against the original integrand. Criterion: consistent coordinate change and explanation of any minus sign.

P5. After a break, without consulting the worked examples initially: (a) write the FTC 1 endpoint rule and its continuity/antiderivative conditions, and write the definite substitution rule including transformed endpoints. (b) A particle has velocity v(t)=2t(t^2-1) metres per second, with t the numerical time in seconds, for 0 <= t <= sqrt(2). Calculate its displacement and total distance. State how you selected the interval split and integration method. Criterion: retrieve conditions/rules separately from applying them to a changed sign-changing composite velocity, with complete units and warrants. No assumption of observed learner recall is made.
