Concerns, consequences and necessary repair

C1 — established localized teaching omission, not a wrong answer.

Location: lesson line 196, before the general definite-substitution formula at lines 199–201 and its first concrete application at lines 206–218.

Exact issue: “Assuming u is continuously differentiable on the interval” introduces a technical condition whose meaning is absent. Baseline lines 138–166 give differentiability and derivative rules; baseline lines 178 and 213 and lesson line 23 give continuity information. Neither the read baseline nor the lesson says that continuously differentiable means that u' exists and is continuous. The full-baseline terminology search did not locate such a definition. Continuity of u and existence of u' are not, as a matter of mathematical meaning, the same condition as continuity of u'. The term cannot be replaced by the reviewer's own university vocabulary in a baseline reconstruction.

Obligation: T1 requires purpose, meaning and relevant conditions before consequential reliance. SKILL.md lines 101–105 and execution-protocol lines 47–51 and 75–77 require actual reconstruction from the admitted prerequisites, including checking assumptions. The substitution condition is consequential because it is presented as the hypothesis licensing the integral identity.

Consequence: the baseline reader can still compute every displayed example by already supplied chain-rule/substitution/endpoint methods. However, the general substitution theorem's condition is not fully interpretable or checkable from the lesson, so a complete explanation of its scope is missing. This defect is limited in severity and should not be inflated into a claim that substitution, nonmonotone mappings, or P1–P6 are numerically wrong. It is more than a preferred synonym because it changes which object's continuity must be checked.
