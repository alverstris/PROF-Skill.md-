Independent calculation packet — D017 r24 generation

These are the complete final learner prompts, without proposed answers or hints. All variables in the purely mathematical tasks are real and dimensionless. Trigonometric arguments are in radians. The original source uses FTC 1 to mean evaluation by an antiderivative. The teaching will introduce additivity, reversal of limits, integral comparison for increasing limits, and substitution through the chain rule.

P1. Evaluate the definite integral of 3x^2 - 2 from x = -1 to x = 2. Show one antiderivative, check its derivative, and show the two endpoint values before subtracting. Explain why an arbitrary additive constant would not change the result. Target: a reliable endpoint evaluation; the response must preserve the lower-endpoint sign.

P2. A particle has velocity v(t) = cos(t) for 0 ≤ t ≤ π. Here t is the numerical time in seconds and v is the numerical velocity in metres per second. Find its displacement and total distance over that interval. Identify the turning time and explain why the two totals differ. Target: distinguish signed accumulation from distance; include the sign intervals and units.

P3. A continuous function f satisfies ∫ from -1 to 2 of f(x) dx = 5 and ∫ from 2 to 4 of f(x) dx = -3. Find ∫ from 4 to -1 of f(x) dx and ∫ from 4 to 2 of f(x) dx. Can the geometric area between f and the x-axis on [-1,4] be determined uniquely from these two data? Justify the answer. Target: combine interval contributions with orientation, and recognise the information lost by cancellation.

P4. The lesson has established e^x ≥ 1+x for x≥0. Let r(x) = e^x - 1 - x - x^2/2. Use the given inequality to determine the sign of r'(x), then use r(0) to establish a lower bound on e^x. Integrate that bound from 0 to 1 to obtain a stronger lower bound on e. Show each inference and compare the result with 5/2. Target: build a bound from an already justified bound, rather than guess from a decimal value.

P5. Evaluate ∫ from 1 to 2 of x(4-x^2)^3 dx. Show a consistent change of variable, its differential, and the new limit corresponding to each old limit. Explain the sign of the final result by inspecting the original integrand. Target: retain the orientation when the new variable decreases.

P6. Return after a break, with the worked examples covered if useful. First state the hypotheses and endpoint rule of FTC 1 from recall. Then let f(x) = (2x-3)e^(x^2-3x) on [1,2]. Find both the signed integral of f and the geometric area between its graph and the x-axis. Choose and justify the method, identify every necessary split, and explain why a zero signed total would or would not force the geometric area to be zero. Target: distinguish recall of the theorem from transfer to a case requiring method choice and a sign decision. A complete response gives exact values and reasons.
