## Formula and numerical-support decisions

All assessed formula families relevant to the operational inventory are explicitly provided in the baseline, including formulas OCR normally prints on papers. In particular, the baseline supplies AP/GP sums, finite and rational binomial expansions, reciprocal trig derivatives, quotient rule, derivative definition, reverse-chain integration patterns, integration by parts, small-angle and angle-addition formulae, trapezium and Newton-Raphson rules, probability rules, descriptive standard deviation, binomial probabilities/mean/variance, normal sample-mean distribution, the paper's nine normal percentiles, and scalar/vector constant-acceleration relations.

PMCC critical values are provided by a question/table in the H240 assessment arrangement; a full critical-value table is not learner recall. Other numerical normal probabilities also require calculator support or supplied values. The baseline explicitly identifies that numerical-support dependency, so a notes-only diagnostic cannot silently assume an external table. Formula inclusion is a usability decision for the proposed learner model, rather than a claim that all formulas are normally memorised.

## Current LDS inspection

The [official H230/H240 workbook currently linked from assessment](https://www.ocr.org.uk/Images/308727-units-h230-and-h240-large-data-set-lds-sample-assessment-material.xlsx) was downloaded read-only. The entire Information sheet, all four data-sheet headers, and representative opening records were inspected. The workbook holds travel-to-work and age-structure census data for 2001 and 2011, aligned to 348 English/Welsh local authorities. Metadata defines the travel method by the longest part by distance of the usual journey and relates it to employment in the week before the census. It describes geography-code prefixes and authority mergers, plus age definitions and retained 2011 mean/median columns. The baseline uses a brief context description and interpretation rules, without reproducing the data or exhaustive historical authority-merger list.

The workbook additionally contains a blank Sheet1; it was not treated as an extra dataset. A large declared column range in one sheet is a workbook extent, not a claim of additional variables. Exact census counts were not turned into memorisation requirements. These historical census years are intentionally retained as the current qualification resource.
