Localized concerns and dependencies

C1 — Scope ambiguity / overbroad standalone condition, T33 before T35. Being defined throughout an interval is not by itself sufficient for the finite integral required by the definition of F. A restricted reading (“within the integrable/continuous setting being developed”) is sound; a literal sufficient-condition reading is not. The baseline already warns in FM28 about singular improper integrals and requires convergence. As a concrete permitted check, assigning f(0)=0 while using f(t)=1/t for t>0 makes a function defined on [0,1], yet the integral from epsilon to 1 is −ln epsilon and has no finite limit as epsilon approaches zero. The assignment at zero cannot fix the blow-up. Natural reader question: is being defined enough, or must the accumulation exist? Last supported statement is orientation for an existing integral. The generic rate example is conditional on existence until the next paragraph supplies continuity as the lesson's sufficient condition. All actual later continuous examples satisfy that condition; none of their results is blocked. I do not treat this as a reason to reject the subsequent proof.

C2 — Local task wording ambiguity, T258, later clarification T335. “Give numerical bounds to the nearest integer” may mean report rounded sizes of the rectangle bounds, or may be read as assert an integer-endpoint inequality. Those are different. The second reading is not licensed by rounding an upper rectangle from about 195.44 down to 195. The exact inequality is fully supported; nearest values 130 and 195 are approximate summaries only. A reader preserving that distinction can complete the task. The solution explicitly protects it, but that later explanation cannot retroactively make the earlier literal inequality request valid. This concern affects the requested form of Q4's numerical answer, not the monotonicity comparison or any certified prime count (none is claimed).

C3 — Finite-sum precision concern, T275 and especially T306. “A single endpoint value contributes no area” is sound as an integral statement. “Changes no rectangle-sum area: its possible contribution has zero width” is potentially misleading if taken literally about finite rectangle sums. A finite rectangle sampled at the endpoint has a nonzero interval width. In Q2 the difference is bounded by 2 times that end width and tends to zero, which is a short available derivation. The natural intended limiting-area reading is sound. I classify this as a local explanatory precision concern rather than a blocked concept, because the permitted baseline gives an accessible full route and all stated B(x) values and derivatives are correct.
