## Confirmed source error

PDF 6, Simpson numerical substitution: the printed parentheses make the expression (1/6)[1+4(4/5+1/2)], which is 31/30, not the stated 0.78333.... The endpoint 1/2 must be outside the factor 4: (1/6)[1+4(4/5)+1/2]=47/60. The general rule, input table and final decimal are correct. The error is visibly present in the original render, not a text-extraction artefact.

## Conditions and proof limits

- The displayed rules require equally spaced nodes, a<b, and finite function values at the sample points. Simpson uses an even number of intervals; a single panel has three nodes and two intervals. These distinctions are essential to the weights, though most are established by the source notation.
- 'Area' describes the pictured nonnegative examples. For sign-changing f the formulas approximate a signed integral; ordinary unsigned geometric area would require absolute values or splitting at zeros.
- The trapezoidal comparison is specifically with the displayed left/right endpoint sums. A midpoint Riemann sum already treats the interval symmetrically; not every possible Riemann sum is asymmetric.
- The Simpson panel formula is stated without proof. Integrating the arbitrary quadratic above verifies it; the same rule is also exact on cubics because the odd centered term integrates and sums to zero. It is not exact for arbitrary f. Composite quadratics need only meet at panel endpoints; their derivatives need not agree.
- The error remark O(h^4) is an asymptotic upper-order statement under sufficient smoothness, such as f in C^4[a,b] with fixed interval and bounded fourth derivative. One standard bound is |I-S_n| <= (b-a)h^4 max|f''''|/180. For comparison C^2 gives |I-T_n| <= (b-a)h^2 max|f''|/12. No such assumptions, constants, tolerance target, or mesh-selection rule are supplied in the notes. Errors may be exactly zero, so 'order of magnitude h^4' must not imply a nonzero lower bound. The example function is smooth on [0,1], so the order claim is applicable there.
- The opening 'no formula' follows a statement about elementary antiderivatives and should be read in that restricted sense. Both integrals have exact special-function or convergent-series representations; numerical approximation is not their only representation. For example termwise integration of the entire series gives integral_0^x cos(t^2)dt=sum_(k>=0) (-1)^k x^(4k+1)/[(2k)!(4k+1)]. The lecture does not prove non-elementarity, and this review does not supply such an advanced proof.
