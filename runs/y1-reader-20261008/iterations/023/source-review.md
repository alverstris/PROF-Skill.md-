## Confirmed source error

PDF 2, FTC 2 display: d/dx integral_a^x f(t)dt=f(t) should end in f(x). The integration variable t is bound inside the integral and cannot remain the free variable on the right. Taking f(t)=t gives F=(x^2-a^2)/2 and F'=x, a direct witness. The nearby definition of F(x) is correct.

## Conditions, signs and proof limits

- FTC differentiation requires suitable integrability plus continuity at the evaluation point (continuity on the whole interval is a familiar sufficient condition). The notes list the theorem without hypotheses; discontinuous integrands need additional care. Derivatives at interval endpoints are one-sided when only the restricted function is considered. A variable bound g(x) would also contribute g'(x), not included in the displayed fixed-a, upper-x case.
- Shell radius is distance to the axis: generally |x|, not negative x. Regions straddling the axis can create overlapping shells, so integrating both sides without accounting for overlap may double-count volume. The washer radii must be ordered nonnegative distances, and different horizontal ranges may require a piecewise setup. The listed x,y-axis choices describe the review scope, not a universal restriction on axes of revolution.
- Work=Force times Distance is valid for a constant parallel force or for the tangential force component times signed displacement. The general statement is the integral of force dotted with displacement. A perpendicular force performs no work despite nonzero magnitude and path length. In the pendulum the gravitational vector is constant but its tangential component varies; this is why the arc-coordinate calculation integrates mg sin theta. The vertical-coordinate calculation instead directly uses constant mg and the height difference.
- The source uses magnitude/downhill force and reverses the angular bounds relative to actual motion. This is consistent with its positive result, but not an oriented integral of positive-theta force from 0 to theta0. For signed or opposite-side angles the signed component -mg sin theta and actual travel bounds avoid ambiguity.
- The model assumes m>0,L>0, fixed pivot, a circular constrained path, approximately uniform downward gravitational acceleration g, and no omitted nonconservative work. The shown acute release geometry is compatible with a taut string. An arbitrary release above the horizontal from rest need not follow that circle with a string; a rigid rod or adequate tension would be required. Tension is radial and does no work along an ideal circular path.
- The computed work is work done by gravity, not externally supplied work. Returning the bob to its initial height costs the same energy in the ideal reversible case; a full cycle gives net gravitational work zero. Thus this calculation rules out net energy extraction from cyclic motion in the stated conservative gravity model. It is not, by itself, a proof about every conceivable machine. Ideal perpetual oscillation without energy extraction is also distinct from a machine providing unlimited net work. Friction would reduce mechanical energy rather than create it.
- The number g is an acceleration (units length/time^2); the source's informal 'gravitational coefficient' does not alter mg as the force magnitude. Resulting work has units mass times length^2/time^2.
