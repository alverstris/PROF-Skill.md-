## Complete content coverage and mathematical witnesses

- PDF 1: MIT OCW cover; no mathematics or figures.
- PDF 2 / printed 1: Entire sine/cosine power setup, the two basic primitives, Method A, Examples 1-2 and substitutions. Exponents are explicitly nonnegative integers. For odd sine exponent, reserving sin x dx and setting u=cos x turns even remaining sine powers into powers of 1-u^2; for odd cosine exponent use u=sin x. Example 1 correctly gives -u^3/3+u^5/5, hence the intended primitive -cos^3 x/3+cos^5 x/5. Its derivative is sin x cos^2 x(1-cos^2 x)=sin^3 x cos^2 x. Example 2 primitive sin x-sin^3 x/3 differentiates to cos x(1-sin^2 x)=cos^3 x. All these primitives and integrands are defined on R.
- PDF 3 / printed 2: Method B, both square-power integrals, Example 3 via two methods, secant/tangent family, identity and two basic primitives. Differentiating x/2+sin(2x)/4 gives cos^2 x; differentiating x/2-sin(2x)/4 gives sin^2 x. The product identity sin^2 x cos^2 x=(1-cos 4x)/8 proves Example 3's x/8-sin(4x)/32. Both source derivations reach that correct result. Also sec^2 x=1+tan^2 x, (tan x)'=sec^2 x and (sec x)'=sec x tan x on intervals with cos x nonzero. Two printed identity typos are listed below.
- PDF 4 / printed 3: Tangent integral, even-secant example, odd-tangent example and stated secant primitive. The real primitive of tan x is -ln|cos x|. For sec^4 x, u=tan x gives integral(1+u^2)du, hence tan x+tan^3 x/3; its derivative is sec^2 x(1+tan^2 x)=sec^4 x. For tan^3 x sec x, u=sec x gives u^3/3-u; derivative of sec^3 x/3-sec x is sec x tan x(sec^2 x-1)=sec x tan^3 x. All secant/tangent formulas are componentwise on intervals avoiding pi/2+k pi.
- PDF 5 / printed 4: Entire secant derivation and circle-area introduction. For u=sec x+tan x, u'=sec x u, and u is never zero where cos x is nonzero because (sec x+tan x)(sec x-tan x)=1. Thus ln|u| has derivative sec x. Figure 1 shows the full circle x^2+y^2=a^2, its horizontal diameter, an arrow from the center to the right rim marked a, and shading only in the upper semicircle. The whole drawn circle is not the graph of y=sqrt(a^2-x^2); that formula supplies its top half. For a>0, integral_(-a)^a sqrt(a^2-x^2)dx=pi a^2/2, as stated. No other figure is on this page.
- PDF 6 / printed 5: Figure 2, all changed limits and trigonometric-substitution calculations. The shaded region is bounded by t=0, t=x, the baseline and upper circle, with 0<x<a in the illustration; the remainder of the full circle is unshaded. Choose u=arcsin(t/a) in [-pi/2,pi/2], with a>0. Then cos u>=0 and sqrt(a^2-t^2)=a cos u, dt=a cos u du. The transformed bounds 0 and arcsin(x/a) and integral a^2 integral cos^2 u du are correct. The last displayed expression simplifies independently to F(x)=[a^2 arcsin(x/a)+x sqrt(a^2-x^2)]/2. For |x|<a, its derivative is sqrt(a^2-x^2); F(0)=0, so it is the claimed definite integral. F extends continuously to x=±a with values ±pi a^2/4. The lecture explicitly leaves the final simplification for Lecture 28.

## Confirmed printed errors and unrestricted formula failures

1. PDF 2, Example 1's final back-substitution prints -cos^3 u/3 instead of -cos^3 x/3. Since u=cos x, replacing u^3 by cos^3 u would insert an extra cosine. The immediately preceding polynomial in u and the derivative witness establish the correction.
2. PDF 3, parenthetical double-angle derivation prints cos^2 x-(1-sin^2 x). This expression is identically zero; it should be cos^2 x-(1-cos^2 x), giving 2cos^2 x-1. At x=0 the printed intermediate expression is 0 whereas cos(0)=1.
3. PDF 3, the common-denominator identity ends with (cos^2 x+sin^2 x)/cos^3 x. The denominator must be cos^2 x. For x=pi/3 the intended sec^2 x is 4 but the printed final quotient is 8.
4. PDF 4's tangent formula -ln(cos x), and PDF 4-5's secant formula ln(tan x+sec x), omit absolute values without stating a positivity domain. They are correct where cos x>0, but fail as real formulas on the other allowed components. At x=pi, tan x and sec x are finite, while both printed logarithm arguments are -1. Correct componentwise real primitives are -ln|cos x| and ln|tan x+sec x|. Intermediate ln(u) steps have the same branch restriction.
5. PDF 5, first line of the secant derivation loses an integral sign on the middle expression: it equates integral sec x dx to sec x[(sec x+tan x)/(sec x+tan x)]dx. The integrand rewrite is correct, but the middle expression must also be integrated for the displayed equality chain to be well typed.

## Conditions and omissions

- Trigonometric arguments are in radians. The definite substitution uses a>0 and |x|<=a; the geometric positive-area version illustrated has 0<=x<=a. For x<0 the same integral is signed and the formula is negative. If a=0 the area degenerates, but x/a and the displayed substitution are unavailable; negative a cannot serve as the depicted positive radius and would reverse the original bounds.
- Without the principal u range, sqrt(a^2 cos^2 u)=|a cos u|, not automatically a cos u. The chosen arcsine branch is exactly what makes the source's simplification correct. Endpoint u=±pi/2 has zero cosine; the original continuous integral is still proper, and endpoint values follow by continuity rather than differentiating the separate singular inverse-sine and radical terms there.
- The phrase 'n even' for secant powers needs n>=2 if the intended method is to reserve sec^2 x and obtain a polynomial in tan x. The declared family also includes n=0; pure tangent powers then need a separate reduction or rational treatment. Likewise n=m=0 in the sine/cosine family is simply the integral of 1 and does not require an eventual odd-power step.
- The power-reduction identities are valid generally; 'Method B requires both even' describes when this strategy is chosen, not a mathematical restriction on the identities themselves. Repeated reductions can produce a sum of integrals and constants rather than one original-form product.
- A globally one-to-one trigonometric substitution is unnecessary to verify the listed indefinite primitives: differentiation verifies each on its domain. For the definite circle substitution, a one-to-one branch and transformed endpoints are used explicitly. Constants of integration may differ across disconnected secant/tangent domains.
- Odd secant/even tangent cases are explicitly deferred. The packet does not claim to finish their reduction. The circle primitive is also deliberately left unsimplified, but its complete expression is already recoverable and verified above.
