## Complete coverage and independent witnesses

- PDF 1: MIT OCW cover; no mathematics or figures.
- PDF 2 / printed 1: Semicircle integral, substitution, domain-endpoint correspondence, primitive and actual definite limits. Figure 1 shows upper semicircle with horizontal labels -a,0,x,a and shading between 0 and x only, where 0<x<a. With a>0 and t=a sin u on -pi/2<=u<=pi/2, the radical equals a cos u. The full branch endpoints -a,a correspond to -pi/2,pi/2; these are not the integration bounds of the displayed 0-to-x problem. The notes subsequently give its actual bounds correctly: 0 and arcsin(x/a). Integral a^2 cos^2 u du=a^2[u/2+sin(2u)/4]+C is correct. The concluding sine identity sin(arcsin(x/a))=x/a is valid for |x|<=a.
- PDF 3 / printed 2: Figure 2, back-substitution and geometric check. The right triangle has hypotenuse a, opposite side x, adjacent side sqrt(a^2-x^2), angle u and a right-angle marker. It directly represents 0<x<a; endpoints are degenerate triangles. The cosine ratio is correct. The final result F(x)=[a^2 arcsin(x/a)+x sqrt(a^2-x^2)]/2 is correct. Differentiation for |x|<a gives sqrt(a^2-x^2), and F(0)=0. The sector area a^2 u/2 and triangle area x sqrt(a^2-x^2)/2 are correct. The preceding intermediate u/4 is a typo, listed below.
- PDF 4 / printed 3: Figure 3, all three table rows, square completion and substitution. Figure 3 splits the shaded 0-to-x area by the radius to (x,sqrt(a^2-x^2)) into an upper sector and lower triangle. Its angle u is measured from the positive vertical radius, consistent with sin u=x/a. Only the region between 0 and x is included. The three identity/substitution pairs are correct with branches specified below: x=tan u for sqrt(x^2+1), x=sec u for sqrt(x^2-1), x=sin u for sqrt(1-x^2). For the worked example, x^2+4x=(x+2)^2-4, v=x+2 and dv=dx. On v>2 choose v=2 sec u, 0<u<pi/2, giving sqrt(v^2-4)=2tan u and dv=2sec u tan u du, so the transformed integrand is sec u du.
- PDF 5 / printed 4: Secant primitive, Figure 4 and all square-completion back-substitution; rational-function preview. Figure 4 is a right triangle with adjacent side 2, positive hypotenuse v, opposite sqrt(v^2-4) and acute u. It only encodes v>2, not negative v. For that branch sec u=v/2 and tan u=sqrt(v^2-4)/2. Therefore integral sec u du=ln[(v+sqrt(v^2-4))/2]+C, and absorbing -ln 2 into the constant gives ln[x+2+sqrt(x^2+4x)]+C for x>0. On the full real integrand domain the correct expression has absolute values. The displayed rational preview integrates 1/(x-1)+3/(x+2) to ln|x-1|+3ln|x+2|+C; differentiating confirms it on each interval avoiding -2,1.
- PDF 6 / printed 5: Complete rational recombination and teaser. The algebra (x+2+3(x-1))/[(x-1)(x+2)]=(4x-1)/(x^2+x-2) is correct. Its primitive is already established on the preceding page; solving the inverse decomposition is explicitly deferred. No figures occur here.

## Confirmed source errors

1. PDF 3, first back-substitution display: a^2[u/4+(sin u cos u)/2] should have u/2. Its derivative in u with the printed coefficient is a^2(1/4+cos(2u)/2), not a^2 cos^2 u. The previous page and following expression both use the correct coefficient.
2. PDF 5, after Figure 4: integral 2sec u du is equated to a logarithm whose derivative is sec u. The 2 on the left is spurious; the preceding transformed integral was integral sec u du. Alternatively retaining 2 would require doubling the entire right side. This typo does not change the source's correct final answer on x>0.
3. PDF 4, the sentence defining dv prints dv=2sec u tan u without du. The next displayed integral includes du correctly. This is a differential-notation omission, not a wrong derivative factor.
4. PDF 5 prints integral sec u du=ln(sec u+tan u)+C without absolute values or a stated positivity restriction, and the final radical primitive similarly omits them. On the illustrated acute-u / x>0 branch the expressions are correct. On x<-4 the original integrand remains real but x+2+sqrt(x^2+4x)<0, so the printed logarithm is not real. Correct on both real components: ln|x+2+sqrt(x^2+4x)|+C. For example x=-5 gives positive radicand 5 but log argument -3+sqrt(5)<0.

Minor notation omission: PDF 6's final unevaluated integral lacks dx; its intended variable is unambiguous from the preceding line.

## Domain, sign and endpoint qualifications

- The semicircle calculation requires a>0 and -a<=x<=a; the shaded geometric decomposition uses 0<=x<=a. Negative x yields a negative signed integral. The principal arcsine branch makes cos u nonnegative; without it sqrt(a^2 cos^2 u)=|a cos u|. F extends continuously to ±a with values ±pi a^2/4. Its interior derivative tends to zero at the ends, although separate derivatives of the two summands have singular factors that cancel.
- The right-triangle pictures are branch devices, not proofs that x or v can be arbitrary signed lengths. For x<0 in the circle formula, the same final expression follows from the principal inverse-trig branch or signed symmetry, rather than a triangle with negative side length.
- The substitution table omits intervals. For 1/sqrt(x^2+1), x=tan u with -pi/2<u<pi/2 gives sqrt(1+tan^2 u)=sec u>0; its primitive ln(x+sqrt(x^2+1))+C differentiates correctly for every real x. For 1/sqrt(x^2-1), the real domain is x>1 or x<-1 and sqrt(sec^2 u-1)=|tan u|. On the positive branch use 0<u<pi/2; on the negative branch a suitable choice pi/2<u<pi has tan u<0, so a sign must be retained. The unified primitive ln|x+sqrt(x^2-1)| has derivative 1/sqrt(x^2-1) on both components. For 1/sqrt(1-x^2), use principal sine with -1<x<1, giving arcsin x+C.
- The worked square-completion integrand requires x<-4 or x>0. Points -4 and 0 are excluded because the denominator vanishes. The corresponding one-sided improper integrals are locally finite (inverse-square-root singularities), but that does not add the endpoints to the ordinary integrand domain. An independent derivative check for v=x+2 and R=sqrt(v^2-4) is d/dx ln|v+R|=(1+v/R)/(v+R)=1/R; v+R is nonzero on both components. Constants may differ by component.
- The opening 'Start/Finish' line describes the full admissible sine branch, not the limits for the shaded integral. Treating it as an instruction to integrate from -a to a would calculate a different area. The subsequent limit discussion resolves the intended use.
- Rational functions require nonzero denominators. The claim that rational functions can be integrated concerns primitives on their domain intervals, not convergence of definite integrals crossing poles. The preview does not attempt to prove the general partial-fraction method here.
