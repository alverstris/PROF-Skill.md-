## Confirmed source errors

1. PDF 3, repeated-factor B calculation: its first left numerator is x^2+1, whereas the original rational function has x^2+2. It must be (x^2+2)/(x+2). The following numerical substitution correctly uses 1^2+2, so the stated B=1 is correct despite this typo. The printed x^2+1 would instead yield 2/3 at x=1.
2. PDF 3, repeated-factor C calculation: the first left fraction has (x-1) to the first power below the bar, with a superscript 2 attached after the fraction at numerator height. The needed expression is (x^2+2)/(x-1)^2. Squaring the whole fraction or its numerator does not represent multiplying the original function by x+2; at x=-2 it would also contradict the next displayed value 6/9. The later evaluation and final C=2/3 are correct. This was checked in the render, not inferred from extraction order.

## Conditions and omitted parts of the general method

- Rational functions require a nonzero polynomial denominator and evaluation only where Q(x)≠0. All identities and primitives are componentwise on that domain. The first two examples exclude -2 and 1; the irreducible-quadratic example excludes only 1 over the reals. Constants may differ on disconnected intervals. No definite integral across a pole is justified by these antiderivatives.
- Cover-up evaluates a cancelled expression or a limit at a formerly excluded point; it is not direct substitution into the original undefined rational function. For the repeated-factor B step, the remaining terms after multiplication are A(x-1)+C(x-1)^2/(x+2), which tend to zero. The source compresses them as '[stuff](x-1)^2'; the bracket may itself contain a simple pole, so the vanishing conclusion needs the explicit remaining powers, as above.
- The stated proper-fraction condition deg P<deg Q is needed for the displayed forms without a polynomial part. If deg P>=deg Q, polynomial division must precede decomposition. That preparatory step is not demonstrated.
- 'Distinct factors' in the initial cover-up discussion must mean distinct linear factors for simple substitution at real roots. The later x^2+1 example correctly introduces a linear numerator for an irreducible quadratic. Over the reals, polynomial denominators factor into linear and irreducible-quadratic factors, possibly repeated; a full general template requires terms for every power of each repeated factor. The packet demonstrates a repeated linear factor but does not explicitly show a repeated irreducible quadratic with a general linear numerator at every power.
- 'Cover-up cannot be used to evaluate A' describes the simple direct-evaluation version shown. Derivative-assisted repeated-pole methods can recover it: the derivative at 1 of (x^2+2)/(x+2) is 1/3. This is a scope limitation of the elementary method, not an impossibility theorem.
- For the unfinished cos^18 u integral, the reduction is finite because the exponent is an even nonnegative integer. An independent exact representation is cos^18 u=2^(-18)[binomial(18,9)+2 sum_(j=0)^8 binomial(18,j) cos((18-2j)u)], obtained by pairing the exponential/binomial terms. Every resulting term has an elementary primitive, confirming the source's claimed completion route without requiring an unprinted numerical result.
