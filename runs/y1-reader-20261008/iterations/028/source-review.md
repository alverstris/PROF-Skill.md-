## Confirmed source error

PDF 2, Cartesian elimination: x^3=y^2 with x,y nonnegative implies y=x^(3/2), not x^(2/3). For t=1/2, the actual point is (1/4,1/8); the printed expression gives (1/4)^(2/3), which is not 1/8. The subsequent parametric length computation does not use the erroneous Cartesian formula and is correct. Figure 1 also depicts the convex rather than the incorrectly claimed concave power curve.

Terminology mismatch on PDF 5: 'For the top half of the sphere' introduces y=sqrt(1-x^2), which is a generating semicircle in a plane. Its full revolution produces the whole sphere, as the later bounds correctly demonstrate. It should refer to the top half of the circle; no factor of two is missing from the final surface area.

## Additional independent witnesses and conditions

- For Example 2 let z=3t/2. Then integral_0^1 t^4 sqrt(4+9t^2)dt=(64/243) integral_0^(3/2) z^4 sqrt(1+z^2)dz. A primitive of the last integrand is [z sqrt(1+z^2)(8z^4+2z^2-3)+3 ln(z+sqrt(1+z^2))]/48. Differentiating gives z^4 sqrt(1+z^2) exactly. Evaluation therefore yields A=28pi sqrt(13)/81+(8pi/243)ln((3+sqrt(13))/2). This certifies the unevaluated source setup and all scale factors; the source itself does not claim this closed form.
- The nonnegative speed/length element is ds=sqrt(x'(t)^2+y'(t)^2)|dt|. Factoring sqrt(t^2) as t is valid because the source explicitly restricts t>=0; otherwise it would require |t|. Although the given parametrization has zero velocity at t=0, it is injective on [0,1] and C^1, so the length integral is valid. The graph parameter x supplies a nonzero horizontal tangent direction there. A parameter interval that retraces a curve counts repeated traversal length and could overcount the same swept surface; that does not occur here.
- For revolution around the x-axis the radius is |y| in general. The source uses y>=0, so 2pi y ds is valid. A smooth or suitably piecewise-smooth generating curve and a single surface sweep are sufficient for the formula. At the pinched endpoint in this example the area integrand behaves as 4pi t^4 and is integrable. The figure's tip is included as a zero-area point.
- The thin-strip circumference-times-width argument is a limiting approximation. For a finite segment with varying radius, exact area is not simply one endpoint circumference times its arc length. A limit of frustum areas, or an independent surface parametrization, justifies the differential formula.
- Both examples concern curved surface only. Adding a disk at the right rim of the trumpet would add pi to a closed-boundary area; a solid spherical slice would need its planar end faces separately. The source's pictures/caption identify these excluded boundaries. The hemisphere result 2pi likewise excludes its equatorial disk.
- At x=±1, the semicircle graph derivative and ds/dx are infinite while y=0. The product cannot be justified by direct substitution of 0 times infinity. It is identically 2pi on the interior and extends by a convergent improper limit. An independent regular angular computation x=cos phi,y=sin phi,0<=phi<=pi has ds=dphi and area element 2pi sin phi dphi, reproducing 4pi and the same band formula without the graph endpoint singularity.
- The band formula presumes ordered real bounds -1<=a<=b<=1. Reversing signed integral bounds produces a negative number and is not physical area. Bounds outside that range are not on the unit sphere.
- Trigonometric substitution uses radians and a branch with sec u>0; otherwise sqrt(sec^2 u)=|sec u|. The stated t interval supplies that branch and finite transformed bounds. The source's generic numerical-method remark is valid for these continuous finite-range integrands, not a guarantee for arbitrary divergent or unspecified integrals.
