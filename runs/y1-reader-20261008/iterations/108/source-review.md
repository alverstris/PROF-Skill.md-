## Independently verified physics and answers

Throughout this report, k = 1/(4π ε0), outward surface normals define positive closed-surface flux, and vector radial directions are measured from the appropriate source center or axis. The stated source distributions are prescribed in vacuum unless a conductor is explicitly in electrostatic equilibrium. Signed charge and a positive field magnitude must not be interchanged silently.

**Flux and Gauss checks (P8–25; C4 §§4.1–4.2).** Directly integrating q/(4π ε0 r²) over a concentric sphere gives q/ε0 because ∫sinθ dθ dφ = 4π. For an arbitrary oriented patch the relevant factor is dΩ = (r-hat·n-hat)dA/r². The oriented solid-angle integral is 4π for an enclosed point and zero for an exterior point; superposition gives net enclosed charge for many sources. A source outside a Gaussian surface can contribute a nonzero E at every surface point while its total flux is zero. The arbitrary-surface extension and superposition are needed reasoning beyond P21's concentric calculation; they are reviewer reconstruction, not a derivation printed on that slide. The chapter's patch argument supports the local relation but does not spell out every possible crossing of a nonconvex surface.

**Standard symmetry calculations (P27–38; C4 examples4.1–4.4 and PDF31 table).** For uniform volume sphere radius a, q_enc = Qr³/a³ inside, hence E_r = kQr/a³ for r<a and kQ/r² for r>a; E is continuous at a and zero at its center. A uniform thin shell has E=0 for r<a and kQ/r² outward for r>a, with a jump σ/ε0. A uniformly charged infinite line has E_r=λ/(2π ε0 r), using side flux E(2πrℓ) and zero endcap flux. A single infinite plane has E_z=σ sgn(z)/(2ε0); it is uniform separately on each side, not one constant vector across both sides. The line/plane idealizations lack V(∞)=0 because their potential differences grow logarithmically/linearly. All displayed final standard-field formulas and graph shapes agree with these checks, subject to the defects below.

**Potential/group problems (P40 and P43–47).** Integrating the sphere E gives V=kQ/r outside and V=kQ(3−r²/a²)/(2a) inside, for V(∞)=0. Both V and its first derivative match at a; V(0)=3kQ/(2a). The slide's graph P45 agrees. For P40's slab −d<x<d, E_x=ρx/ε0 inside and ρd sgn(x)/ε0 outside. For P46 with V(0)=0, V=−ρx²/(2ε0) for |x|≤d and V=−ρd(|x|−d/2)/ε0 for |x|≥d. These are independently derived answers to unworked group prompts, not source answer keys. For P47's *uniform, concentric, thin* shells +Q at a and −Q at b: E=0 for r<a and r>b, E=kQ/r² r-hat for a<r<b; V=kQ(1/a−1/b) for r≤a, V=kQ(1/r−1/b) for a≤r≤b, and V=0 for r≥b. Shell uniformity (or an isolated concentric conducting arrangement producing it) is an assumption needed to obtain those fields; equal opposite total charges alone do not ensure spherical symmetry. Surface fields require one-sided limits.

**Conductors, energy and pressure (C4 §§4.3–4.4).** In electrostatic equilibrium E=0 within the conducting material, the connected conductor is equipotential, the tangential exterior field vanishes, and E_out·n=σ/ε0. This does not mean E=0 in a cavity containing charge or in a conductor carrying a current. A cavity charge q in a conductor of net charge Q induces −q on the inner wall and Q+q on the outer wall (example4.5). For the isolated spherical shell, V_inside=kQ/a, V_outside=kQ/r and U=∫₀^Q kq dq/a=kQ²/(2a)=QV/2 (example4.6). The extra factor1/2 is assembly of the source itself, whereas the interaction energy of a test charge Q in a fixed potential from other charges is QV. Two far-separated connected spheres satisfy q1/a1=q2/a2 to the chapter's stated approximation; their surface-field ratio is a2/a1. This is not an exact local-curvature law for arbitrary conductors in arbitrary external fields.

The force on a locally flat small conductor surface element must use the field of the other charges, equivalently (E_out+E_in)/2, not its total own-sided field. Thus traction is σ²/(2ε0)n and pressure is ε0 E_out²/2, as derived in Eq4.4.8. Patch replacement by an infinite plane is a local limiting argument; the field of a finite patch is not uniform everywhere. A static Maxwell tensor check T=ε0(EE−I E²/2) gives T·n outward for a face whose normal is parallel or antiparallel to E, and inward for a face with normal perpendicular to E. This verifies the box arrows on C4 PDF25 and exposes the prose error on PDF26.

**Appendix checks (C4 PDF25–30).** F=qE gives the stated downward acceleration of a positive charge initially moving upward in a downward constant field. Its kinetic energy decreases until the turning point, then increases. In the electrostatic limit, integrating the cross term of the source charge's radial field and a uniform external field in T over a sphere gives qE_external; the pure self and uniform-background terms integrate to zero. This is an exact reviewer check of the net force, not the chapter's order-of-magnitude estimate. Its estimate ε0 E0²A/2 with A=q/(ε0E0) is qE0/2 and is correctly only an order estimate. The depicted motion/energy-flow statements cannot be quantitatively verified from static frames alone. Time-varying field momentum and magnetic fields, and whatever external support holds the charge at rest in animation4.2, are not specified; do not treat the static electric tensor alone as a full dynamic conservation proof. Like charges repel and unlike charges attract in the pendulum examples, but the full pendulum motion also includes tension, gravity and the prescribed support motion. The source explicitly says the drawn field lines are artificially terminated; those ends are display conventions, not physical charge-free terminations.

**Solved problems (C4 §4.8).** The +σ plane at z=d/2 and −σ plane at z=−d/2 produce E=−σ/ε0 z-hat between and zero outside, as in Eq4.8.1. The square of side2ℓ, perpendicular distanceℓ from +Q, subtends 4π/6; its positive flux is Q/(6ε0), and the complete centered cube has Q/ε0. Reintegrating Qℓ/[4π ε0(x²+ℓ²+z²)^(3/2)] over x,z∈[−ℓ,ℓ] agrees with the chapter's final value, despite the separate primitive error F06. The gravitational analogue is ∮g·dA=−4πGM_enc; zero field inside a **uniform** spherical shell follows from symmetry and zero flux, not from zero flux alone. At an ideal massive shell's material surface one must distinguish limits. §4.8.4's final sphere-potential expression is correct despite its malformed intermediate integrand.

**Conceptual questions, C4 PDF37/printed36 (unkeyed; reviewer deductions).** (1) If E vanishes throughout an open region, Gauss implies total charge density zero there, not absence of positively and negatively charged constituents; E=0 at just one point is insufficient. (2) The plane has uniform field-line spacing on each side; more exactly, planar translation/reflection symmetry and a pillbox yield distance-independent magnitude. (3) A point charge q inside a sealed conducting pipe does not by itself determine the exterior field: an initially neutral isolated conductor has outer charge q and generally nonzero exterior E depending on outer geometry; a grounded conductor with no other exterior sources can have zero exterior field. Initial net charge, grounding and geometry are absent. (4) For isolated spherical conductors with the same Q>0 and infinity reference, the smaller radius a has larger V=kQ/a; nearby-conductor mutual effects are excluded by the isolated-sphere idealization.

**Additional problems, C4 PDF37–39 (unkeyed; reviewer deductions).**

1. §4.10.1: figure4.10.1 places the radius-R cavity center at displacement R y-hat from the radius-2R sphere's center. Superpose a full +ρ sphere and a −ρ sphere occupying the cavity: E=ρR/(3ε0)y-hat everywhere in the cavity. For arbitrary center displacement d-vector, replace R y-hat with d-vector. The source explicitly neglects material polarization.
2. §4.10.2: take zero background E. For the prescribed ±ρ0 depletion slabs, E_z=−ρ0(a−|z|)/ε0 for |z|<a and zero outside. Hence V(P1)−V(P2)=ρ0a²/ε0 for z1>a,z2<−a; reversing the requested order reverses the sign. The problem's charge profile is an idealized prescribed model, not a claim of uniform free-carrier density in a real junction.
3. §4.10.3: write the density coefficient as α (source calls it a) to avoid confusing it with a radius. Q(r)=παr⁴ and Q_total=παR⁴. E_r=αr²/(4ε0) inside, αR⁴/(4ε0r²) outside. With V(∞)=0, V_inside=α(4R³−r³)/(12ε0), V_outside=αR⁴/(4ε0r). Assembly energy U=πα²R⁷/(7ε0)=4kQ²/(7R), obtained from ∫[kQ(r)/r]dQ(r). Part(d) says cylinder despite defining a sphere; if “sphere” is intended, V(r)−V(0)=−αr³/(12ε0). The literal cylinder problem lacks a defined geometry.
4. §4.10.4: E_x=ρx/ε0 inside |x|<d/2 and ρd sgn(x)/(2ε0) outside, treating the “large” slab as laterally infinite or points well away from edges. A midplane-to-x pillbox with the midplane face at E=0 gives the same result as a centered pillbox.
5. §4.10.5: uniform solid-sphere assembly yields U=3kQ²/(5R), not the shell value kQ²/(2R).
6. §4.10.6: the graph joins V=15−5z² inside −1<z<1 metres to V=20−10|z| outside, with volts understood. E_z=10z V/m inside when z is measured in metres, +10 V/m for z>1m and −10 V/m for z<−1m. The slab occupies −1m<z<1m, with positive ρ0=10ε0 V/m²≈8.854×10⁻¹¹ C/m³. E is continuous at ±1m, so no additional surface delta charge is needed. The source explicitly says V is independent of x,y, permitting this inference.

## Assumptions, missing derivations and residual limits

P11's positive flux presumes choosing the open-surface normal along E. P16's sign statements concern the normal field component or net predominance, not a requirement for E to be normal everywhere. “Flux of field lines” is a visualization proportional to physical flux; the number of drawn lines is arbitrary. Q7's field-*magnitude* interpretation requires V to depend only on x, while the x-component alone follows from the displayed derivative regardless of unspecified transverse gradients.

The conductor statements are restricted to electrostatic equilibrium in its material. C4 PDF22's sharp-curvature argument is an approximation based on far-separated spheres; its lightning-rod sentence is qualitative motivation, not a validated lightning-protection design rule. No current safety standard or design recommendation was inferred. C4 PDF37–38's P–N junction story motivates a prescribed ideal charge profile; the record verifies that electrostatic model, not the accuracy of a particular semiconductor device's doping/depletion profile.
