## Conditions and independently checked derivations

- **C4 conductor claims:** E=0 means electrostatic equilibrium in conducting material; a cavity with a separate charge can have E≠0. Free excess charge migrates; positive ions in a metal need not travel as pictured. E_normal=σ/ε0 is signed outward; magnitude is |σ|/ε0. Surface potential is continuous and constant on each connected equilibrium conductor. The cavity example gives inner charge−q and outerQ+q. Integrating kQ/r² with V(∞)=0 gives V=kQ/r outside and kQ/a inside; assembling dq gives U=Q²/(8πε0a), distinct from a fixed test charge's qV.
- **C4 PDF21–22:** q1/r1=q2/r2 and E1/E2=r2/r1 use the explicitly distant spheres and negligible wire. The sharp-point discussion is qualitative, not an exact local-curvature law for arbitrary conductors. **PDF22–24:** the patch approximation is a local surface limit; field acting on its charge excludes its self-field, hence σ(Eabove+Ebelow)/2. This gives outward tractionσ²/(2ε0), including negativeσ. It is not the full external field timesσ without the factor1/2.
- **C5 §§5.1–5.4:** Q is a positive plate-charge magnitude and C>0; signed potential drops are distinguished from |ΔV|. The capacitor as a whole has net zero charge in the stated two-conductor model. Uniform plate fields, zero outside field and C=ε0A/d neglect fringing; “geometry only” also fixes the medium. Coaxial formula2πε0L/ln(b/a) ignores ends; spherical4πε0ab/(b−a) follows by radial integration, with4πε0a as b→∞. Series equal charges require initially neutral isolated intermediate nodes, explicitly stated in §5.3.2; precharged nodes are not the same problem. Example5.4 correctly gives(C1+C2)C3/(C1+C2+C3).
- **C5 PDF13–14:** integrating variable charge q gives U=∫0^Q(q/C)dq=Q²/(2C). “Battery work equals stored energy” needs quasistatic reversible charging; a suddenly connected fixed-voltage ideal source delivers QV while the capacitor gains QV/2 and the remainder has other destinations in a real transient. The alternative plate-separation argument's F independent of d requires fixedQ, not fixed batteryV. With fixedV, U decreases as d rises while mechanical work and returned battery energy still balance. Energy densityε0E²/2 equals conductor traction in the specific vacuum, normal-field boundary context; it is not an isotropic pressure law for every field geometry.
- **C5 PDF15, Fig5.4.2 prose:** equal potential of a contacting sphere and plate does not establish equal local charge density. Surface geometry controlsσ=ε0En. After detachment its **total charge**, rather than its pointwise charge density, is fixed in the ideal no-leakage model; surface charge can redistribute as the sphere moves. The text's density wording should not become a quantitative claim. The screenshot cannot independently establish the actual density or lift threshold. Electrostatic forces can lift the sphere, but the static PDF supplies no measured mass/voltage/threshold. Example5.5 arithmetic is39.825≈40J/m³ for its stated3MV/m; this is a nominal air condition, not a universal breakdown constant.
- **C5 PDF18–21, Eqs5.5.5–12:** bound surface chargeσb=P·n and uniform-P bulk charge zero are consistent. However Epolarization=−P/ε0 is the wide parallel-face/slab limit, not the field throughout the pictured finite cylinder for arbitrary aspect ratio. A finite cylinder's end disks give a position-dependent field. This missing geometric condition was independently checked against Markus Zahn, *Electromagnetic Field Theory*, Ch3 example3–2, printed166–168/PDF32–34: https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/res-6-002-electromagnetic-field-theory-a-problem-solving-approach-spring-2008/2cccce322d387cccbc78c3ed65843f49_MITRES_6_002S08_chp03_text.pdf . Linear scalarχ assumes homogeneous isotropic response, and the E0/κ reduction compares the same free-charge sources/appropriate plate geometry, not all externally constrained systems.
- **C5 PDF22–24:** isolated full insertion gives(C,Q,V,E)=(κC0,Q0,V0/κ,E0/κ); fixed battery gives(κC0,κQ0,V0,E0). Qp=Q(1−1/κ) uses the actual freeQ, not necessarily its pre-insertion value.
