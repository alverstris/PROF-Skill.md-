## Confirmed source errors — four grouped findings

**E1 — wrong force-equation cross-reference.** C8 PDF8/printed7, paragraph after Fig8.4.1, cites Eq8.4.1 to explain zero forces on sides1/3. Eq8.4.1 on that page actually gives the nonzero side2/4 forces. The operative relation is the current-element/straight-wire magnetic-force rule; the chapter's general relation is numbered8.3.1 outside this assigned excerpt. The zero-force conclusion and the displayed side2/4 equations are correct.

**E2 — loop-plane versus normal angle.** C8 PDF9/printed8, paragraph before Fig8.4.2, treats the loop and its area vector as if they made the same angle with B. The diagram and Eqs8.4.5–7 use θ between the **area normal** and B. The plane makes the complementary angle. Preserve the normal-based definition; the torque expressions are correct.

**E3 — inconsistent finite-wire angular integration.** C9 PDF6/printed5, Eq9.1.5 and preceding bound explanation, prints lower limit −θ1 but evaluates the integral as a positive sum of endpoint cosines. Direct evaluation with that printed limit gives a cosine difference, not the stated sum. With Fig9.1.3's positive acute endpoint angles and x=a cotθ, the correct lower limit is π−θ1 and the upper limit θ2. The symmetric case has θ2=θ1, not the printed θ2=−θ1. This fixes the derivation while preserving the correct final Eqs9.1.5–7. Independent Cartesian check:

`Bz = (μ0 I / 4πa) [ x / sqrt(a²+x²) ] from x_left to x_right`.

For endpoints ±L, this is `μ0 I L/(2π a sqrt(a²+L²))`; its infinite-length limit is `μ0 I/(2πa)`. No negative distance, field-direction reversal or alternate angle convention makes all the source's printed steps consistent.

**E4 — wrong spherical-coordinate figure reference.** C9 PDF27/printed26, paragraph between Eqs9.5.9 and9.5.10, refers to Fig9.6.1 for the polar-angle definition. The displayed location/basis diagram is Fig9.5.3 on PDF26/printed25. ## Assumptions, qualifications and unresolved source scope

**A1 — P19's force identity is conditional, not a general algebraic identity.** For a fixed small dipole in a prescribed static external field, the mechanical potential is `U=−μ·B` and `F=∇(μ·B)`. With constant μ,

`∇(μ·B) = (μ·∇)B + μ×(∇×B)`.

P19 drops the second term without stating a condition. Its simplification is valid in the locally current-free magnetostatic region used in the bar-magnet/coil examples (or when the extra term otherwise vanishes). It is not universally valid wherever B is nonuniform. The slide's “after math” omits both the identity and this assumption. The assigned C8 Eq8.4.15 correctly retains the gradient form. Caltech/Feynman II14 Eq14.2 supplies the magnetostatic curl condition; II15 §§15–1–2 clarifies fixed-current mechanical energy. This is a missing-scope condition; no wrong answer is inferred for the pictured free-space geometry.

**A2 — force/torque describe acceleration, and attraction requires an orientation/dynamical condition.** P8's rotation hand-rule prose, P10, P16 and Q3–4 assume a released coil initially at rest (or ask for torque tendency). Zero net force implies zero center-of-mass acceleration, not zero velocity. Torque gives angular-momentum change, not necessarily the current rotation direction. Settling into alignment requires damping; without it a suitably constrained rigid dipole can oscillate. C8 PDF12–13 itself describes undamped oscillation. For the particular narrative starting aligned and rotating, a turning point also requires initial rotational energy below `2μB`; otherwise full rotation is possible.

Q9–10's answer3 is the intended explanation for freely reorienting dipoles in the relaxing laboratory situation. “Free dipoles attract” is not an unconditional dynamical theorem: exact anti-alignment has zero instantaneous torque and is unstable, and undamped or rapidly moving/spinning initial conditions need not settle before separation. P24–25's attraction/repulsion applies to the pictured **coaxial** parallel/antiparallel arrangement; parallel moments placed side by side need not attract. Initial speeds, mechanical constraints, damping and live trajectories are not specified in these PDFs, so a universal future-motion claim remains unsupported. Preserve answer3 with these conditions, not as an always-true statement.

**A3 — nonuniformity permits, but does not guarantee, dipole force.** C8 PDF11/printed10 opening sentence says a nonuniform field will produce net force. The precise criterion is nonzero `∇(μ·B)` for the fixed dipole. A field can vary while this scalar gradient vanishes for a particular moment/orientation/location. P19's wording that nonuniformity is necessary is valid for the stated fixed dipole model. P20's “negative” derivative of a vector is shorthand for the downward axial derivative in the symmetric setup; vector order is not otherwise defined.

**A4 — energy/work and field-line language.** C8 PDF10–11 Eqs8.4.10–15 and P19 use the effective mechanical interaction energy for fixed μ/current and quasistatic external displacement/rotation. They do not include all electrical source/field energy. Calling the result magnetic work on the body does not assert that the magnetic Lorentz force performs work on each charge: `q(v×B)·v=0`. Faraday/field-line tension descriptions in C8 PDF12–13 and P11 are qualitative field-stress interpretations, not literal elastic strings. Equal/opposite mechanical torque exchange with the Earth's source currents is the quasistatic picture; a fully dynamical account must also include electromagnetic angular momentum. Caltech/Feynman II15 §§15–1–2 and II31 §31–6 support these distinctions.

**A5 — steady currents, moving charge and carrier sign.** P3 and C9 §§9.1–9.2 use the steady-current Biot–Savart law; the moving-charge formula is a low-speed quasistatic approximation. C9 PDF11/printed10 states v≪c but low speed alone does not eliminate acceleration/radiation or retardation at arbitrary distance. Feynman II21 §21–1 and Eqs21.21–24 provide the consequential qualification. C9 PDF10–11 Eqs9.1.18–19 take `I=nAq|v|` with ds parallel to v: this consistently uses positive carriers or signed current along the carrier path. For negative carriers it cannot simultaneously mean nonnegative conventional current with ds along conventional flow. The final signed-charge formula is correct.

**A6 — dipole/Earth model and coordinate transition.** C9 Eq9.5.5 is the external point-dipole field (or leading far field of a localized finite source), not an exact near field inside a real magnet/finite loop. The stated μE=7.79×10²² A m² and angles169°,109° are historical model inputs without an epoch/uncertainty supplied here. They are not a present-day measurement. NOAA confirms that the real field has multiple sources and changes with time/location, and that geographic-to-dipole coordinates require a rotation. No current MIT field prediction is claimed.

C9 PDF27 first obtains geographic colatitude48°, then magnetic colatitude37° relative to −μE, and uses37° in Eq9.5.10. The intervening description of θ relative to +k leaves this axis change implicit. **The numerical ratio is nevertheless correct for the stated inputs.** The north dipole axis is at colatitude11°, longitude289°, exactly the same longitude as the stated MIT position; the magnetic/geographic meridian tangents therefore coincide at that point. Direct Cartesian evaluation below independently resolves the apparent conflict. Away from this special meridian one must rotate/project rather than substitute a geographic colatitude into `2 cot θmag`.

**A7 — historical SI.** C9 PDF3/printed2 Eq9.1.2's exact μ0=4π×10⁻⁷ T m/A matches the 2005 SI definition. Since the 2019 SI revision μ0 is experimentally determined. This is a historical convention update, not a 2005 source error (BIPM resolution1, Appendix2).

## Independent mathematical and answer checks

The following calculations are reviewer checks, not claims that the slides contain these derivations.

1. **Uniform-field loop:** `F=I∮ds×B=0`. In P7/C8 Fig8.4.1, side2 has `I(−a j)×(B i)=+IaB k`, side4 the opposite. With r2=−(b/2)i and r4=+(b/2)i, total torque is `IabB j`. More generally `τ=μ×B`, μ=NIA n, and its magnitude is NIAB sinθ for the normal-based θ. `−dU/dθ=−μB sinθ` agrees with the aligning torque, with stable θ=0 and unstable θ=π.
2. **Nonuniform axial loop:** the Biot–Savart cross product in C9 Eq9.1.10 gives transverse terms proportional to cosφ′/sinφ′, both integrating to zero. `Bz=μ0 I R²/[2(R²+z²)^(3/2)]` is even and peaks at μ0I/(2R), as Fig9.1.7 shows. Differentiation gives `Fz=−3 μz μ0 I R² z/[2(R²+z²)^(5/2)]`. Aligned μ is pulled toward the loop for z≠0; anti-aligned μ is pushed away; the axial force is zero at z=0. This independently validates Eqs9.1.15–17 and the signs in the nonuniform-field PRS.
3. **PRS1, Q1–2, answer1:** for rightward separation and both currents upward, the left wire makes B into the page at the right wire. `+y×(−z)=−x`, so the right wire moves/accelerates toward the left; the opposite force acts on the left wire. `F/L=μ0 I1 I2/(2πa)` in the long-wire limit agrees with C9 Eq9.2.1 after axis relabeling.
4. **PRS2, Q3–4, answer1:** μ has positive upward component and B points right; μ×B points into the page, a clockwise torque. The depicted opposed forces cancel translationally. “Not move” means no translation from rest; it does not follow solely from zero force for arbitrary initial motion.
5. **PRS3, Q5–6, answer2:** upward μ in the displayed spreading upward field is attracted toward the stronger field below. In cylindrical coordinates at the loop, `eφ×er=−ez`; the outward Br produces downward force, while radial forces cancel by symmetry. This is also Fz=μz∂zBz<0 in the small-loop limit. Finite-loop direct force uses the actual outward radial field around the ring.
6. **PRS4, Q7–8, answer2:** reversing both current and B preserves `I ds×B`; μz<0 and ∂zBz>0 still give Fz<0. The south-up lower magnet/downward test dipole inset has opposite adjacent poles and agrees.
7. **PRS5, Q9–10, intended answer3, conditional:** for a fixed moment aligned with local B, U=−μ|B| gives attraction toward larger |B|; anti-alignment reverses that tendency. Options1 and2 incorrectly remove orientation dependence. The dynamics/relaxation restrictions are A2, not supplied experimental data.
8. **Coaxial pair versus orientation:** point-dipole energy `U=−(μ0/4πr³)[3(μ1·rhat)(μ2·rhat)−μ1·μ2]`. For coaxial parallel moments, U=−2(μ0/4π)μ1μ2/r³ and the radial force is attractive; antiparallel reverses it. For side-by-side parallel moments, U is positive and the radial force repulsive. These checks support the scope of P24–25.
9. **Dipole field and spherical components:** substitute μ=−μ k and rhat=sinθ j+cosθ k into `B=(μ0/4πr³)[3(μ·rhat)rhat−μ]`. It gives exactly Eq9.5.1, Br=−2Cμ cosθ, Bθ=−Cμ sinθ and ratio2cotθ, with C=μ0/(4πr³); equatorial/axial limits are consistent. The source defers its full derivation to unassigned §9.8, so this substitution is a check, not fresh inspection of that derivation.
10. **Earth-input arithmetic:** from angles169°,109°, μhat=(−0.0621213,0.1804134,−0.9816272). From MIT colatitude48°, longitude289°, rhat=(0.2419443,−0.7026572,0.6691306). Their negative dot product is cos37°. `B/(μ0 μE/4πrE³)=(−0.5175546,1.5030876,−0.6215472)`. Projection into geographic spherical directions yields Br=−1.5972710, Bθ=−0.6018150, Bφ≈0 and Br/Bθ=2.6540896. This confirms Eqs9.5.7–10 at their displayed rounding; it is a calculation within the source's dipole model, not empirical validation of its epoch-free Earth values.
