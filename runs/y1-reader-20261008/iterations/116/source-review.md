## Confirmed source defects

1. Chapter PDF p6, Eq. 9.1.5 and associated angle prose: the lower integration limit is printed as −θ1. With the acute endpoint angles of Fig. 9.1.3, the correct lower parameter is π−θ1. Evaluating the printed integral gives cos θ2−cos θ1, not the printed sum. The final finite-wire field μ0 I(cos θ1+cos θ2)/(4πa), symmetric result and infinite-wire limit are correct under their intended endpoint convention. The prose that makes one angle negative, and θ2=−θ1 in the symmetric case, is inconsistent with that convention. This is a derivation defect, not a defect in the final familiar formula.
2. Chapter PDF p35, off-axis appendix: the displayed current-element line writes I ds=R dφ(−sinφ i+cosφ j), omitting I on the right or erroneously retaining it on the left. Eq. 9.8.3 labels its left side ds×r-hat but expands ds×r, missing division by |r| if the hat is retained. The subsequent Biot–Savart expression uses the displacement vector over r³ and the final field integrals are correct. PDF p50 repeats the cross-product label r-hat while expanding the unnormalized displacement. These are dimensionally demonstrable intermediate notation defects.
3. Chapter PDF p41, Eq. 9.9.5: the printed prefactor in both forms is μ0 N I²/2. Differentiating Eq. 9.9.1 gives μ0 N I R²/2. The correct midpoint derivative is 3 μ0 N I R²(l²−R²)/[R²+(l/2)²]^(7/2). The Helmholtz condition l=R remains correct; the printed prefactor has incorrect dimensions. The magnified source equation confirms this is not an extraction artifact.
4. Chapter PDF p44, Animation 9.7: the caption says counterclockwise current seen from above; prose says clockwise, while both describe repulsion of a magnet whose north pole points downward below the upper coil. Counterclockwise gives upward coil moment, antiparallel to the downward magnet moment, and repulsion; clockwise gives parallel moments and attraction in the pictured coaxial configuration. The prose current direction is wrong for the described repulsion.
5. Chapter PDF p45, Animation 9.9: the text asserts a torque while the magnet is fixed upward and both coil moments/axis field are exactly parallel or antiparallel to it. At that exact orientation τ=μ×B=0. A perturbed antiparallel orientation can be unstable and acquire torque, which is different from nonzero torque at the stated collinear configuration. Zero net force also assumes the symmetric midpoint and equal effective coil strengths.
6. Chapter PDF p67, Problem 9.13.8(e): Planck’s constant is assigned units J/s; the required units are J·s. The numerical constant is the expected historical rounded value. The integer-angular-momentum statement is the Bohr orbit assumption used by this semiclassical estimate, not the general modern rule for orbital-angular-momentum magnitude.
7. Chapter PDF p69, Problem 9.13.13: the two supplied numerical answers do not follow from the stated n=16 turns/cm=1600 m⁻¹, I=1.3 A and χ=2.7×10⁻⁴. Within the stipulated long-solenoid linear-material model, M=χnI=0.5616 A/m and ΔB=μ0 M=0.705727 μT, rather than 0.68 A/m and 0.86 μT. No change of the stated data was assumed. This verifies the calculation under the source’s material model; no unstated temperature or chromium phase was chosen.

Minor source errors with recoverable intended referents: PDF p16 Eq. 9.3.2 repeats segment cd for the final da integral; p27 refers to Fig. 9.6.1 where Fig. 9.5.1 supplies the coordinate geometry; p31 cites Eq. 10.7.7 for 9.6.7; p32 cites equation “8-5” for the permeability relation; p39 describes z as measured from a coil center although the equations and picture use the midpoint; p49 cites Fig. 9.10.1 instead of 9.11.1; p53 writes endpoint (−a,+d) in a problem whose dimension is b; p60 Fig. 9.11.11 labels a differential strip’s current I although the immediately preceding calculation correctly uses dI. Presentation p34 labels J=J0R/r as “total current”; it is a current density.

## Conditional models and unresolved source scope

- The chapter’s Biot–Savart and Ampere laws are magnetostatic. Presentation pp23–28 and p49 do not establish their unrestricted validity for changing fields. Current piercing a loop controls circulation, not whether local B exists anywhere on the loop. The symmetry table describes when Ampere’s law directly determines a field, rather than where the law is valid. Loop/surface orientations must agree. For arbitrary closed planar contours the angular integral is 2π times the winding number, not always 2π.
- Infinite wire, ideal solenoid and ideal toroidal confinement require their stated or implied geometric idealizations. Chapter p23’s nearly-uniform claim throughout |z|<l/2 is too broad near the finite solenoid ends: the exact formula tends to B0/2 at an end of a very long solenoid. The finite-solenoid antiderivative on p22 is correct: its numerator is z′−z. Uniform volume-current results extend continuously to their interfaces; an ideal surface sheet instead has specified one-sided tangential fields. The thin-sheet limit keeps K=Jb fixed.
- Presentation p34’s J=J0R/r is integrable in total current but singular at r=0. Direct integration gives Ienc(r)=2πJ0Rr, I=2πJ0R², Bφ=μ0J0R for 0<r<R and μ0J0R²/r outside. At the axis its azimuthal direction has no continuous unique limit; “everywhere” needs an axis limitation or a regularized model. No ordinary B(0) was silently supplied.
- Presentation pp11–15: diamagnetic levitation requires a suitable field gradient and gravity balance, not merely a strong uniform field. For weak isotropic induced response the energy is −χVB²/(2μ0), with the factor 1/2 absent from the fixed-dipole energy; F=χV∇B²/(2μ0). χ<0 seeks weaker field, but stability requires the full local energy curvature. The superconducting image construction correctly reverses the normal moment and retains the parallel moment. Repulsion at arbitrary angle over an ideal infinite plane does not by itself establish full translational and orientational stability; horizontal displacement is neutral in that model, and flux pinning/finite geometry are not specified.
- Material relations B_M=μ0M and B=B0+μ0M follow the chapter’s long cylinder with axial uniform magnetization. They are not shape-independent statements for arbitrary samples. Linear susceptibility is an assumed regime; the chapter explicitly supplies ferromagnetic domains, nonlinearity and hysteresis. No microscopic field is equated to the volume average. The moving-point-charge formula requires the quasistatic/nonradiative setting in addition to low speed if acceleration is allowed.
- Chapter pp43–44: the statement that the field at the midpoint vanishes applies to equal opposite coils (Fig. 9.9.6a). Caption 9.9.6b instead states that the bottom current is twice the top current, and its pictured null is above the upper loop. Those configurations must not be merged. Presentation pp37–39 show component field lines from separate coils; their intersections are not intersections of one total vector field.
- Chapter p64 Problem 9.13.3 supplies only outer radius a in text; the drawing places P at the outer center and the hole apparently tangent at both P and the outside. If those intended geometric relations are exact, the hole radius and offset are a/2. Otherwise the hole radius/offset are missing numerical geometric data. The general cavity result B=(μ0J/2) z-hat×d records the dependence without silently choosing dimensions.
- Chapter p65 Problem 9.13.4(c) leaves the winding/return-current model important: the ideal stack of closed circular loops has no azimuthal field; a real single helix carries net axial current and produces an exterior azimuthal component. Its return lead changes that component. “In the direction of the wire” is also distinct from a purely azimuthal component for a finite-pitch helix. The assigned source does not resolve these choices. At the stated radius 0.06 m the isolated-helix axial-current contribution would be μ0I/(2πr)=1.0 μT; the ideal ring model gives zero azimuthal component.
- Chapter p66 Problem 9.13.7 literally says “an finitely long wire” and gives no wire length or endpoints. Its usual infinite-wire force is a conditional result, not an exact consequence for an unspecified finite wire. Chapter p68 Problem 9.13.10 gives nonuniform coil B: instantaneous curvature is determined, but a global circular path or constant-radius helix is not justified. Particle mass is needed as a parameter.

## Independent equation and answer witnesses

These are source checks, including unsolved source prompts; they are not an authored lesson or a learner assessment.

- Examples 9.1–9.2: ds×(rP−r′), with the correct limits, gives the finite-wire field and Bz=μ0IR²/[2(R²+z²)^(3/2)]. The loop’s transverse components cancel. For a fixed axial μ, Fz=−3μzμ0IR²z/[2(R²+z²)^(5/2)], with the expected attraction/repulsion sign. The loop profile is even and peaks at z=0.
- Parallel wires: force per length μ0I1I2/(2πa), attractive for equal direction. Example 9.3’s uniform wire has B∝r inside, 1/r outside, matching at R. Example 9.4’s x-current sheet has By=−μ0Jz inside |z|<b/2 and −μ0Jb sign(z)/2 outside. Presentation’s differently oriented z-current sheet instead has By=μ0Jx inside |x|<d and μ0Jd sign(x) outside. Sheet units distinguish J (A/m²) from K=2dJ=nI (A/m).
- Ideal solenoid B=μ0nI and toroid B=μ0NI/(2πr) in its annulus follow the depicted oriented paths. Chapter finite-solenoid limits recover the infinite central value and far decay. Off-axis loop integrals reduce to the axial formula at y=0, and their dipole limit yields μ0[3(μ·r-hat)r-hat−μ]/(4πr³) away from the origin.
- Earth-field example: the given dipole angles (169°,109°) and MIT angles (48°,289°) lie on one magnetic meridian. Direct vector substitution gives Bφ=0 to roundoff and Br/Bθ=2.65409, supporting the printed 2.65. The general tilted-dipole coordinate concern does not invalidate this particular numerical example.
- Helmholtz pair: same-current B is even; B′(0)=B‴(0)=0 and B″(0)=0 at l=R. At the exact midpoint the fixed axial dipole force is zero, not merely small. Opposite equal currents give odd B and B′(0)=3μ0NIR²l/[2(R²+l²/4)^(5/2)], confirming Eqs. 9.9.10–12 and the nonzero central dipole force.
- Solved Problems 9.11.1–8: the general finite-wire Cartesian expression has the right y sign and infinite-length limit; points on the line-current segment are excluded, and y=0 outside it is a removable limiting case. Arc result μ0Iθ(1/a−1/b)/(4π) points into the page; radial segments contribute zero. Rectangular U-wire result simplifies to μ0I[b−sqrt(a²+b²)]/(2πab), into the page and tending to zero as a→0 for b>0. Hairpin B=μ0I(π+2)/(4πr) points out. Two equal −x wires give By=μ0Iz/[π(a²+z²)], with maximum magnitude at z=±a; the displayed positive-z maximum is μ0I/(2πa). J=αr gives I=2παR³/3 and B=μαr²/3 inside, μαR³/(3r) outside, continuous at R (μ=μ0 here). The thin-strip logarithm requires uniform current across width; Bz=μ0I ln(1+w/s)/(2πw) for s>0, recovering μ0I/(2πs) for w≪s. Both perpendicular semi-infinite wires give +z field in x,y>0; the displayed integrated sum is correct.
- Chapter conceptual prompts: same-direction spring turns attract axially in the close-coil model; integration paths exploit symmetry; coplanar concentric loops have no net translation by symmetry but radial mutual forces can draw their wires toward one another, so simple “attract/repel” language must distinguish deformation from translation. Three parallel currents can all have pairwise attraction if their signs agree, but cannot be pairwise opposite for all three pairs. Biot–Savart versus Coulomb compares vector cross-product current sources with scalar charge sources under their static regimes.
- Problem 9.13.1: 10 A at 10 mm gives 2.00×10⁻⁴ T. Tracing the entire intricate orange closed boundary, including its narrow indentations, encloses wires 1, 3, 6 and 8; the arrows set positive normal out of page. Thus signed Ienc=2I0(1+3−6−8)=−20I0. The multiple visible adjacent arcs are parts of this one contour. A single circulation cannot isolate arbitrary local B for this nonsymmetric arrangement; individual wire fields superpose.
- Problem 9.13.2: hollow conductor B=0 for r<a, μ0I(r²−a²)/[2πr(b²−a²)] for a<r<b, μ0I/(2πr) outside. B is continuous at a,b but its slope changes. Opposite central line current subtracts μ0I/(2πr); exterior cancellation and central singularity follow. Zero field has no direction.
- Problems 9.13.3–7: cavity result and geometric ambiguity are above. Ideal-solenoid value for N=200,l=.25 m,I=.30 A is 3.01593×10⁻⁴ T. Rotating disk dI=σωr dr gives B=μ0σωR/2, axial with the signed rotation/charge. Four square-corner wires give B=2μ0I/(πa) downward in the drawn plane: horizontal components cancel. The rectangular-loop force, if the neighboring wire is infinite, is μ0I1I2l[1/a−1/(a+w)]/(2π) away from the wire for the pictured opposing nearest currents; opposite vertical-side forces cancel.
- Problem 9.13.8: Coulomb/centripetal balance at r=.53 Å gives v≈2.18×10⁶ m/s, T≈1.52×10⁻¹⁶ s, |I|≈1.05 mA, μ≈9.27×10⁻²⁴ A·m² along −z, and mvr≈ħ. The current runs opposite the electron’s orbit. These are estimates in the explicitly semiclassical orbit model.
- Problem 9.13.9: n≈8.52×10²⁸ m⁻³, N≈2.68×10²², M≈1.53×10⁶ A/m, μ≈.482 A·m² and equivalent single-loop current ≈1.53×10³ A support all supplied rounded answers. This equivalent current reproduces dipole moment; it does not imply an identical near field to a finite-thickness disk.
- Problem 9.13.10: on the +x coil axis Bx=μ0IR²/[2(R²+x²)^(3/2)]; qv j-hat gives initial force −qvBx k-hat and instantaneous radius mv/(q|Bx|). Magnetic force does no work. Subsequent field variation precludes asserting a uniform-field orbit.
- Problem 9.13.11: Mπ(.0055 m)²(.048 m)=.02418 A·m² and the far-axis dipole field at 1 m is 4.84×10⁻⁹ T=4.84×10⁻⁵ G, matching supplied answers as a far-field approximation. Problem 9.13.12 gives μ0(3000/.60)5=.031416 T, 314.16 G, 628 times the stated .5 G; “about 600” is appropriate. Rotating shell K=σv0 and interior B=μ0σv0 follow, exterior zero for the explicitly infinite cylinder; direction assumes positive signed σ.
- PRS pp1–2: answer 5 (“none”) is consistent with opposite loop moments and the stronger bottom source in the shown geometry. At the pictured axial null, the two local fields are equal and opposite; “the bottom field is stronger” must refer to source strength, not unequal fields at the null. Inferring the current ordering needs the comparable/equal loop radii implicit in the apparatus picture; no quantitative current ratio is supplied. PRS pp3–4: A attracts, B repels, answer 2, consistent with the connected versus separated field pattern and fixed coaxial moments. PRS pp5–6: answer 3, zero circulation. Full-boundary inspection encloses three dots and three crosses, including the upper dot in the thin lobe and right upper cross; the upper-left dot, middle-left dot, central cross and lower-right cross lie outside. No current lies on the boundary.
