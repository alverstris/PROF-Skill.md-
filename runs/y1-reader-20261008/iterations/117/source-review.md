## Confirmed source defects and explicit conflicts

1. Chapter physical p13, Eq. 10.4.4, writes I=|ε|/R=(NBAω/R)sinωt and immediately calls I an alternating signed current. These equalities cannot all hold when sinωt<0. For signed current use I=ε/R; for current magnitude use |I|=|ε|/R. Eq. 10.4.5’s intermediate I|ε| is likewise inconsistent if I is signed. Its final nonnegative power (NBAω)²sin²ωt/R is correct. The mechanical-power derivation also needs τ to mean the externally supplied torque magnitude or appropriately signed external torque; the magnetic torque on a loaded generator opposes the imposed rotation.
2. Chapter physical p16, Appendix 10.7, says the force on a charge “must be the same” in different inertial frames and derives E_nc=v×B as an exact equality. Three-force is not invariant under Lorentz transformations. In the special instantaneous comparison used here, with E=0 in the magnet frame and a charge moving at the loop velocity v, the loop-rest electric field is E′=γ(v×B), and its transverse electric force is γ times the magnet-frame magnetic force. The printed relation is the leading v/c≪1 approximation, not an exact consequence of relativity. The charge can be treated as instantaneously at rest before drift starts; a circulating carrier is not generally at rest in the loop frame. This is a proven overstatement of model scope, not an objection to low-speed induction equivalence.
3. Chapter physical p26, Problem 10.11.2: text specifies vertically downward B while the original Fig. 10.11.2 has upward blue B arrows. The induced current direction is consequently unresolved by the source: text-down gives a→b on the pictured bar; figure-up gives b→a. Magnitudes and terminal-speed formulas depend on B² and do not resolve the conflict. The same prompt assigns resistance R to the bar, while the figure draws a distinct fixed resistor R across the upper rails. It is not stated whether that icon represents the intended total resistance or another physical resistor. Calculations below retain R_total instead of silently choosing R or 2R.

Minor source references: Appendix p16 points to Section 10.4 for induced electric circulation, whose derivation is in 10.3. Eq. 10.9.27 reuses I for both the source-wire current on the right and induced-loop current on the left; the numerical dependence is correct but the two currents must be distinguished. Presentation p33 and PRS pp3,5 use a vector derivative with a scalar inequality and describe flux as “up”; the intended axial component and chosen oriented area are recoverable from the arrows. Flux itself is a signed scalar.

## Scope and conditions that are not algebraic defects

- Faraday’s −N dΦ/dt uses equal flux per turn and coherent winding orientation. For differing turn fluxes it is −d(ΣΦj)/dt. BA cosθ presumes a uniform field over a planar loop; the source also gives the correct surface integral. A loop and its spanning surface need consistent right-hand orientation. Wb=T·m² and Wb/s=V are consistent throughout.
- Presentation p18’s ε=∫E·ds omits the closed-path mark and does not by itself cover a moving circuit. Fixed-loop electric circulation is ∮E·dl=−dΦ/dt. Motional emf requires the circuit-element velocity term ∮(u×B)·dl; the general moving material-loop expression includes both E and u×B. The chapter explicitly distinguishes stationary and moving cases, but the slide definition needs that scope. An open moving bar’s end-to-end voltage is not automatically a potential difference for every nonconservative field path.
- All direct I=ε/R calculations use an ohmic lumped circuit with negligible self-inductance/transients or an appropriate steady response. Lenz’s law opposes the flux change; it does not universally fix total current independently of an initial stored current. Similarly, “force must oppose motion” applies to the passive induction situations shown, not every possible externally driven magnetic system. The falling-magnet and jumping-ring static evidence does not identify complete drive frequency, inductance or thermal parameters.
- The source’s time-varying infinite-wire fields and prescribed confined changing B are quasistatic idealizations. The presentation expressly acknowledges the pending displacement-current correction on pp8,34; p35 is a provisional equation list. No universal time-dependent Ampere law was inferred from its magnetostatic form.
- Presentation p39 groups speakers and microphones under “magnetostatics” but supplies only an external speaker-diagram URL. A moving-coil speaker’s force can use a prescribed permanent B, whereas a moving-coil microphone’s generated voltage needs induction. The heading alone does not provide a complete microphone model; no linked diagram was counted as read.
- Problem 10.11.3’s asymptotic capacitor charge assumes initially uncharged C, a continuing specified flux ramp and the usual RC model. Without initial charge and ramp duration, “maximum” is not uniquely fixed. The spherical-shell conceptual prompt likewise needs the direction of B relative to the selected equatorial normal; time variation alone need not change equatorial flux.
- The finite-width field-region problems use a sharp ideal edge, ignore self-inductance and fringing, and may have jumps in emf at the ideal switching instants. Problem 10.11.11 explicitly assumes terminal motion while the loop straddles the field edge. Once the loop is wholly outside, induction braking vanishes; it cannot have that same permanent terminal speed thereafter.
- No source figure was treated as an animation trajectory or measured dataset. The square-loop exit figure depicts an intermediate overlap; the text separately defines t=0 at the leading vertex/boundary. These are compatible.

## Independent technical witnesses

- Presentation pp6–7: for the bent ideal solenoid N=2πRn, so the central toroidal circle has B(R)=μ0NI/(2πR)=μ0nI; the depicted inner crosses give clockwise B. The minor radius a sets the torus cross section rather than that central result. Current directions in the preceding overview figure are reversed consistently with its opposite circulation arrow.
- Chapter pp3–6: the product derivative is −A cosθ Bdot−B cosθ Adot+BA sinθ θdot. All four flux-sign/current cases in Fig. 10.1.7 and the sign table check: ε and dΦ/dt have opposite signs; an upward positive normal makes positive circulation counterclockwise as viewed above. The approaching north-down magnet generates counterclockwise current and repulsion.
- Open bar, chapter pp7–8: rightward u and inward B give u×B upward, making the top endpoint positive, E downward and Va−Vb=Blv. For the closed rails, their positive inward-flux convention gives ε=−Blv and counterclockwise actual current. The equivalent source polarity on p9 agrees. Magnetic drag is −B²l²v/R, required external force is its opposite, and external power equals I²R=B²l²v²/R. Released-bar v=v0e^(−t/τ), τ=mR/(B²l²), is correct; total subsequent displacement is v0τ and initial kinetic energy is ultimately dissipated in this model. Presentation p30’s five group answers follow these same signs and expressions.
- Chapter pp10–12: for increasing inward B confined to radius R, Eφ=(r/2)Bdot inside and (R²/2r)Bdot outside, counterclockwise. The two branches match at R, the center field is zero, and the displayed graph’s linear then reciprocal shape is correct. This induced E exists outside the B-filled region and does not require a physical wire there.
- Generator, chapter pp12–13 and presentation p38: signed Φ=BA cosωt and ε=NBAω sinωt are correct. With signed I=ε/R, electrical power εI=(NBAω)²sin²ωt/R≥0. Opposing electromagnetic torque and equal external mechanical power preserve energy; the source’s absolute-value error does not change the final sin² result. The generator has slip rings; the DC motor figure has a commutated drive. The guitar diagram’s magnets, vibrating ferromagnetic string, pickup coil and signal are distinct roles; the shown waveform has no calibrated axes for numerical inference.
- Eddy-current p14 diagrams: clockwise current while the slab exits inward B produces leftward magnetic drag. Laminations/cuts interrupt large circulating paths. At a prescribed emf and negligible reactance, ε²/R decreases with higher R; no claim of fixed emf under arbitrary strong magnetic feedback is needed.
- Solved 10.9.1: inward Φ=μ0Il ln[(s+w)/s]/(2π), ε=−μ0bl ln[(s+w)/s]/(2π) for I=a+bt, giving counterclockwise induced current. This uses s>0,w>0 and the long-wire/quasistatic model. Solved 10.9.2: square-to-rhombus area changes from l² to l²sinθ; average current magnitude Bl²(1−sinθ)/(RΔt), clockwise for the depicted contraction. It is an interval average, not necessarily the instantaneous current.
- Solved 10.9.3: left-moving rod gives bottom-positive motional voltage. Both resistors carry upward current, with left-loop clockwise and right-loop counterclockwise circulation. The rod carries their sum downward. P=B²l²v²(1/R1+1/R2), magnetic force points right with magnitude P/v, and required external force points left. Solved 10.9.4: bar parallel to the infinite source wire has constant B along its own length, so |ε|=μ0Ilv/(2πr). For the pictured leftward wire current and upward bar velocity, the left endpoint is positive.
- Solved 10.9.5: inward Φ(t)=πa²(B0+bt), ε=−πa²b, induced current counterclockwise with magnitude πa²b/R, P=(πa²b)²/R. Solved 10.9.6: inward flux decreases as r grows; ε=μ0I_source l v w/[2πr(r+w)] in clockwise positive orientation, so induced current is clockwise. The source’s alternative motional line integral gives the same result and includes cancellation between near and far bars.
- Conceptual prompts p25: south-first downward magnet gives an upward flux pulse; with positive counterclockwise current, the resistive current is negative on entry, zero at a symmetric flux maximum and positive on exit. A freely falling magnet’s time graph need not be symmetric because speed changes. Decreasing counterclockwise current in upper coil A induces counterclockwise B current and attraction; increasing A current induces clockwise B current and repulsion. A rigid loop translating fully within spatially uniform steady B retains constant flux and has zero net emf. Equatorial shell circulation depends on the changing normal flux, not merely on |B| changing somewhere.
- Additional 10.11.1: with the pictured battery, the bar initially carries downward current in outward B and moves left. Taking speed positive left, m vdot=(ε−Blv)Bl/R gives the stated v=[ε/(Bl)](1−e^(−t/τ)), τ=mR/(B²l²); motional emf opposes the battery as speed rises.
- Additional 10.11.2, preserving the conflicting field/resistance inputs: |ε|=Blv cosθ, |I|=Blv cosθ/R_total, uphill drag along the rails is B²l²v cos²θ/R_total, and terminal speed is mgR_total sinθ/(B²l²cos²θ), for 0<θ<π/2. Terminal current magnitude is mg sinθ/(Bl cosθ), and I²R_total=mg v_terminal sinθ. The two source B directions reverse current but not these magnitudes. At θ=0 there is no gravitational drive; at θ=π/2 the specified vertical B provides no induction drag along the rails and there is no finite magnetic terminal speed.
- Additional 10.11.3: for initially uncharged C and sustained decreasing inward B, emf magnitude is παr² and the clockwise driving direction charges plate b positive. q(t)=Cπαr²(1−e^(−t/RC)), asymptote Cπαr². The induced electric field, not magnetic work on stationary charges, drives charge separation.
- Additional 10.11.4: upward B and motion toward the pulley produce b→a bar current of magnitude Blv/R. The combined inertia is m+M, so (m+M)vdot=Mg−B²l²v/R and v=MgR/(B²l²)[1−exp(−B²l²t/((m+M)R))]. Using only m would omit the hanging mass’s acceleration.
- Additional 10.11.5: for the pictured counterclockwise rotation and inward B, positive charges feel an inward radial magnetic force of magnitude qBωr. Integration gives VO−Vtip=Bωl²/2. The source’s unspecified ΔV is valid as a magnitude or with that endpoint order; the opposite endpoint order has opposite sign.
- Additional 10.11.6: l=.10 m is vertical; w=.08 m is along the motion; the field region is .40 m wide. At v=.020 m/s, entry lasts 0–4 s, full overlap 4–20 s and exit 20–24 s. Choosing inward normal, flux rises with slope .004 Wb/s to .016 Wb, remains flat, then falls at −.004 Wb/s. ε is −4 mV, 0, +4 mV respectively; current magnitudes are 2 mA, 0, 2 mA, counterclockwise entering and clockwise leaving. Sharp-edge derivative values at the switching instants are idealized one-sided values.
- Additional 10.11.7: clockwise as viewed from the left makes the positive normal rightward. North-first right-moving magnet gives a positive flux pulse; in the symmetric, constant-speed resistive model current is negative before t=0 and positive after. Magnetic drag is leftward on both entry and exit. The pulling agent supplies the ohmic heating, subject to transient field-energy storage if self-inductance is retained.
- Additional 10.11.8: ε0=2πfNabB. For the stipulated peak 120 V, f=60 Hz and B=.40 T, Nab=120/(2π·60·.40)=0.795775 m²-turn; many geometries satisfy this. No RMS 120 V was substituted. At an early positive rotation angle the current opposes the decrease of initially inward flux. A signed normal initially aligned with B gives the printed positive sine emf.
- Additional 10.11.9: the positive circuit arrow is counterclockwise, consistent with outward Bz. Graph slopes are +2.5 T/s on 0–2 s, 0 on 2–4 s, −1.25 T/s on 4–8 s, and 0 thereafter. Thus ε=−1.96350 V, 0, +.981748 V, 0; I=−98.1748 mA, 0, +49.0874 mA, 0. The first interval’s resistor current goes right-to-left, then left-to-right during decay. P=.192766 W,0,.0481914 W,0. The printed partial answers give correct magnitudes; their omission of signs is intentional in a prompt that explicitly asks for signs.
- Additional 10.11.10: let δ=vt and a=l/√2. The field-edge chord is c=2δ for 0≤δ≤a and c=2(2a−δ) for a≤δ≤2a. Power supplied is B²v²c²/R, zero outside that exit interval, continuous at the center, with peak 2B²l²v²/R at t=l/(√2v). This checks both triangular overlap branches of the unlabeled field boundary.
- Additional 10.11.11: as outward flux decreases, current is counterclockwise. |ε|=Bwv, I=Bwv/R, net magnetic force is upward B²w²v/R; horizontal forces cancel. The assumed partial-overlap terminal speed is mgR/(B²w²), and mgv_terminal=I²R.
- PRS pp1–2: answer 2, downward force, follows either μz dBz/dz<0 for the widening upward field or the paired Idl×B arrows. PRS pp3–4: answer 1, clockwise for increasing upward flux. PRS pp5–6: answer 2, counterclockwise for decreasing upward flux. PRS pp7–8: answer 1, upward force on the downward-moving loop below the upward-moment magnet; upward flux decreases, so the pictured counterclockwise current has upward μ, attracts toward the magnet, and gives upward force.
