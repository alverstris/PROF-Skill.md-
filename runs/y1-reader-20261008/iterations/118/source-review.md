## Governing laws, conventions and limits

1. Supplied Faraday law: epsilon=-d(lambda)/dt, lambda=sum of oriented turn fluxes; -N dPhi/dt presumes equal linked flux per turn. Phi=integral B.dA, Wb=T m^2; positive contour fixed by the right-hand rule about the chosen normal. Uniform planar Phi=BA cos(theta) uses angle to normal. Product differentiation in 10.1.5 is correct. Reversing normal reverses signed flux/emf/current reference, never physical current.
2. Lenz opposes the *change* of linked flux. The p6 four-row table is correct for an upward normal and CCW viewed from above positive. A current requires a closed conducting path and a circuit model; emf does not require current. Motion or nonzero B alone is insufficient. The introductory relative-motion language is limited to the depicted approach/withdrawal. A rigid loop translating wholly inside a static uniform field has zero net emf.
3. Motional contribution is integral (u cross B).dl; total emf for a moving circuit is integral (E+u cross B).dl. Stationary-loop Faraday electric circulation uses a fixed surface. Do not identify -d(flux through an arbitrarily moving surface)/dt with integral E.dl alone. E potential differences in 10.3.1 are path independent only for electrostatic/conservative E; changing-B induced E has nonzero circulation.
4. Resistive results I=epsilon/R assume negligible circuit self-inductance/transient storage, fixed R, and quasi-static field models. Magnetic force q v cross B does no work on the charge's instantaneous motion; external mechanical work supplies Joule heating in moving-conductor examples. Slab braking and slitting results are geometry-dependent eddy-current approximations, not a single universal lumped resistance.
5. p8 takes inward normal for positive Blx, hence epsilon=-Blv corresponds to CCW physical current. In the isolated bar a is positive relative to b. p9 force is left, Fext=B^2 l^2 v/R right, P=Fext v=epsilon^2/R=I^2R. Unforced v=v0 exp(-t/tau), tau=mR/(B^2 l^2), x(infinity)-x0=v0 tau. This ideal asymptotic stopping does not promise unlimited available rails or absence of friction.
6. Circular increasing inward B: induced E is CCW; E=r dot(B)/2 inside and R^2 dot(B)/(2r) outside. It is continuous at R, zero at r=0 and decays as 1/r outside although local B there is zero. These are magnitudes for positive dot(B); reverse direction if dot(B)<0.
7. Generator: signed epsilon=NBA omega sin(omega t), signed I=epsilon/R; power=(NBA omega)^2 sin^2(omega t)/R. Mechanical torque exerted by field opposes rotation; external torque maintains it. The usual ideal generator omits internal resistance, inductive loading and losses. Average power is half the peak power for the stated resistive load.

## Consequential source defects and interpretation controls

- **Error, Chapter 10 p13, 10.4.4-5:** prints I=|epsilon|/R followed by a signed sine and says current changes sign. Use I=epsilon/R for signed I or |I|=|epsilon|/R. Likewise P=I|epsilon| is inconsistent with signed I; use P=I epsilon=epsilon^2/R. Later torque/dipole formulas mix signed components and magnitudes; state the opposing field torque and positive external input power explicitly.
- **Overstatement, Chapter 10 p16:** same physical event in two frames does not make three-force invariant. E_nc=v cross B is a low-speed relation for the specified moving-magnet/conductor comparison, with each velocity/field attached to its frame. Exact Lorentz transformations have gamma factors. Also moving conduction charges are not literally at rest once current flows. The cross-reference to section 10.4 for induced E should be 10.3. Targeted Caltech primary-text verification is recorded below.
- **Contradiction, Chapter 10 p26, problem 10.11.2:** prose says vertically downward B; original figure shows upward arrows. Current direction must be conditional; speed/power depend on B^2 and agree for either polarity. Do not silently choose the picture or prose.
- **Incomplete sign answer, Chapter 10 p31, 10.11.9:** partial answers list positive magnitudes. For the indicated CCW positive contour the initial rising positive B gives *negative* emf/current. The correct signed values are below.
- **Figure/time mismatch, Chapter 10 p31, 10.11.10:** picture shows a partly emerged diamond, while t=0 is explicitly the rightmost vertex just reaching the field boundary with the whole loop still inside. Use text for time origin; picture is a later configuration.
- **Ambiguous N, Chapter 11 11.1.1/5:** receiving-turn factors absent from final surface-integral expression unless surface means full linked multi-turn surface; detailed in SR-11.1.
- **Error, presentation p20:** F proportional to epsilon^2/R confuses force and dissipated power/speed dependence. In the stated fixed-geometry, low-speed resistive model epsilon proportional omega, P=epsilon^2/R proportional omega^2, torque=P/omega proportional omega; linear force scales with speed, not speed squared. p19 entering-material loop is CCW; p20 exiting loop is CW, both yield torque opposing clockwise disk rotation.
- **Measurement interpretation, presentation p12:** screenshot top axis is current in A and time in s; eight negative/positive pulse pairs appear with a current-zero offset. Lower green curve is expressly y=-integral(x) in the software, with rising baseline and label 'FLUX'. Without R/N and integration constant, this is a flux proxy in A s, not an established Wb calibration. From RI=-N dPhi/dt, Phi-Phi0=-(R/N) integral I dt under the resistive approximation. Small constant current bias produces linear integration drift. The black arrow identifies imperfect current zero. No measured R, N, raw dataset, complete wiring, or uncertainty is supplied here; do not invent them or claim to have performed this lab.
- **Missing promised PRS content:** presentation p10 announces five Experiment 9 prediction questions, but the official ten-page PRS contains only the four p13 topics plus the p22 uniform-field question. The five experiment predictions are not present in this assigned PRS. No guessed replacement was fetched. This is an honest packet-content gap, distinct from readability.
- **Media/apparatus limitation:** p5/15 show only the original cooled copper ring/magnet still and textual claim; p16/17 external pendulum/disk video links, p18 demo heading, p21 superconductor still/link, p25 remote-speaker heading, p27 nail and Jacob's ladder headings provide no complete procedure or observed time sequence. These media were not played and lab manuals are outside assignment. Braking-to-Joule-heat is a model explanation supported by the equations, not a claimed observation of those missing demonstrations. A superconductor's levitation is not proof of ordinary resistive braking; the still does not identify material, cooling history, Meissner/pinning contribution or stability mechanism.
- **Constitutive limits, presentation pp31-36:** dielectric E=E0/kappa assumes the usual fixed-free-charge comparison; fixed voltage would hold E=V/d. Magnetic B=kappa_m B0 and B=B0+mu0 M require the simple geometry/field convention (B0=mu0 H and appropriate internal H), not arbitrary externally applied field for every finite sample. M=sum(mu_i)/V has units A/m. A constant scalar kappa_m cannot describe the very next hysteresis plot: ferromagnets are nonlinear, history dependent and can saturate. Domain ordering need not produce net magnetization in an unmagnetized specimen. Paramagnetic induced alignment disappears in equilibrium when field is removed; ferromagnetic remanence depends on history. 'Some items can be picked up' is qualitative and does not classify every attracted item as paramagnetic or every ferromagnet as a permanent magnet. Targeted MIT primary text confirmed these limitations.
- **Transformer signs/model, p26:** written positive N dPhi/dt is a magnitude/terminal-polarity convention, whereas oriented loop emf has Faraday's minus. The voltage ratio magnitude Ns/Np assumes shared time-varying core flux and ideal coupling; winding sense controls relative polarity. Constant DC after transients gives no induced secondary voltage. Laminations suppress eddy-current loss; turns ratio does not create power.
