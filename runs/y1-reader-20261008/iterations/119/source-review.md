## Laws, models, signs, units and independently checked examples

Use oriented flux linkage lambda=sum(Phi_turn), positive contour/right-hand normal together. For a fixed linear inductor lambda=LI, self-emf epsilon_L=-L dI/dt. Under the passive terminal convention (current enters the positive terminal), v_L=+L dI/dt. These differ by reference/sign, not by physics. For variable geometry or nonlinear magnetic materials use d(lambda)/dt; L=constant and the simple energy formula need qualification. L and M have H=Wb/A=V s/A, L/R has seconds, LI dI/dt has W, LI² has J, and B²/(2mu0) has J/m³. Self-induced emf opposes change of current, not necessarily its present direction. The separately drawn “induced current” arrows on pp6/17 express an emf tendency; there is one actual circuit current, not two independently flowing currents.

The rise/decay graph shapes and 1/e marks are correct under constant R,L and a single first-order lumped state. At steady current self-emf disappears but L, B and stored energy persist. ## Consequential defects and interpretation controls

- **Textbookp16 direction contradiction:** it defines a counterclockwise contour/current and obtains -epsilon+IR=-L dotI, but describes the traversal as clockwise against current immediately before11.4.2. The displayed equation corresponds to CCW along current. Its reference to section11.1 for self-flux should be11.2. Choose one contour explicitly; do not propagate the contradictory prose.
- **Textbookp20 Fig11.4.6(b) arrow error:** the battery in (a) is positive at the bottom, so initial current runs right through the lower R and up through L. This CCW current must continue immediately after switching. The equivalent emf in (b), also positive at bottom, is consistent with sustaining that CCW current, but its top I arrow points right (CW), opposite both (a)'s initial current and (b)'s sustaining emf. Reverse that arrow for a consistent physical picture. Scalar exponential decay formula remains correct when I is the positive magnitude along initial current.
- **Textbookp19 cross-reference error:** the power balance comes from multiplying the governing LR equation11.4.5 by I, not multiplying the exponential solution11.4.7 as the prose says. Eq11.4.9 really has absolute-value bars around -L dotI in the original; these are lost in extraction. It is a correct magnitude expression, not a signed-emf error.
- **Textbookpp10–11 sign condition:** increasing energy requires I dotI>0 (increasing magnitude of I), not simply dotI>0 without assuming I positive. Decreasing positive current returns energy. Source language implicitly assumes the illustrated positive current.
- **Presentationp4:** “You need AC currents” is stronger than the equation. Any changing current, including a one-way ramp or switching transient, induces emf for fixed nonzero M; periodic alternating current is not required. Constant DC after settling produces none.
- **Presentationp10/p16:** “does nothing” for constant current means no induced emf or power transfer; field/energy remain. “Stop” change at switch closure should mean prevent a jump, not make dotI zero.
- **Presentationp17 dimensional graph error:** voltage curves retain the current-axis label I and marking .36 epsilon/R. Voltage axes must be V and a voltage scale epsilon, with vL(tau)=.3679epsilon. For the labeled circuit, Va-Vb=vL and Vc-Va=vR; “a to b” must identify meter polarity/path. Current scale epsilon/R belongs only to the preceding current plot.
- **Presentationp18 overgeneralization:** “All Quantities Either” simple exponential growth or decay applies only to the specified linear first-order state/voltage/current responses. General response is Xfinal+(Xinitial-Xfinal)exp(-t/tau). Powers/energies involve products or squares; multi-storage circuits need not have one real exponential. Read tau from the derivative coefficient only after normalizing the X coefficient, with the effective R of the actual topology.
- **Textbookp15/presentation21:** changing-flux E is nonconservative and its line integral between endpoints depends on path. “Potential is now meaningless” is too broad: scalar potential still appears in E=-grad(phi)-partial A/partial t; it alone cannot describe the total field. Lumped terminal voltages remain useful under explicit flux/lead assumptions.
- **Presentation21 two-resistor puzzle:** 1A gives Ohmic drops10V and100V along the clockwise loop, total electric circulation110V. Faraday requires -dotPhi=110V for the clockwise-positive normal. This is not a contradictory single-valued electrostatic potential around the loop; any numerical voltmeter claim would need a lead path.
- **Presentation23–24 and textbook11.4:** localizing self-flux permits either explicit Faraday flux in the loop equation or an effective inductor terminal voltage; avoid counting both. The p24 zero integral along ideal zero-resistance winding means zero tangential total Ohmic field along that conductor path. It does not imply the terminal voltage, measured via an external path, is zero, or that E vanishes everywhere around the coil. With standard lumped assumptions that terminal voltage is L dotI. Targeted primary verification below resolves this subtle distinction.

Animation11.1 (pp12–13): supplied model spins positively charged rings CCW, builds upward B and induces CW electric field opposing acceleration; on spin-down the induced field is CCW and returns energy. The stills and arrows agree. This is a quasistatic energy-exchange model; omitted radiation requires the relevant slow-variation approximation, not merely small speed. Charge-carrier kinetic energy is distinct from magnetic energy. J·E describes local field/matter exchange; local field energy also changes through Poynting transport, so broad language about creating/destroying field energy is shorthand.

Animation11.2 (pp13–15): supplied zero-R, axially constrained ring above a sufficiently strong magnet can turn around and return under the stated initial conditions. Conserved flux is total self plus external linkage, not external flux alone. A consistent fixed-L model is L dotI+RI=-dotPhi_ext(z), force I partial_z Phi_ext, m ddotz=-mg+I Phi_ext'. Consequently d[.5mv²+mgz+.5LI²]/dt=-I²R. The p14 “energy is not conserved” claim for finite resistance concerns mechanical plus recoverable magnetic energy; including heat restores conservation. Lost gravitational energy is measured between positions, so “all initial gravitational energy” needs its zero specified. “About a above the ring” is an apparent location typo; the illustration/narrative concerns height above the magnet. The zero-R bounce is not proof of a static stable levitation state or unconstrained three-dimensional stability. For finite L, current need not reverse instantly when the external flux derivative changes sign while passing the magnet; instantaneous resistive reasoning needs a short L/R limit. No motion timings, measured dissipation or live animation outcomes are asserted.
