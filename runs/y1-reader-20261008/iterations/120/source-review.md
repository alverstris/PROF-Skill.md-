## LC model, independent derivation and full figure audit

Supplied model: fixed, linear lumped L,C; closed source-free loop after switching; negligible resistance, radiation, leakage and stray storage. Let Q be signed charge on the initially positive plate and choose positive I flowing out of it through the inductor (clockwise in textbookFig11.5.1). Then I=-dotQ and capacitor rise Q/C balances passive inductor drop L dotI. Therefore L ddotQ+Q/C=0. U=Q²/(2C)+LI²/2 is conserved under these assumptions. Differentiating U yields the same ODE where I is nonzero; division by I cannot itself establish the equation at I=0. Kirchhoff's equation, or smooth continuation from it, covers the turning points and initially zero current.

For Q(0)=Qi and I(0)=0: Q=Qi cos(omega0 t), I=omega0 Qi sin(omega0 t), omega0=1/sqrt(LC), Imax=Qi/sqrt(LC), T=2pi sqrt(LC), f=1/T. Direct substitution verifies the ODE and both initial conditions. General amplitude/phase form is A cos(omega0t+phi); initial charge is A cos(phi), not generically A. Positive-Q maximum precedes positive-I maximum by T/4 in this sign convention; equivalently I lags Q by pi/2. Reversing I reference reverses the apparent phase sign, not the energy exchange.

UE=U0 cos²(omega0t), UB=U0 sin²(omega0t), U0=Qi²/(2C)=LImax²/2. Energies repeat every T/2 and remain nonnegative; charge/current repeat every T and change sign. The textbookp23/presentation20 superposed Q/I graph has separate amplitude labels Q0 and I0: the differently dimensioned quantities do not share a literal numerical vertical unit. Their drawn amplitude-height ratio is not a numerical physical ratio. The energy plots correctly start with all electric energy and alternate with magnetic energy at twice the charge/current frequency.

The source's I=I0/Imax labels at both quarter-cycle diagrams denote current magnitude; the reverse arrows at3T/4 encode its negative signed value. The switches are drawn open in initial-state schematics and closed in subsequent stages; the equations concern the stipulated closed state. “Trade-off between charge and current” means conversion of their associated energies, not conservation of a sum Q+I, which has incompatible units.

Mechanical equations independently reproduce m ddotx+kx=0, x=A cos(omega0t+phi), v=-omega0 A sin(...), omega0=sqrt(k/m); kinetic plus spring energy=.5kA². The force law assumes a Hookean spring about equilibrium, with negligible friction. Textbook Table11.6.1 correctly maps Q↔x, L↔m, 1/C↔k, R↔b, magnetic↔kinetic energy and electric↔spring energy. With the chosen discharging-current convention dotQ=-I, v corresponds specifically to -I; the table's ±I explicitly allows a different reference.

### Consequential notation/sign issues

- Textbook11.5.11 briefly writes sin²(omega t) instead of sin²(omega0 t), and substitutes (-omega0 Q0)² even though it defined positive I0=omega0Q0. The square leaves energy unchanged. Do not transfer that stray minus into the already-defined signed current.
- Presentation14 writes epsilon_L=-L dotI=-L ddotq, implying I=+dotq. Presentation15 then writes capacitor epsilon=+q/C, and presentation19 explicitly uses the opposite I=-dotQ for a discharging positive plate. These cannot be combined using one silently unchanged charge/current reference. With the p19/textbook discharge convention epsilon_L=+L ddotQ and Q/C-L dotI=0. Alternatively choose a charge increasing with I and use the corresponding negative restoring capacitor drive. The analogy preserves m↔L and k↔1/C; its isolated emf signs do not replace a consistent loop convention.
- Presentation10/19 label phi a “time offset”; phase is an angle (radians), with time translation -phi/omega0 for cos(omega0t+phi). Presentation12 switches to cos(omega0t-phi); its phi=pi/2 gives a positive sine, a quarter-period delay of the cosine. The sign is a convention change; the horizontal time-shift arrow is phi/omega0, not a duration pi/2 seconds. T=1/f=2pi/omega is correct.
- Inductors “hate change” and capacitors being “opposite” (p3) are informal. Finite voltage gives continuous inductor current but allows nonzero slope; finite current gives continuous capacitor voltage. At steady current the inductor's self-emf vanishes but B, L and stored energy persist (p4's “does nothing”). Initial LR slope is epsilon/L, not zero despite “stop it.”

## RLC derivation, damping and initial-condition correction

Let R be total effective series resistance, including coil/source/lead resistances when applicable. Stored electromagnetic energy U=Q²/(2C)+LI²/2 obeys dotU=-RI². Total energy including dissipated heat remains conserved; “total energy decreases” here means the stored circuit energy. The discharge loop Q/C-L dotI-RI=0 with I=-dotQ yields L ddotQ+R dotQ+Q/C=0, agreeing with11.6.3. A resistor causes damping; finite R does not necessarily produce oscillation.

Define gamma=R/(2L), omega0²=1/(LC). Characteristic roots are -gamma±sqrt(gamma²-omega0²). The supplied underdamped solution is valid only for 0≤R<2sqrt(L/C)=2omega0L, with omegad=sqrt(omega0²-gamma²). Critical damping is equality; stronger damping gives two nonoscillatory real exponential modes. These limits are independent deductions from the assigned ODE, not a claim to have read unassigned Appendix11.8. Both gamma and angular frequency have s^-1, LC has s², Rcritical has ohms. Damping reduces the free-oscillation frequency relative to omega0 for fixed L,C; it is not a frequency continuously decreasing with time for a fixed linear R,L,C circuit, nor a statement about forced-resonance frequency.

**Initial-condition ambiguity, textbook11.6.4 and presentation22:** the chapter first calls Q0 the initial capacitor charge, then reuses Q0 as the prefactor of exp(-gamma t)cos(omegad t+phi), before saying Q0 and phi are set by initial conditions. Those are different parameters for a capacitor initially charged with zero inductor current. With initial charge Qi and I(0)=0, independent differentiation gives

Q(t)=Qi exp(-gamma t)[cos(omegad t)+(gamma/omegad)sin(omegad t)],

I(t)=Qi(omega0²/omegad)exp(-gamma t)sin(omegad t).

Equivalently the cosine prefactor is A=Qi omega0/omegad and phase phi=-arctan(gamma/omegad), not A=Qi,phi=0 when R>0. Both initial conditions and the ODE are satisfied; R→0 recovers the LC result. The simplistic curve Qi exp(-gamma t)cos(omegad t) instead has I(0)=gamma Qi. Presentation22's schematic exponential-envelope plot should not be used as an exact zero-initial-current trace without resolving this phase/prefactor issue. Its condition R<2omega0L is correct. The small stray outlined artifact by R does not obscure the frequency formula.

For the zero-initial-current solution, positive-current maxima solve tan(omegad t)=omegad/gamma, rather than exactly at T_d/4. At a current extremum Q/C=RI, so Q generally is not zero: the ideal-LC quarter-cycle inference must not be carried into damped RLC. Charge/current envelopes decay with rate gamma; exact instantaneous stored energy is not simply U0 exp(-2gamma t), because dotU=-RI² varies around the cycle and vanishes whenever I=0. A cycle-averaged or weak-damping energy envelope can decay approximately at2gamma. The PRS energy plot appropriately has a monotone total with varying slope.

## Presentation graphs and Experiment10 interpretation

Presentationp5 is an illustrative unipolar square drive, approximately0 to3V, with high/low halves roughly8ms and displayed times0–.03s. “AC output” here means time-varying output with DC offset, not a zero-mean sine. For an ideal closed LR path on each constant-source interval, I=Ifinal+(Istart-Ifinal)exp(-Delta t/tau), tau=L/R. vL=L dotI is positive during the rise and negative during decay, while I remains continuous and of the shown sign. vL+RI=Vsource. Exact return to zero or steady plateau in a finite half-cycle is an approximation if the interval is several time constants. The DC-battery schematic alone is not a full generator/measurement wiring diagram; low source voltage must retain a discharge path, and simply opening S does not produce the plotted closed-loop decay.

Presentationp6 uses a first-order real-coil model: ideal L in series with winding resistance r. Its signed change in the current direction is -L dotI-rI; passive terminal drop is vcoil=L dotI+rI. Calling the whole expression an emf must not turn Ohmic rI into Faraday self-emf; these are distinct contributions. This model omits core loss, saturation, stray capacitance, frequency-dependent resistance and instrument loading, so it is a model for the intended regime, not an exhaustive description of every real coil.

Presentationp7's screenshot has output (V), “Voltage, ChB” (V), “Current, ChA” (A), and time(s) to roughly.12. Orange output alternates near0/3V; red current rises toward roughly.18A and decays toward zero; blue coil-terminal voltage jumps and relaxes, retaining a positive high-state offset of order1V before switching. The marked offset is consistent with rI after the inductive contribution has become small. These are observations of the supplied screenshot, not of a new experiment. The plot alone does not give a precision measurement of r,L or wiring.

The prescribed “time constant from I or V” and “resistance from V just before switch” are valid with conditions: fit the exponential relative to its nonzero asymptote, not blindly relative to zero. For a near-steady high state, r≈vcoil/I; at a finite nonzero slope the exact relation is r=(vcoil-L dotI)/I. Use L=tau Rtotal with all series resistances, not just the explicitly added resistor. The screenshot suggests r of order(1V)/(.18A), roughly5–6ohms, **only a coarse visual estimate under the near-steady assumption**, not a reported fitted lab value. Finite sampling, offsets, generator resistance, lead orientation and incomplete settling can change inferred values; none are specified well enough to fabricate a corrected numerical result. No exact L or component value is asserted.

Presentationp16 explicitly shows charging through battery and resistor with switch at a, then connecting capacitor and inductor at b, disconnecting the battery/R branch. With long charging, Qi=Cepsilon and I_L(0)=0. Ideal subsequent values follow above: Imax=epsilon sqrt(C/L), U0=.5Cepsilon², T=2pi sqrt(LC). The drawn charging resistor is not in the ideal b-position oscillation loop. Real coil resistance supplies damping if included; it is not legitimate to leave that charging R in the b-position equation merely because it remains drawn elsewhere.
