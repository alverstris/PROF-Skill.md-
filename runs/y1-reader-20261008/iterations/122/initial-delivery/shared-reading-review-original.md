## Laws, deductions, and conventions

**Pages 3–6: displacement current and Maxwell equations.** Maxwell's addition is an introduced physical law, motivated by continuity and the capacitor surface inconsistency; it is not proved by that one capacitor. With a common oriented boundary, ∮B·dl = μ0(I + ε0 dΦE/dt). For the ideal uniform vacuum parallel-plate gap, ΦE=EA=Q/ε0 and Id=dQ/dt=I when the spanning surface captures all the plate flux. A smaller disk captures only its corresponding area fraction. This is changing electric flux, not charge conduction across an insulating gap. The two spanning surfaces in Figures 13.1.1–2 were inspected. A flux over an open spanning surface must not be read as a closed Gaussian surface; the closed-surface glyph used for the gap flux on p4 is misleading notation.

Gauss's magnetic law is a law of the classical model. Its zero divergence does not prove every field line closes in a finite region; lines can also extend to infinity. Figure 13.2.1 supplies the bar-magnet example, not a universal topological proof. The hypothetical magnetic-charge normalization Qm/μ0 is a convention, not a measured SI magnetic charge definition to introduce without qualification. The differential Maxwell table on p6 uses ε0, μ0 and E,B but describes ρ,J as free sources. In vacuum these coincide with total sources; in material media the displayed microscopic equations require total charge/current, or the macroscopic free-source version requires D,H and constitutive relations. Source-free wave derivation requires local ρ=0,J=0, not merely zero net enclosed charge/current.

**Pages 7–13: vacuum traveling waves.** The xy Faraday loop and xz Ampere loop in Figures 13.4.1–3 give ∂xEy=−∂tBz and −∂xBz=μ0ε0∂tEy for the chosen right-handed axes. Differentiation and commuting smooth mixed derivatives give ∂x²Ey=μ0ε0∂t²Ey, and likewise Bz. Thus v=1/√(μ0ε0). The source's rounded ε0=8.85×10⁻¹² and μ0=4π×10⁻⁷ give 2.99863×10⁸ m/s, not the p10 displayed 2.997×10⁸; use 3.00×10⁸ at that precision. Identification with measured light speed supports the electromagnetic model of light. The spectrum illustration is qualitative, with overlapping named bands; it does not specify present telecommunications allocations.

The chain-rule proof for f(x±vt) is valid for twice differentiable f, or with a weak/distributional interpretation for discontinuities. For E=E0 cos(kx−ωt)ŷ and B=B0 cos(kx−ωt)ẑ, substitution gives ω=ck and B0=E0/c. E,B are transverse, in phase, and E×B points +x. These are properties of a single traveling vacuum plane wave; they are not universal properties of all electromagnetic fields or arbitrary superpositions. E=cB in magnitude remains meaningful at nodes; division E/B does not. A specified E alone determines the accompanying pure traveling-wave B once extra static/background solutions have been excluded.

**Pages 13–15: standing waves.** Superposing opposite waves with the reflected electric sign reversed gives E=2E0 sin(kx)sin(ωt)ŷ, B=2B0 cos(kx)cos(ωt)ẑ. Electric nodes x=nλ/2 and magnetic nodes x=(n/2+1/4)λ alternate by λ/4; successive nodes of one field are λ/2 apart. n is any integer on an unrestricted axis, nonnegative only for a selected half-domain. The fields are in time quadrature where both spatial amplitudes are nonzero. A perfectly conducting cavity wall imposes tangential E=0; two such walls admit lengths mλ/2. Figures 13.4.5–6 show superposition and node placement, not a propagating wave with a universal instantaneous E/B=c.

**Pages 15–19 and 34–35: energy.** Vacuum energy densities are uE=ε0E²/2 and uB=B²/(2μ0), in J/m³. For a traveling plane wave they are equal pointwise, u=ε0E², S=E×B/μ0, |S|=cu, and I=〈|S|〉=ε0cE0²/2=E0B0/(2μ0)=cB0²/(2μ0), in W/m². The cylinder of Figure 13.6.1 correctly yields energy crossing area A in dt as uAc dt. E0,B0 are peak sinusoidal amplitudes, not rms values. A uniform ideal plane wave has no geometric spreading; isotropic spherical radiation has I=P/(4πr²). A local plane-wave approximation to the latter is not global plane geometry.

Example 13.6.1 independently gives E0=1.00844×10³ V/m, B0=3.36146×10⁻⁶ T and P=3.81704×10²⁶ W from the given I=1350 W/m² and R=1.50×10¹¹ m. Printed rounding is sound. These are a hypothetical sinusoidal equivalent and the source's solar input, not newly measured sunlight fields or a current solar-irradiance update. The comparison to one tenth of Earth's magnetic field needs a specified terrestrial location/field value and is not universal.

For Example 13.6.2's alternative phase convention, E=2E0 cos(kx)cos(ωt)ŷ, B=2B0 sin(kx)sin(ωt)ẑ, direct multiplication gives Sx=(E0B0/μ0)sin(2kx)sin(2ωt). The time average of signed Sx is zero; the time average of |Sx| generally is not. Energy oscillates locally and need not vanish. The general field-energy theorem is dUfield/dt=−∮S·n̂ dA−∫J·E dV. The abbreviated balance on pp19/34 omits the work term and therefore requires a source-free volume (or a carefully defined total internal energy). A resistor is a counterexample to using that abbreviation indiscriminately.

**Pages 20–22: solenoid example.** In the ideal long air-core quasistatic model, Bz=μ0nI, Eφ=−μ0nr I′/2, and Sr=−μ0n²rII′/2. Integrating gives Pin=μ0n²πr²l II′=LII′=d(LI²/2)/dt. The inward/outward sign depends on II′, not on I′ alone. Statements that an increasing current increases energy presume positive current in the stated orientation, or mean increasing magnitude. The figure's induced E is a circulating field; it does not establish a separate physical conducting loop or measured induced current. The model ignores fringe fields and higher-order displacement/electric-energy effects. Surface-power integrals need dA and a surface integral, not a closed line-integral glyph.

**Pages 22 and 35: momentum.** For normal incidence, an incident energy packet U brings momentum U/c; absorption transfers U/c and perfect reversal transfers 2U/c. The corresponding mean pressures are I/c and 2I/c, in Pa = J/m³. The p35 introductory phrase calling ΔU energy “absorbed” is wrong for perfect reflection: there it means incident energy. No absorbed heat is required for reflection pressure. Oblique incidence and partial transmission require momentum components and coefficients; the displayed factors are not universal.

## Radiation and conducting-sheet appendix

**Pages 23–26, Figures 13.8.1–7.** Static charges and stationary continuous current distributions do not radiate in this model. An individual accelerated charge has radiation fields, but claims about a collection require summing fields and allowing cancellations. The antenna snapshots distinguish near electric storage, near magnetic storage, and outgoing radiation. A zero instantaneous charge separation does not mean every retarded electric field everywhere is zero. Far-zone radiative E,B fall as 1/r and carry outward power; the full near fields need not be in phase or have E/B=c.

The source specifies two λ/4 arms (total half-wave antenna) and then presents I∝sin²θ/r². The latter is the electrically short-dipole pattern; treating it as exact for the specified half-wave antenna is a model mismatch. The qualitative axial nulls and transverse maximum survive. Primary MIT notes give half-wave Eθ proportional to cos[(π/2)cosθ]/sinθ, whereas short-dipole Eθ is proportional to sinθ. The current distribution matters. “Quarter-wave antenna” on p26 is ambiguous beside a depicted two-arm structure whose wavelength is twice tip-to-tip length; do not silently invent a monopole ground plane. The animation stills were inspected but no linked animation was executed. Field-line motion is a visualization convention, not a material velocity measurement. The p25 static-offset dipole has a static near-field component in addition to its oscillating radiative part.

**Pages 27–33: infinite moving sheet.** All kink/front diagrams were read. An infinite charged sheet at x=0 has E0=±σ/(2ε0)x̂. Starting v=−vŷ generates Ey=σv/(2ε0c), Bz=±μ0σv/2 behind fronts |x|=ct. K=−σvŷ and the wave's Ey/B magnitude ratio is c. Force per area from the perturbation field opposes motion; the external power per area is σ²v²/(2ε0c). The two outward fluxes each have magnitude σ²v²/(4ε0c), giving exactly that power. For the initially charged sheet the total E0×B also has lateral components; the normal radiative flux calculation uses the perturbation field. A coincident stationary opposite sheet cancels E0, leaving the wave fields. The abrupt start is an idealization, not experimental evidence.

For arbitrary prescribed uniform surface current, Ewave=−(μ0c/2)K(t−|x|/c), and Bwave=±x̂×Ewave/c on the respective sides. The p33 magnetic jump is explicitly a magnitude μ0|K| and is correct; oriented boundary form is x̂×(B+−B−)=μ0K. The sinusoidal expressions on pp31–32 have the correct retarded arguments, and B reverses sign across the sheet. The plane source is infinite; its velocity-dependent field does not contradict the acceleration dependence of an individual charge's radiation. This review does not claim to have read the cited Feynman chapter. Caption 13.9.4/text reference 13.8.11 on p32 is an internal numbering defect.

**Pages 35–39: reflection.** Let Z0=μ0c and sheet resistance Rs=ρ/D. The source's sheet model gives Et=Ei/(1+Z0/(2Rs)), K=Ei/(Rs+Z0/2), and scattered E=−Z0K/2 on each side. Recomputing: electric reflection amplitude r=−Z0/(2Rs+Z0), transmission amplitude t=2Rs/(2Rs+Z0); continuity gives t=1+r. Energy fractions are R=Z0²/(2Rs+Z0)², T=4Rs²/(2Rs+Z0)² and A=4RsZ0/(2Rs+Z0)², summing to 1. These are deductions within the ideal uniform resistive-sheet model, not a slab measurement.

For Rs→0, Et→0, K→2Ei/Z0, reflected E reverses sign, and the x<0 fields become E=2E0 sinωt sin kx ŷ and B=2B0 cosωt cos kx ẑ; fields vanish on the x>0 side. The p36 wording initially suggests J follows incident E alone, but pp37–38 correctly use the total sheet field in Ohm's law. One must keep that self-consistency. Thin relative to the vacuum wavelength alone does not establish a uniform current through a real conductor: attenuation over skin depth also matters. Primary MIT notes confirm skin depth δ≈√[2/(ωμσ)] in a good conductor and δ can be much smaller than vacuum wavelength. Thus the perfect-conductor limit is valid as a surface boundary model; the finite uniform-thickness derivation is not a complete real-metal penetration model.

The p37 pressure formula needs a peak/instantaneous distinction. With the p36 constants E0,B0 denoting amplitudes, instantaneous pressure is 2E0B0 cos²ωt/(μ0c); its mean is E0B0/(μ0c)=2I/c. Writing 2E0B0/(μ0c)=2S/c without the cos² factor is only a peak-pressure equality or requires E0,B0 to be reinterpreted as instantaneous fields. Surface-force use of the incident B equals the average of limiting total B across the ideal sheet, avoiding self-force double counting. Equation 13.10.9's reference to itself for the substitution should reference 13.10.8; minor internal citation error.

## Solved and conceptual problems

**Pages 39–42:** Traveling-wave procedure and Examples 13.12.1–2 recomputed. For E=E0 sin(kz−ωt)x̂ (or cosine), propagation is +z, λ=2π/k, f=ω/(2π), B=(E0/c)sin(kz−ωt)ŷ, S=E0²sin²(kz−ωt)/(μ0c)ẑ. I=E0²/(2μ0c); reflected normal mean pressure is E0²/(μ0c²)=B0²/μ0. First and second derivatives in the cosine wave-equation exercise give −k²E and −ω²E; ω²=c²k² is necessary. Direction vectors reverse when the sinusoid changes sign; saying B is always “in +y” means its chosen positive polarization, not its instantaneous sign.

**Pages 43–45, capacitor:** With the depicted positive Q and I=Q′ in +z, E=Q/(ε0πR²)ẑ, B=μ0rQ′/(2πR²)φ̂ for r<R, and S=−QrQ′/(2π²ε0R⁴)r̂. Stored electric energy is Q²h/(2ε0πR²), derivative QhQ′/(ε0πR²). Thus **−∮S·n̂ dA**, with outward n̂, equals this positive derivative. Equation 13.12.19 drops the minus sign while presenting an oriented flux, and uses a contour-style integral. It can be read as inward power only if the orientation is explicitly changed. The finite uniform-field/no-fringing/quasistatic assumptions are needed; Q′>0 alone implies inward flow only with Q>0, which the example supplies.

**Pages 45–46, conductor:** For uniform steady I in +z, E=I/(σπa²)ẑ, B=μ0I/(2πr)φ̂ outside the wire, and S(a)=−I²/(2σπ²a³)r̂. Inward surface power =I²l/(σπa²)=I²R, with R=l/(σπa²). Equation 13.12.24 again needs a minus for outward surface flux. Its dU/dt cannot mean electromagnetic energy storage in a steady field; it represents power delivered to material/heat, with subsequent thermal outflow depending on circumstances. No actual heat accumulation experiment is supplied.

**Conceptual questions 1–7 (pp46–47):** (1) Yes, e.g. finite-conductivity material with time-varying E has both J and ε0∂tE. (2) Accelerated individual charges/time-varying source distributions can generate radiation; account for system cancellations. (3) Touch can change coupling/effective antenna length and impedance; improvement is contingent, not guaranteed. No body or antenna circuit is specified. (4) Conducting structure can reflect/attenuate and alter multipath; the amount depends on openings, frequency and geometry, so a bare steel frame is not automatically a closed Faraday cage. (5) Sound is mechanical and needs a medium; vacuum EM waves do not. Fluid sound is typically longitudinal while a vacuum plane EM wave is transverse; solids can have transverse sound. (6) A nonzero single traveling vacuum plane wave cannot have parallel E,B; arbitrary superpositions are not constrained by that single-wave relation. (7) At fixed waveform/medium I∝E0², so factors 1/4 and 4.

## Additional problems — independent results and missing premises

All subparts on pp47–51 were considered, including ones without supplied solutions. Results below are deductions, not source answers or measurements.

1. **Solar sail:** For normal perfect reflection, 2[P☉/(4πr²)]A/c=GM☉m/r² gives A=2πcGM☉m/P☉, independent of r within inverse-square assumptions. The problem supplies m=1650 kg but no solar mass/gravitational parameter. Using the chapter's I at R and an explicitly additional approximate GM☉=1.327×10²⁰ m³/s² gives A≈1.081×10⁶ m² (square side about 1.040 km). That numerical constant is not in the assignment; the symbolic result is complete without it. Sail orientation/reflectivity, fixed total mass, and neglect of other forces matter.

2. **Bulb reflection:** Missing isotropic emission assumption is needed for I=100/(4π·1²)=7.95775 W/m². With that and the stipulated local sinusoidal plane wave, E0≈77.42 V/m, B0≈2.581×10⁻⁷ T. PEC peak surface current density K0=2B0/μ0≈0.41075 A/m. This is current per width, not a total amperage; the face width is unspecified. The ideal reflector description does not describe real skin.

3. **Coax:** Choose +z from battery to load. Inner charge −Q and outer +Q imply E=−Q/[2πε0lr]r̂=−ε/[r ln(b/a)]r̂ in a<r<b. A passive positive load then carries conventional current along the outer conductor toward the load and inner conductor back, so B=−μ0I/(2πr)φ̂ when I>0 is the circuit-current magnitude. Consequently S=εI/[2πr² ln(b/a)]ẑ and ∫S·ẑ2πrdr=εI=I²R. E,B are zero in the ideal empty inner cavity and exterior of equal/opposite infinitely long conductors. Finite ends, load/battery vicinity, conductor thickness and fringing prevent determining literal “everywhere” from these data. If the unspecified word “down” is taken to mean +z on the inner conductor, its positive-current direction conflicts with the stated negative inner charge/passive-load polarity; define directions explicitly rather than silently reversing one source statement. The actual diagram's battery and load were inspected.

4. **Superposition:** Write a=E10cos(kx−ωt), b=E20cos(kx−ωt+φ). Co-propagating S=(a+b)²/(μ0c)x̂ and I=[E10²+E20²+2E10E20cosφ]/(2μ0c). Phase coherence is presumed. For reversed second propagation use b=E20cos(kx+ωt+φ), B=(a−b)ẑ/c, S=(a²−b²)x̂/(μ0c), mean signed flux [E10²−E20²]/(2μ0c). Equal amplitudes yield zero mean signed transport, not zero field energy or zero mean |S|.

5. **Diagonal polarization:** Emax=√2 E0, B=(E0/c)cos(kz−ωt)(−x̂+ŷ), S=2E0²cos²(kz−ωt)/(μ0c)ẑ, I=E0²/(μ0c), reflected mean pressure=2ε0E0². Treating E0 as total vector amplitude would lose a factor two.

6. **Pressure:** uE=uB=ε0E0²sin²/2; 〈utotal〉=ε0E0²/2=B0²/(2μ0). Normal absorption gives P=I/c=〈utotal〉. At 1350 W/m², u=4.50×10⁻⁶ J/m³ and P=4.50×10⁻⁶ Pa. Orientation must be normal for this stated scalar pressure equality.

7. **Energy:** With Erms=0.030 V/m, I=ε0c Erms²; through normal 1.00 cm² in 3600 s the transported energy is 8.6022×10⁻⁷ J. No extra factor 1/2 is allowed for rms. At the given solar I, energy in 1.00 m³ is 4.50×10⁻⁶ J if the local beam has the stated intensity. Using an upper-atmosphere input “near the surface” omits atmospheric attenuation/weather and is an ideal estimate, not an actual surface measurement.

8. **Alternative axes:** For E=Ez(x,t)ẑ,B=By(x,t)ŷ, curl equations give ∂xEz=∂tBy and ∂xBy=μ0ε0∂tEz as printed. A +x traveling wave then requires By=−Ez/c; a −x wave requires By=+Ez/c. The signs are a genuine coordinate consequence, not an error.

9. **Rectangular pulse:** Need a≠0,b≠0 and b²=c²a²; speed/direction is v=−b/a. Source does not state the signs. With s=sign(v), E=−s cB0 f(ax+bt)ẑ, S=s cB0²f²/μ0 x̂. For a,b>0 the pulse goes −x and E points +z when on. Discontinuities require distributional Maxwell equations. If exactly half the energy reflects and half transmits back into vacuum with no absorption and normal incidence, momentum balance gives pressure magnitude (1+R−T)Sinc/c=Sinc/c=B0²/μ0 during the flat pulse under the ideal simultaneous-flux assumption. Pulse entry/exit transients through a finite dielectric sheet cannot be timed from an energy fraction alone; thickness, delay, and constitutive data are absent. Do not invent those details.

10. **Ferrite sinusoid:** Phase gives +x, λ=3m, f=10⁶Hz, v=3×10⁶m/s. The stipulated nondispersive scalar μr=1000, εr=10 gives c/√10000≈3×10⁶m/s, consistent at source precision. B=−10⁻⁴ cos[(2π/3)x−2π10⁶t]ŷ T. The frequency remains fixed at a stationary interface, giving vacuum λ≈300m. Material ferrite properties here are model givens, not verified broadband constants; real anisotropy/loss/dispersion are not specified. Source skips label (f), proceeding (e) to (g), without an extra hidden task.
