## Consequential findings

1. **Answer-key numbering error, PRS pp1-2.** The picture has E upward at the sheet, B out of the page immediately right and into it left. The sheet current is downward; for the positive sheet used in the presentation, its velocity is downward. The correct choice is **2**. The printed answer says “(1) Down”; its prose is right and number wrong. Without the positive-charge convention the field establishes current direction, not charge velocity.
2. **Surface-flux signs, reading pp45-46, Eqs13.12.19 and 13.12.24.** The computed S is inward, but the displayed positive incoming powers are equated to positive closed surface integrals. With outward dA, incoming power is **-∮S·dA**. Eq13.12.19 should evaluate at r=R. In the steady resistor, electromagnetic stored energy does not grow at I²R: incoming power becomes heat. The printed dU/dt can only mean accumulated delivered/dissipated energy there, not U_EM.
3. **Energy balance scope, reading pp19-22 and summary pp34-35.** In general dU_EM/dt = -∮S·dA - ∫J·E dV. The displayed boundary-only balance needs no volume work, or an explicit total-energy definition including matter. The ideal solenoid/capacitor calculations are quasistatic, with fringing and higher-order electric/magnetic storage neglected. Signs of storage change are governed by I dI/dt (or Q dQ/dt), not the derivative alone if signed current/charge can be negative.
4. **Traveling-wave scope.** E=cB, E perpendicular B, equal electric/magnetic energy densities, and I=c<u> apply to a single vacuum traveling plane wave, not every Maxwell field or arbitrary superposition. The chapter itself correctly constructs standing waves violating the pointwise E=cB relation. At simultaneous field zeros prefer E=cB over an undefined E/B ratio. “90 degrees out of phase” for standing fields refers to temporal quadrature where both local amplitudes are nonzero. Zero mean S does not mean zero stored energy or zero instantaneous energy exchange.
5. **Antenna approximation and naming, reading pp23-26; presentation pp17,19-20.** Two λ/4 arms make a λ/2 tip-to-tip dipole. Calling that whole object a quarter-wave antenna is ambiguous; the figure does not establish a single λ/4 monopole above a conducting ground plane. The sin²θ/r² intensity law is the electrically short dipole pattern, not the exact thin half-wave pattern. The latter is proportional to [cos((π/2)cosθ)/sinθ]²/r². Both have axial nulls and broadside maxima, so the PRS qualitative answers survive. Static field-line pictures/“moving strings” are visual models, not material trajectories or a derivation of an exact finite antenna field. The assertion that the field vanishes when end charge vanishes must be local/quasistatic, not all space at that instant.
6. **Conducting-sheet model, reading pp35-39.** Recomputing with sheet resistance R_s=ρ/D and Z0=μ0c gives E_sheet/E_inc=2R_s/(2R_s+Z0), K=2E_inc/(2R_s+Z0), r=-Z0/(2R_s+Z0), t=2R_s/(2R_s+Z0). This supports the ideal reflection limit and standing fields. D much smaller than vacuum wavelength alone does not justify uniform current in a good conductor; thickness must also be small relative to the relevant penetration/skin depth for that finite-resistivity approximation. Taking ρ→0 at fixed D is not a uniformly valid volume-current approximation, although the perfect-conductor boundary result is correct. Eqs13.10.5-6 use amplitudes without their cos² factor: instantaneous pressure is 2S_inc(t)/c; cycle mean is 2I/c. Do not confuse amplitude product with average pressure.
7. **Radiation pressure wording, pp22,35.** For reflection ΔU in Δp=2ΔU/c is incident energy, not energy absorbed by an ideal mirror (zero). These pressures assume normal incidence. At oblique incidence use the normal momentum flux and projected area.
8. **Model versus hardware, presentation pp12,23-25,30-35.** “100% efficiency” belongs to the ideal infinite prescribed-current sheet and its electromagnetic work balance; it is not a measured spark-gap efficiency or a claim excluding mechanical acceleration work and dissipation. Spark pictures show oscillation states, not enough evidence to reconstruct all wiring. The displayed 33 pF and 4.5 MΩ produce τ=148.5 μs; the 31 mm arms imply λ≈124 mm, f≈2.419 GHz. The fast damped burst time and RC repetition scale differ; no experimental signal, uncertainty, or actual result is supplied.
9. **Other conventions/limitations.** Reading p6 calls ρ,J free/conduction sources while writing vacuum microscopic E,B equations; in matter these require total sources or D,H constitutive treatment. Hypothetical monopole normalization on p5 is not established by experiment; the used law is ∇·B=0. The summary should not claim all divergence-free lines necessarily form finite closed loops. Presentation p18's radio band and tower data are historical supplied context, not independently verified current facts. λ at the supplied 700 kHz is 428.6 m and λ/4=107.1 m; the quoted 747 ft tower is plainly not λ/4. Figure caption p32 says13.9.4 while text calls13.8.11; p15 uses13.4.6 for the standing-wave figure. These are navigation defects. Eq13.12.11 is correct in its image; extraction misleadingly displaced t, so no source derivative error is alleged.

## Independently checked derivations and examples

The integral/differential Maxwell table (p6) and oriented loops (pp7-9, Figs13.4.1-3) yield ∂xE_y=-∂tB_z and ∂xB_z=-μ0ε0∂tE_y, hence the wave equation with c=1/√(μ0ε0). Wave phase kx-ωt travels +x, kx+ωt travels -x; B=n×E/c. The chain-rule calculation pp10-11 is sound for sufficiently differentiable functions, with weak solutions needed for ideal steps. SI dimensions: k in m^-1, ω in s^-1, ε0E² and B²/μ0 in J/m³, E×B/μ0 in W/m², I/c in Pa, K in A/m.

The counterpropagating fields pp13-15 give E_y=2E0 sin(kx)sin(ωt), B_z=2B0 cos(kx)cos(ωt); E nodes nλ/2, B nodes λ/4+nλ/2. Example13.2 uses the equally valid shifted standing wave E∝cos(kx)cos(ωt), B∝sin(kx)sin(ωt), giving S_x=(E0B0/μ0)sin(2kx)sin(2ωt), mean zero. The positive energy densities do not cancel.

Example13.1 pp17-18: with the supplied 1350 W/m², c=3.00×10^8 and ε0=8.85×10^-12, E0=1008 V/m, B0=3.36 μT, P=3.817×10^26 W at R=1.50×10^11 m. These are equivalent monochromatic amplitudes for the explicitly assumed sinusoidal model; actual sunlight is broadband. Spherical inverse-square dilution presumes isotropic luminosity; local plane-wave approximation is separate.

Solenoid pp20-22: B=μ0nI zhat, E_φ=-(μ0nr/2)dI/dt, S_r=-(μ0n²rI/2)dI/dt. Side influx = μ0πn²r²l I dI/dt = d(LI²/2)/dt, L=μ0n²πr²l. The figure's inward arrows agree for I,dI/dt positive.

Infinite sheet pp27-33 and presentation pp6-13: with K=-σv yhat, E1=+σv/(2ε0c)yhat on both sides, B1=±μ0σv/2 zhat for x≷0. S_x=±σ²v²/(4ε0c). Total two-sided electromagnetic power/area σ²v²/(2ε0c) equals external work against the sheet's retarding field. Retarded time is t-|x|/c. A stationary opposite charge sheet cancels the static E0; it does not cancel the oscillatory current. The acceleration-versus-velocity distinction for a single charge versus an infinite coherent sheet is explicitly noted on p33 and is consistent with the supplied infinite-geometry model.

Solved13.12.1-2 pp41-42: B=(E0/c)cos(kz-ωt)yhat; substituting either sinusoid yields (-k²+ω²/c²) times the field, zero for ω=kc. Solved13.12.3 pp43-45: E=Q/(επR²)zhat, B=μ0rQdot/(2πR²)φhat, S=-QrQdot/(2ε0π²R⁴)rhat; influx QhQdot/(επR²)=d[Q²h/(2επR²)]/dt. Solved13.12.4 pp45-46: E=I/(σπa²)zhat, B(a)=μ0I/(2πa)φhat, S(a)=-I²/(2π²σa³)rhat, incoming power I²l/(σπa²)=I²R. These confirm magnitudes while exposing the surface-sign defects above.

Presentation p23 group problem: under the indicated long cylindrical/parallel-plate lumped approximation (height h, cylinder radius d, plate overlap2d, gap d), C=2ε0h and L≈μ0πd²/h. Therefore ω0≈c/[d√(2π)], f0≈c/[2πd√(2π)]. This is a model deduction, not an exact resonance of the pictured finite, partially open conductor; fringing, end effects and radiation are unprovided. Presentation p32 antenna orientation maximizes E projection; differing element lengths select different wavelength ranges and director/reflector behavior, without identifying individual wiring from the photograph.
