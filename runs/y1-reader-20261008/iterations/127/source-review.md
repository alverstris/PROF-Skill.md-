D127-C01 | quasistatic_work_and_self_energy_conditions | lecture2.pdf PDF 2,3/slides 3,4,5,6 | Work and assembly energy
External-work signs and the pair-energy sum are correct. The equality F_external = −F_Coulomb assumes quasistatic motion or suitable control.
Keep other charges fixed, neglect radiation, avoid coincident charges and set interaction energy zero at infinite pair separation. Generally W_external = ΔU + ΔK.
Since d(1/r) = −r̂·ds/r², W_external = Qq(1/r2 − 1/r1). For N distinct charges, U = Σ_(i<j) qi qj/r_ij = (1/2)Σ_iΣ_(j≠i) qi qj/r_ij. The first charge has zero interaction energy, not zero self-energy. Point-charge self-energy diverges; opposite-charge interaction energy can be negative.

D127-C02 | test_field_and_field_line_conditions | lecture2.pdf PDF 4,9,10,11/slides 7,8,17,19,20,22 | Electric field and field-line visualization
E = F/q is valid for an unperturbed prescribed source field. Line-count and crossing language needs scope.
A test charge must not appreciably perturb the source. Smooth field lines have unique direction where E ≠ 0; at a zero the direction is undefined. Density/counts are conventions with a fixed represented flux per line.
Local uniqueness for dr/ds = E(r) prevents distinct smooth trajectories from crossing. Net flux counts outward minus inward crossings, including repeated crossings. Opposite enclosed charges can produce zero net flux; the word net on slide 20 matters. Field units are dyne/esu.

D127-S01 | independent_ring_and_disk_derivation | lecture2.pdf PDF 5,6,7/slides 9,10,11,12,13 | Ring and disk fields and limits
The ring formula and finite disk formula are correct; the latter properly retains |z|.
Assume an ideal thin ring or disk with fixed uniform charge density. The disk formula is stated for z ≠ 0 because its ideal surface has a field jump.
For the ring, E = Qz/(R²+z²)^(3/2) ẑ. For a disk, dq = 2πσs ds gives E_z = 2πσ[sgn(z) − z/√(R²+z²)]. Thus E_z(0±) = ±2πσ. For |z| ≫ R, E_z = (Qz/|z|³)[1 − 3R²/(4z²) + O(R⁴/z⁴)], Q = πσR². At fixed σ and R → ∞, E = 2πσ sgn(z) ẑ.

D127-C03 | sheet_conductor_and_side_conventions | lecture2.pdf PDF 6,14/slides 12,28 | Infinite conductive plane and boxed field
The boxed 2πσ ẑ describes the positive-z side. Conductive does not specify whether σ means total charge per projected area of a two-sided sheet or charge on one conductor face.
For an isolated symmetric thin sheet, E = 2πσ sgn(z) ẑ. A conductor face bordering a zero-field interior has E_out,n = 4πσ_face. A symmetric slab of total projected density σ has σ/2 on each face.
A pillbox yields E_n^+ − E_n^− = 4πσ. Reflection symmetry gives opposite fields and the factor 2π; E_inside = 0 gives 4π for a conductor face. The field exactly on an ideal charged surface has distinct one-sided limits.

D127-N02 | minor_variable_notation_defects | lecture2.pdf PDF 7/slides 13 | Far-field disk slide

Expanding (1 + R²/z²)^(-1/2) after the leading cancellation gives Q/z² times [1 − 3R²/(4z²) + …]. This verifies the leading result and its error scale.

D127-C04 | surface_orientation_terminology | lecture2.pdf PDF 7,8,9/slides 14,15,16,17,18 | Area vectors and flux
The phrase 3D surface refers to a closed boundary enclosing volume; that surface itself is two-dimensional.
Choose a consistent normal on an orientable open surface. A closed boundary of a bounded region has a natural outward normal. Edges between smooth faces have zero area measure.
For uniform velocity and a planar surface, flux = vA cos θ and has units volume/time. A closed cylinder in uniform axial E has cap fluxes +EA and −EA and zero side flux, hence zero total flux.

D127-G01 | gauss_law_heuristic_proof_and_domain | lecture2.pdf PDF 10,11,12/slides 19,20,21,22,23 | Extension of Gauss law to arbitrary surfaces
The centered-sphere calculation is rigorous; the general field-line extension is a heuristic, and the source explicitly defers a fuller proof. No matter what E or S is too broad literally.
Use a physical electric field associated with the stated charge and an oriented, closed, piecewise smooth boundary. Keep point charges off the boundary or specify a distributional/limiting treatment.
For one charge at a, E = q(r−a)/|r−a|³ has divergence zero away from a. Apply the divergence theorem after excising a small sphere around an enclosed source. Outer flux equals its small-sphere flux 4πq; an exterior source gives zero. Superpose charges or integrate a regular density. Equivalently use the signed solid-angle integral, including nonconvex recrossings.

D127-C05 | gauss_law_does_not_alone_determine_field | lecture2.pdf PDF 12,13,14/slides 23,25,26,27,28 | Field extraction from Gauss law
Gauss law always constrains flux. Suitable symmetry allows the field magnitude to be taken outside the surface integral; the law remains useful without symmetry.
The source and boundary conditions must share the symmetry. Gauss law alone does not determine the whole field.
Adding a constant vector C to E changes no closed-surface flux. For an isolated spherical source, rotational symmetry and the boundary condition yield E = E_r(r) r̂. For an isolated uniform sheet, translations, rotations and reflection imply equal opposite normal fields. These facts justify the displayed factorizations.

D127-E01 | confirmed_malformed_direct_integral | lecture2.pdf PDF 12/slides 24 | Approach 1 Coulomb-volume integral
The displayed setup contains dq/r′ with a first-power denominator, uses an upper radial bound r without distinguishing body radius R, duplicates angular differentials and omits a consistent source-to-field vector projection.
Replace the setup with the vector Coulomb integral over the full charged body. The subsequent Gauss-law sphere answer is correct independently.
E(r) = ∫_(|r′|≤R) ρ(r′)(r−r′)/|r−r′|³ d³r′. On the z axis for uniform ρ, E_z = 2πρ ∫_0^R s² ds ∫_0^π [(r−s cos θ) sin θ/(r²+s²−2rs cos θ)^(3/2)] dθ. This gives the correct body bound, separation kernel and one angular measure. Inside observations require handling the integrable source-point singularity. An enlarged original-PDF crop confirms the first-power printed denominator.

D127-S02 | sphere_shell_and_boundary_verification | lecture2.pdf PDF 12,13,14/slides 24,25,26,27 | Solid sphere and hollow shell
The displayed fields are correct for uniform volume density ρ or uniform spherical surface density σ. The origin and ideal charged surface require interpretation.
For a solid sphere, Q = 4πρR³/3 and E_inside = (4πρ/3)r, including zero at the origin and continuity at R. For a shell, E_inside = 0 and E_outside = Q r̂/r², with different one-sided surface limits.
Spherical Gauss law gives r²E_r = Q_enclosed. For a general radial density, E_r = (4π/r²)∫_0^r ρ(s)s² ds. For a shell Q = 4πR²σ, so the field jump is Q/R² = 4πσ. Zero flux implies zero interior field here because of spherical symmetry, not in general.
