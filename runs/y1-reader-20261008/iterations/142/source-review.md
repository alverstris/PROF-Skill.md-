D142-S01 | mechanical_analogy_and_damping_regimes | lecture17.pdf, PDF 2,3, slides 5,6,7 | Undriven equation and characteristic roots
The analogy L↔mass, R↔linear viscous damping coefficient, 1/C↔spring constant and characteristic equation are correct. The three damping regimes are now explicitly distinguished, unlike the preceding packet’s unqualified oscillatory discussion.
For L,C>0,R≥0, set a=R/(2L), Ω=1/√LC. Characteristic roots β=−a±√(a²−Ω²). For a<Ω use e^(−at)[Acosωd t+Bsinωd t], ωd=√(Ω²−a²); a=Ω needs (A+Bt)e^(−at); a>Ω gives two real decays.
Insert e^(βt) into Qddot+(R/L)Qdot+Q/(LC)=0. At R=0 roots are ±iΩ; for positive R both real parts are negative. A single exponential trial finds eigenvalues but not both integration constants. Critical damping is the boundary giving fastest asymptotic return among nonoscillatory modes at fixed L,C; fastest for every finite-time metric/initial velocity is not implied by the source shorthand.

D142-C01 | weak_damping_energy_and_initial_data | lecture17.pdf, PDF 3,4, slides 8,9 | Approximate Q,I and energy exponential
The slide headings explicitly restrict these formulas to weak damping. Their exact-looking equalities for energy and initial phase should be understood at that order, not as exact finite-R identities.
Require a/Ω≪1. For exact Q(0)=Q0,I(0)=0 and I=−Qdot, Q=Q0e^(−at)[cosωd t+(a/ωd)sinωd t], I=Q0Ω²/ωd e^(−at)sinωd t. The source drops the small sine correction and replaces ωd with Ω.
Exact energy U=Q²/(2C)+LI²/2 obeys Udot=−RI². With the source leading-order approximations, U≈Q0²/(2C)e^(−Rt/L). At t=0 exact I=0 implies Udot=0, whereas differentiating that pure energy exponential gives −(R/L)U0; this simple check proves it is not an exact instantaneous identity for the stated initial state. Cycle averaging/high-Q leading order makes the exponential decay useful.

D142-C02 | quality_factor_normalization_and_frequency_scope | lecture17.pdf, PDF 4, slides 10 | Two definitions of quality factor
The boxed dimensionless formula includes the necessary factor ω: Q=ω U/Pavg. The prose ratio alone has units of time. Q counts radians accumulated over an energy e-folding time, not directly the number of cycles. The printed ΔU=1/e lacks energy normalization.
For a weakly damped series resonator, conventional Q≈ΩL/R (exact resonance parameter ΩL/R for constant RLC), energy time τE=L/R and cycles per energy e-fold approximately Q/(2π). Use U(τE)/U(0)≈1/e; energy lost is approximately U0(1−1/e).
At resonance with sinusoidal peak current I0, total LC energy is LI0²/2 and Pavg=RI0²/2, so ΩU/Pavg=ΩL/R. For arbitrary driven ω, mean stored energy is I0²[L+1/(ω²C)]/4, giving ω Ubar/Pavg=ω[L+1/(ω²C)]/(2R); it is not generally ωL/R unless the resonant/equipartition condition applies. For free underdamping, ωdτE and ΩL/R differ beyond weak damping. The source tuning-fork/table comparison is qualitative, not measured Q data.

D142-C03 | phasor_steady_state_and_component_scope | lecture17.pdf, PDF 5,6,7,8,9,10, slides 11,13,14,15,16,17,19,20,22 | Using complex impedances
The complex method describes a sinusoidal particular/steady response of linear time-invariant lumped components at a common nonzero frequency. It does not automatically include arbitrary initial conditions or switching transients.
Use e^(iωt), passive branch-current conventions and constant R,L,C. Physical signals are real parts. With positive R the free transients decay; in a lossless LC circuit they need not, and at exact lossless resonance a bounded sinusoidal steady state does not exist. For a pure ideal inductor, integrating V adds an arbitrary constant DC current not represented by the AC phasor.
Derivative becomes iω, hence ZR=R, ZC=1/(iωC)=−i/(ωC), ZL=iωL. For series elements complex voltage drops add and common current gives Ztot=R+i(ωL−1/(ωC)). The source initial Q/I sign from free discharge need not match its new passive branch convention I=+Qdot for a driven capacitor. arctan(y/x) in the complex-number recap needs quadrant correction/atan2; at z=0 phase is undefined.

D142-E01 | rotating_generator_derivative_sign | lecture17.pdf, PDF 5, slides 12 | Generator EMF line
The displayed |EMF| equals a positive derivative of BS cosωt and then a positive sine, repeating the earlier magnitude/sign inconsistency.
For oriented flux Φ=BS cosωt, ε=−Φdot/c=(BSω/c)sinωt; |ε|=(BS|ω|/c)|sinωt|. The derivative of cosine itself is negative.
Differentiate explicitly. The chosen source cosine V0cosωt in the next calculations is simply a time-reference shift from this sine generator output. For the stated 60-Hz example, ω=2πν=376.991... rad/s, so 377 is a correctly rounded angular frequency, with rad/s units understood.

D142-E02 | current_impedance_reciprocal_slips | lecture17.pdf, PDF 8,10, slides 18,21 | First formula above capacitor/inductor phasors
Slides 18 and 21 print I(t)=ZC V0e^(iωt) and I(t)=ZL V0e^(iωt), respectively, before equating to the correct explicit current. Both should divide by impedance.
Replace those intermediate factors by 1/ZC and 1/ZL, or call the multiplying factor admittance Y=1/Z. The surrounding definitions ZC=1/(iωC), ZL=iωL and explicit currents are correct.
I_C=V/ZC=iωCV, while I_L=V/ZL=V/(iωL). Multiplying V by ohms has dimensions incompatible with current. The phasor arrows agree with the corrected reciprocal expressions: capacitor current leads voltage by π/2 and inductor current lags by π/2.

D142-E03 | inductor_peak_timing_caption_reversed | lecture17.pdf, PDF 9, slides 19 | Blue parenthetical under lag statement
The main text and plotted/analytic inductor current correctly lag voltage by 90 degrees, but its parenthetical says current maxima occur before voltage maxima.
Change before to after, for the next corresponding maxima and the displayed e^(iωt) convention.
V=V0cosωt peaks at t=0 mod T; I=(V0/(ωL))sinωt peaks at t=T/4 mod T. This is a lag of T/4. The capacitor instead has I=−ωCV0sinωt and peaks T/4 before the voltage; the source likely retained that caption, but its cause is not required to establish the error.

D142-S02 | series_current_amplitude_phase_and_limits | lecture17.pdf, PDF 10,11,12, slides 22,23,24,26 | Complex reciprocal and frequency plots
The Ztot, amplitude I0 and tanφ expressions are correct for positive R and the chosen current I(t)=I0cos(ωt−φ). The phase graph and resonance tails match those limits.
Let X=ωL−1/(ωC), |Z|=√(R²+X²), I0=V0/|Z| and φ=atan2(X,R). Then current leads at low ω and lags at high ω. Take the principal phase in (−π/2,π/2) for R>0.
1/Z=(R−iX)/(R²+X²)=(1/|Z|)e^(−iφ). As ω→0, I0≈V0ωC and φ→−π/2; as ω→∞, I0≈V0/(ωL) and φ→+π/2. φ increases monotonically since Xdot with respect to ω is L+1/(Cω²)>0. At Ω=1/√LC, X=0, I0=V0/R and φ=0. This maximizes series current for fixed V0 and frequency-independent R,L,C. It is not the damped free frequency ωd, and maxima of other response variables can occur elsewhere. Unspecified plot parameter sets do not certify their arbitrary axis numbers as shared measured data.

D142-S03 | motor_phase_numerics_and_rotating_field_conditions | lecture17.pdf, PDF 12, slides 25 | Two RL coils on recovered page
The quoted phase difference 64 degrees is correct to rounding for the stated 60-Hz parameters, assuming both applied voltage phases are the same. Orthogonal fields with that phase separation form an ellipse in field-component space, not generally a constant-magnitude circular field.
For each coil, current lags its drive by arctan(ωL/R). With ω=2π60: φ1≈13.812979 degrees for 2.3 ohm/1.5 mH, φ2≈77.925392 degrees for 2.5 ohm/31 mH; Δφ≈64.112412 degrees.
For orthogonal components Bx=Acos(ωt−φ1), By=Bcos(ωt−φ2), elimination of time gives an ellipse when amplitudes are nonzero and sinΔφ≠0; equal amplitudes and a 90-degree phase difference are needed for a circle. The field orientation circulates, but metal-can torque/speed requires geometry, induced-current, slip and loss information. The source states a demonstration effect, not enough data for a quantitative rotor prediction; no demonstration was observed.

D142-E04 | resonance_units_and_demo_conditions | lecture17.pdf, PDF 13,14, slides 27,28,29 | L8 frequency labels and L6 tuning
For L=50 mH and C=0.3 microfarad, Ω≈8.2×10³ rad/s and ν≈1.3 kHz are correct on slide 27. Slide 28 labels the peak ω0=1.3 kHz, mixing angular frequency symbol with ordinary-frequency units.
Use ν0≈1.3 kHz or ω0≈8.2 krad/s. The tunable relation L=1/(Cω²) is correct for ideal series-current resonance at fixed angular frequency, but does not guarantee visible lamp operation for every switch setting.
Compute Ω=1/√(0.05·0.3×10^−6)=8164.965809 rad/s and ν=Ω/(2π)=1299.494669 Hz. Measuring VR identifies the current peak if R is fixed and the scope input negligibly loads it. A frequency sweep must be slow enough for near-steady response and source amplitude must be controlled; the lecture omits sweep calibration. Movable iron core, lamp temperature and finite tuning range can change L/R and brightness threshold. No unprovided lamp power or achievable tuning range is inferred.
