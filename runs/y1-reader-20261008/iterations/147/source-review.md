**Equations, units and signs.** Pages 9, 15, 16 and 27 use Gaussian conventions. For the nonrelativistic circular-orbit model, period `2πr/v` gives `|I|=ev/(2πr)` and `|μ|=IA/c=evr/(2c)`. With positive charge magnitude `e`, the electron's vector moment is `μ=-eL/(2m_e c)`, antiparallel to its mechanical angular momentum. Page 8's electron velocity and conventional-current arrows are opposite; its downward axial field is consistent with the illustrated loop. The orbital coefficient must not be reused for spin: `μ_s=-g_s eS/(2m_e c)` with positive `g_s≈2.0023`. The SI loop formula has no `1/c` (F04).

On page 17 **`H=χ_m M` is reversed**. It must be `M=χ_m H`; only then does `B=H(1+4πχ_m)` follow. Keeping the printed first relation gives `B/H=1+4π/χ_m` and fails the zero-susceptibility limit. Linear scalar susceptibility is itself a restricted constitutive model, using internal H; anisotropy and hysteresis need more information (F13).

Page 18's numerical scale is strongly consistent with **SI volume susceptibility**, despite the surrounding Gaussian equations. The table does not identify its convention. [NIST's conversion table](https://www.nist.gov/document/magneticunitspdf) gives `χ_SI=4πχ_G`. Thus direct use of these entries in the displayed Gaussian B formula would enlarge the susceptibility correction by `4π` (F14).

`M` is moment per volume; `μ=MV` assumes uniform M, otherwise integrate. `[M]=[B]`, `B=H+4πM` and vacuum `B=H` are Gaussian statements; SI uses `B=μ₀(H+M)` with M,H in A/m and B in tesla. H is not generally the field that the free currents would make without material. The curls on page 16 are algebraically correct **in magnetostatics**: `J_M=c∇×M`, with the additional surface current `K_M=cM×n`. Time dependence adds polarization current and the displacement term in curl H. Page 27's four intended Maxwell equations have correct Gaussian factors and signs, including negative Faraday induction and positive displacement current. The original and fresh render retain stray square glyphs, but the equations are recoverable; this is not evidence of a truncated cache (F11-F12,F25).

**Microscopic and macroscopic limits.** The orbit drawings are classical models, not literal quantum trajectories. Intrinsic spin is not a rotating charged ball. Quantum orbital/spin/band structure and interactions underlie magnetism, while temperature, geometry and history affect its measured response. Randomly oriented existing moments and cancellation within filled shells are different mechanisms. Lenz's law supplies an induced-response sign, not a complete equilibrium theory; permanent orbital moments can also contribute positively. The three categories are selective, and positive susceptibility alone does not define a paramagnetic phase (F03,F05,F07).

The page 10 torque `τ=μ×B` is correct, but **torque alone does not produce alignment**. Independently, `dμ/dt=γμ×B` preserves `μ·B` in constant B: it gives precession. Thermal equilibration/relaxation is needed for the population bias toward lower `U=-μ·B` ([MIT NMR discussion](https://web.mit.edu/course/22/22.920/www/lecture1/lecture01.html), F06).

The inside-field comparisons require geometry. My ellipsoid check gives `B_inside/B₀=(1+χ)/(1+Nχ)` in SI; an infinite slab normal to the field has `N=1` and unchanged B despite nonzero M. A small weak body in a vacuum field has `F≈χV∇B₀²/(2μ₀)`: dia/paramagnets move toward weaker/stronger field **when a gradient exists**. A uniform field does not supply that translational force. Susceptibility sign alone also does not determine a body's long-axis orientation. Page 14 omits shape/pivot/anisotropy and field-profile information needed to establish its aluminum orientation assertion or a unique bismuth orientation. Its arrows indicate a rightward gap field; assigning left N/right S is only a diagram inference (F08-F09).

Page 13's heading calling its examples diamagnetic is a typo. **O₂ is paramagnetic as a gas**, and has two unpaired molecular-orbital electrons; it is not an exception requiring liquefaction. Page 18 itself contradicts that claim. Cooling and liquid density make a classroom effect more visible; thermal motion does not erase gas susceptibility. The listed ordinary-condition examples are qualitatively appropriate, but atomic pairing is not a sufficient model for metals, and phase/temperature conditions matter (F10; [Purdue molecular-orbital discussion](https://chemed.chem.purdue.edu/genchem/topicreview/bp/ch8/mo.php)).

Ferromagnetism is not defined by extreme paramagnetism or by nonlinearity. An independently checked noninteracting two-state model already gives nonlinear `M=nμ tanh(μB/k_BT)` and saturation. Ferromagnetic order involves interactions and spontaneous magnetization below Tc; a multidomain specimen can nevertheless have nearly zero total moment. Remanence depends on specimen and history (F15; [MIT exchange discussion](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/3-024-electronic-optical-and-magnetic-properties-of-materials-spring-2013/1e6cd7942a6e0514d806af7190d53538_MIT3_024S13_2012lec24.pdf)).

**Domains, notes and demonstrations.** Page 20's before/after arrows are schematics, not microscopy or quantitatively cancelling vectors. Its domain-size range is not universal; grains and domains differ. Favorable domains grow through wall motion, and magnetization can rotate; the speaker notes improve the slide's simpler flipping picture. Persistent complete alignment is not automatic. The compass array illustrates metastability, not microscopic exchange. The pickup coil converts abrupt magnetic-flux changes into amplified sound. Suitable instruments can image domain-wall jumps; copper's lack of ferromagnetic Barkhausen jumps does not mean it can generate no induction signal (F16,F18; [official Caltech discussion](https://www.feynmanlectures.caltech.edu/II_37.html), [primary Kerr measurements](https://journals.aps.org/prb/abstract/10.1103/PhysRevB.72.064433)).

The notes' `1 T / 0.0002 T = 5000` arithmetic is correct. Approximately 2.1 T is an iron saturation-polarization scale `μ₀M_s`, not a universal external field or ceiling on total B. Under negligible demagnetizing field, the given operating point corresponds to `H≈159 A/m`, `M≈7.956×10⁵ A/m` and secant `χ_SI≈4999`. The exact annealed-iron operating point lacks specimen/curve evidence and remains unverified ([CERN author's paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1103.1069), F17).

Page 21's 5 kg is an apparatus-dependent added load, approximately 49.0 N before plate/hardware weight. No gap, area or magnetic-circuit data support a calculated holding field. “Domains break” is not a precise explanation of detachment or proof of demagnetization (F19).

Page 22 **confuses lowering frequency with reducing amplitude**. AC demagnetization uses alternating field with an amplitude gradually approaching zero; slowing reversals alone is insufficient. The source drawing does not show winding handedness, so rod polarity is indeterminate. I personally read the cached [MIT J7](https://pirl.mit.edu/demo/J_7.html) text and viewed its full photograph; its Variac supports amplitude control, but the prose does not state a complete diminishing-amplitude procedure (F20; [primary demagnetization model](https://journals.aps.org/prb/abstract/10.1103/PhysRevB.71.064423)).

Pages 23-24 contain **unscaled illustrative loops, not measured hysteresis data**. The H/B axes are appropriate. Decreasing H from positive magnetization follows the upper branch; at H=0 the branch value is `+B_r`, or `-B_r` on the reverse history, not the text's `±H`. A return sweep is needed to close the loop. A demagnetized state can have zero remanence. Saturation of M does not make B strictly constant as H grows. No numerical coercivity, remanence or loop loss is available (F21).

Page 24 omits the measurement conversion: primary current may estimate H through a shunt and magnetic-circuit model, but **secondary emf measures `-N dΦ/dt`, requiring integration to estimate B**. These are **schematic-based calculations**, not measured calibration or reconstructed 2004 wiring (F22-F23).

A nut's sudden release is a force threshold, not evidence that magnetization itself jumps discontinuously or that the nut has reached precisely 770°C.
